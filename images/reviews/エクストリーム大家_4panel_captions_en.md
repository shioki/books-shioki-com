### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through a row of old nagaya houses in Edo, listening to townspeople gossip about “people with debts” and “those who can’t find a guarantor.” She tilts her head in curiosity, wondering why some individuals lack a place to call home. A stray cat rests near a broken shoji door, symbolizing exclusion and neglect.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study illuminated by candlelight, the townsgirl reads a new Dutch-style scroll titled “Ekusutorīmu Ōya.” The marginal portrait features a wise man with calm eyes, imagined as the author Harukawa Kentarō, depicted as a thoughtful middle-aged scholar with neat short hair and a gentle demeanor. She contemplates his idea that “risk and compassion can coexist in trade.”
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, the townsgirl assists an elderly carpenter in repairing a run-down tenement. Rather than opting for extravagant decorations, she proposes simple reinforcements and clean tatami. The townspeople express surprise, questioning, “You’re renting to those people?” She smiles and responds that trust is also a form of currency. The scene captures her confidently holding a broom like a merchant's banner, with ink lines radiating energy.
**Dialogue**: “You’re renting to those people?”  
**Dialogue**: “Trust is also a kind of currency.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As night descends, the townsgirl writes a waka on a narrow tanzaku by lamplight, her brush moving gracefully. The cat from earlier now sleeps peacefully beneath the eaves of a newly repaired house.  
**Tanka (translation)**: “Connecting bonds, more precious than gold, is the heart;  
Lending is living, sharing life together.”  
**Tanka (original)**: 「縁つなぐ　金より重き　心あり  
　貸すは生くるを　共にすること」
