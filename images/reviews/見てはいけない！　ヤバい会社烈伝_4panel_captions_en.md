### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a bustling Edo street, the townsgirl observes a merchant giving contradictory orders to his workers, causing confusion and hesitation among them. She tilts her head in curiosity, reflecting on how fear can disrupt a group's purpose. The background features fluttering shop banners and apprentices standing still in confusion.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small room at night, the townsgirl studies a Dutch-bound scroll titled “Company of Many Minds.” The scroll depicts a scholarly man with calm eyes and round spectacles, seemingly gazing back at her. He points to a diagram illustrating how “trust” connects individuals like threads, while candlelight flickers across her focused face.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, the townsgirl gathers children at the terakoya and demonstrates a lesson using abacus beads. She explains that when one bead moves, others follow, illustrating the concept of trust and cooperation. The beads tumble everywhere, causing laughter among the children, yet they grasp the lesson about shared intent.
**Dialogue**: “See, when trust moves, the whole flows.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: By the riverbank under a quiet sky, the townsgirl kneels with her brush, reflecting on her lessons. The wind ripples the water as she writes a tanka on a small paper slip, her expression serene and resolute.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: Without a guiding principle, the group wanders and loses its voice; a single thread of trust illuminates the path.  
**Tanka (original)**: 理念なく  
群れは迷いて  
声を失う  
信のひと筋  
道を照らせり
