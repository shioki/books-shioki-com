### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through a bustling Edo street filled with merchants and lanterns, carrying scrolls of arithmetic problems. She overhears craftsmen arguing about delayed deliveries and chaotic workflows, her eyes filled with curiosity as she wonders, “Why do tasks pile up like tangled threads?” In the background, a small workshop is cluttered with half-finished goods.

**Dialogue**: “Why do tasks pile up like tangled threads?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her candle-lit study, the townsgirl opens a mysterious imported book on organization and flow, filled with diagrams and Dutch annotations. The page features faint illustrations of five men resembling scholarly spirits guiding her learning: Gene Kim, Kevin Behr, George Spafford, Takahiro Nagao, and Akira Sakakibara. She studies intently with a brush in hand, murmuring about “reducing unplanned work” and “flow of value.”

**Dialogue**: “Reducing unplanned work... Flow of value...”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At dawn, the townsgirl organizes the neighborhood’s craftsmen by arranging their tasks on wooden tablets like a kanban board, limiting ongoing work, and creating a rhythm of delivery. The carpenters and dyers cheer as work begins to flow smoothly. She stands confidently atop a stool, her hair fluttering, pointing her brush like a conductor’s baton, embodying humor and energy.

**Dialogue**: “Let’s create a rhythm of delivery!”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the evening's tranquility, the townsgirl kneels by a paper lantern, writing a waka on a tanzaku. Her expression is serene, illuminated by soft ink wash strokes. Next to her, scrolls labeled “信頼” and “改善” lie open.

**Tanka (translation)**: “The flow is trust, do not let it fade; with daily ingenuity, flowers will bloom.”

**Tanka (original)**: 「流れとは　人の信なり　絶やすなり　日々の工夫に　花は咲くなり」
