### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a bustling Edo street at dusk, listening to merchants arguing about fairness and power. She tilts her head thoughtfully, pondering the possibility of “democracy” in her town. The background features wooden shopfronts, paper lanterns, and townsfolk engaged in animated debate, hinting at social division.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit study room, the townsgirl studies a Dutch book on governance and a handwritten scroll titled "民の理（デモクラシー）". A ghostly ink portrait of Aoyama Naoatsu appears on the scroll’s margin, depicted as a calm, middle-aged scholar with thoughtful eyes, tied-back hair, and a gentle expression, dressed in simple robes. His spectral figure seems to convey insights about “the fractures between the powerful and the people.”
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, the townsgirl organizes a gathering at a riverside teahouse where villagers and merchants freely exchange opinions. She uses an abacus and maps to illustrate how mutual understanding strengthens the town. The atmosphere is lively yet harmonious, with bold brushstrokes depicting movement and laughter, symbolizing a reborn sense of community trust.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Under the moonlight, she kneels by the river, brush in hand, writing on a tanzaku. Her eyes are serene, reflecting the light of the lanterns. The short poem she writes encapsulates the book’s essence.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: "The shadow of power, the voice of the people illuminates the path; from a distant land, the wind of justice blows."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「権の影　民の声こそ　道を照らす  
　遠き国より　義の風わたる」
