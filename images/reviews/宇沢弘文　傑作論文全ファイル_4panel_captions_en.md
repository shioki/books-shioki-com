### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through a lively Edo street, observing merchants shouting and coins changing hands. She stops at the riverbank, frowning slightly as she contemplates, “Is this prosperity… or loss?” The background features detailed Edo townhouses and a bridge bustling with people.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a tranquil temple study, the townsgirl studies a Dutch economic treatise and a scroll titled “Social Common Capital.” A calm, scholarly elder with soft eyes and a gentle demeanor appears as a portrait or spirit emerging from the book, imagined as Hirofumi Uzawa: an elderly man with an intellectual aura, wearing a traditional scholar’s robe, without glasses, with neat graying hair and a serene expression suggesting deep ethical thought.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, she gathers her neighbors in a small garden by the river, planting saplings and repairing a communal well. Children laugh, and elders smile. She explains with bright eyes that wealth is not just silver, but also clean water, learning, and trust. The composition highlights her outstretched hands and the revitalized flowing river.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dusk, she kneels by the riverbank, brush in hand, composing a waka on a tanzaku. The wind ripples the water’s surface. The short poem reads in delicate calligraphy:  
“Though the world may be swayed by the melody of wealth, the path of living together is what is truly precious.”  
Her serene face reflects the glow of lantern light, evoking both enlightenment and gentle melancholy.
**Tanka (translation)**: Though the world may be swayed by the melody of wealth, the path of living together is what is truly precious.
**Tanka (original)**: 人の世は　富のしらべに　惑うとも　共に生きゆく　道こそ尊し
