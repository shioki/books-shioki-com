### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a lively Edo street, observing merchants and apprentices working diligently under strict supervision. She tilts her head, sensing something unusual—everyone appears dutiful yet joyless. A breeze rustles her sleeve as she softly questions, “Why do they look so tired when the day is so bright?” The background features rows of shops and a merchant reprimanding a clerk.  
**Dialogue**: “Why do they look so tired when the day is so bright?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study, the townsgirl unrolls a Dutch treatise on commerce alongside a Japanese essay about “trust in management.” A portrait scroll hangs nearby, depicting an imagined scholar inspired by 渋谷和宏, with calm, reflective eyes, medium build, neatly tied hair, and scholarly robes, holding a brush in contemplation. His expression conveys gentle intelligence and sincerity. The townsgirl studies intently, her ink brush poised as she murmurs, “It is not the workers’ fault, but the way they are led.”  
**Dialogue**: “It is not the workers’ fault, but the way they are led.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Now in a teahouse, the townsgirl gathers weary neighbors. She draws on the tatami with chalk, illustrating how “trust breeds creation” using abacus symbols and geometric patterns. The group laughs as they begin to suggest new ways to share work and value. A humorous moment occurs when her abacus suddenly collapses, scattering beads like shooting stars—everyone laughs, joy returning to their faces.  
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the evening, the townsgirl sits by her window, gazing at the glowing lanterns across Edo. With a serene smile, she writes a waka on a small tanzaku, her brush shimmering in the lamplight.  
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]  
**Tanka (translation)**: “Trusting each other, nurturing the sprouts of humanity,  
Without fearing frugality, they become a tree of value.”  
**Tanka (original)**: 「信じ合い　任せて育つ　人の芽は  
　倹を恐れず　価値を生む木に」
