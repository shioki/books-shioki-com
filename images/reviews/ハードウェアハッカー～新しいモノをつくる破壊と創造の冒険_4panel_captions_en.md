### Panel 1
**Scene**: In the vibrant streets of Edo, the townsgirl observes a craftsman skillfully repairing a mechanical toy with makeshift parts. She tilts her head in contemplation, pondering the relationship between imitation and invention. The background is filled with a lively merchant street adorned with tools, gears, and fluttering paper lanterns.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside her small study, the townsgirl is engrossed in a Dutch mechanical treatise. A scroll on the wall features three scholars who inspired her — Andrew “Bunnie” Huang depicted as a gentle foreign tinkerer with round glasses and inquisitive eyes; Masakazu Takasu as a spirited, lean inventor with his hair tied back and a sharp gaze; and Hiroo Yamagata as a thoughtful translator with a calm demeanor, holding a brush. Her eyes shine with excitement as she uncovers the concept of “open creation.”
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: She brings her newfound insight to a local workshop, where she eagerly dismantles and reassembles an abacus-like device. The townsfolk watch in awe as she makes it calculate faster — a playful homage to “hardware hacking” in the Edo style. Ink splashes illustrate movement and energy, symbolizing the act of “breaking and creating.”
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As dusk falls by the riverside, the girl writes a waka poem on a tanzaku, illuminated by the soft glow of lantern light. The poem encapsulates the essence of creation through sharing and the spirit of the maker.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: With dirty hands,  
Learning through imitation,  
I make it anew,  
Breaking and creating —  
The heart is the true path.  
**Tanka (original)**: 手を汚し  
まねて学びて  
改める  
破りて創る  
心こそ道
