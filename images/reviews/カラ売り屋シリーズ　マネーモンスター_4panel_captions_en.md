### Panel 1
**Scene**: In the lively Edo marketplace, the townsgirl observes merchants in a heated argument over silver coins and rice futures. She tilts her head, perceiving the conflict between greed and fairness. A sign reading "money changer" hangs in the background, while cherry petals float gently through the air as she reflects on the "boundary between greed and justice."
**Dialogue**: "欲と正義のはざま"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her candle-lit study, the townsgirl is deeply engrossed in a Dutch treatise on commerce and an old arithmetic book. A portrait scroll of a dignified man labeled "Mr. Kuroki Ryo" hangs on the shelf, imagined with calm eyes and short dark hair, exuding the aura of a reflective scholar. His ink-drawn face seems to silently guide her on "ethics in the market," connecting different eras and ideas. The townsgirl's eyes sparkle with understanding.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At dawn, she stands on a bridge over the river, releasing paper boats labeled "truth" and "profit." Curious townsfolk watch as the boats drift apart — one sinks while the other sails ahead. She smiles, realizing that integrity allows the current of society to flow smoothly. The bold ink lines and flowing water illustrate moral clarity amidst chaos.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In her small garden, the townsgirl kneels with her writing brush, crafting a waka on a tanzaku slip. Moonlight illuminates her face as she softly recites the poem that encapsulates the essence of her thoughts:
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: In the sea of desire,  
With the wind of justice, I set my sail.  
Whether I sink or float,  
It all depends on my heart.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
欲の海  
正義の風に  
帆を立てて  
沈むも浮かぶも  
心ひとつで
