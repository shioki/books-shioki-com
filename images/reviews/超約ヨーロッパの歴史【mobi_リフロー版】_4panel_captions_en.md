### Panel 1
**Scene**: At dusk on an Edo street, the young townsgirl pauses before a temple school where a monk lectures about the “history of the West.” The tiled roofs and wooden signboards glow faintly in the evening light as children chant their lessons. A gentle breeze stirs fallen leaves around her feet.  
**Dialogue**: “Can a civilization be reborn again and again?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small candlelit room, she opens a foreign book titled *History of Europe*. From its pages emerge three spectral mentors — a calm, bearded foreign scholar; a kind Japanese translator with round spectacles; and a dignified historian in traditional attire. They gesture toward scrolls showing how Greek reason, Christian faith, and Germanic spirit intertwine. The townsgirl listens in awe, her brush hovering above the paper.  
**Dialogue**: *(none)*

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Before a group of townsfolk debating how to share the temple’s well water, the townsgirl sketches a simple “contract” on a wooden board, inspired by ideas of social agreement she learned from the West. Hands point and faces light up in surprise as she smiles confidently, the lantern light flickering across bold ink lines.  
**Dialogue**: *(none)*

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Night falls. Sitting by the window, the townsgirl gazes at the moon reflected in her inkstone. With calm, luminous eyes, she writes a waka poem on a hanging tanzaku.  
**Dialogue**: *(none)*  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
Civilizations blend,  
are born anew, then transform—  
reason and compassion  
draw their endless circle,  
ever turning through the ages.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
文明は  
混ざりて生まれ  
また変わる  
理と情けの  
輪を描きつつ
