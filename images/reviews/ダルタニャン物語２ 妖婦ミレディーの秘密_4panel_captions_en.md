### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through an Edo street at dusk, pausing to listen to two merchants debating whether love or logic governs the world. She holds her abacus thoughtfully, surrounded by the ambiance of a paper lantern shop and narrow alleyways, capturing the tension between reason and emotion.

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small, candlelit study, the townsgirl reads a thick foreign book titled “D’Artagnan Monogatari.” From the pages, ghostly figures appear: one resembling Alexandre Dumas, dignified with curly hair and expressive eyes, and a calm-faced Japanese scholar in Edo robes, holding a brush. They whisper to her about love, justice, and fate.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, the townsgirl mediates a quarrel between two friends over a broken promise, balancing reason and emotion. She holds her abacus in one hand and a fan in the other, her expression a blend of wisdom and compassion. A gust of wind scatters cherry petals around her, symbolizing fleeting passions.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Night has fallen. The townsgirl sits by the window, brush in hand, composing a tanka on a small paper strip. Moonlight illuminates her serene face, and the poem floats beside her in elegant vertical calligraphy.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: Love is like fire, burning beyond reason,  
Tears fall in the shadow of justice.  
**Tanka (original)**: 愛は火よ　理をも越えて　燃えさかる  
正義の影に　涙こぼるる
