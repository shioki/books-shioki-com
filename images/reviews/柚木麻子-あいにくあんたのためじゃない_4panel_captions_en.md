### Panel 1
**Scene**: At dusk on an Edo merchant street, the young townsgirl walks past neighbors gossiping about favors and obligations. She tilts her head, uneasy at the thought of living only for others. Lanterns glow softly, and a narrow alley leads to a small bookstore where open scrolls flutter in the evening breeze.  
**Dialogue**: (thinking) “Is life meant only to serve others…?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her modest study, she opens a newly arrived Dutch-bound book. On the frontispiece, a portrait of a serene, intelligent woman—her imagined mentor—seems to come alive. The woman’s calm eyes meet hers, whispering wisdom across time.  
**Dialogue**: (whisper from the portrait) “To live for yourself is also to save others.”  
**Dialogue**: (townsgirl, softly) “For myself… and for others…”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl gently declines a neighbor’s unnecessary gift, bowing with gratitude yet firm resolve. The neighbor, surprised, then smiles in understanding. The wind lifts her sleeves; the setting sun filters through shoji paper, illuminating their shared moment of respect.  
**Dialogue**: (townsgirl) “Thank you, but I must decline.”  
**Dialogue**: (neighbor) “Ah… I see.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Night falls. By lamplight, the townsgirl writes a waka on a slender tanzaku strip. Her brush moves gracefully, her expression calm yet strong. The soft wind outside seems to echo her newfound peace.  
**Dialogue**: (none)  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
Though not for your sake,  
the light I kindle within me—  
even the night wind  
turns gentle, embracing this  
quiet town beneath the stars.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
あいにくと　  
あなたのためじゃ　ないけれど  
灯（ひ）ともす我に　  
風もやさしき  
夜の町かな
