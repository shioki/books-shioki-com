### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through a lively Edo street, observing a stranger assisting another person with heavy goods. She pauses, tilting her head in curiosity, pondering why people help those who are not related to them. The background features wooden shops, passersby, and a peaceful canal reflecting lantern light.

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small room filled with scrolls and rangaku texts, the townsgirl studies a Dutch book about human kindness. From the pages, a vision emerges—two scholars, one a middle-aged Western man with short light-brown hair and a simple shirt and jacket, and the other a slim Japanese man with wavy black hair, glasses, and a gentle smile. They appear as wise spirits discussing how evolution and reason shape kindness.

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl decides to test the idea by helping a complete stranger, an elderly man who has dropped his abacus in the street. Her friend looks surprised and exclaims, “He’s not kin!” She smiles and replies that perhaps reason itself teaches the heart to reach beyond blood ties. Expressive brushstrokes illustrate her motion and the warm light shared between people.

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At twilight, the townsgirl sits by the river with a brush in hand, composing a waka on a small tanzaku paper. The wind creates ripples in the water as she contemplates the paradox of kindness that arises from both instinct and reason. The short poem written in Japanese appears on the tanzaku:

**Tanka (translation)**: A heart that expands beyond blood ties,  
In the bloom of mistaken flowers,  
The light of reason  
Begins to blossom.

**Tanka (original)**:  
「血のさけを  
　越えてひろがる　こころかな  
　誤作の花に  
　理性のひかり  
　咲きそめにけり」
