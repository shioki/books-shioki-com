### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through a bustling Edo street, surrounded by book vendors and tea houses, holding a well-worn book. She observes a lively debate among townsfolk, each passionately sharing their interpretations of a story. 
**Dialogue**: Her inner monologue reflects her curiosity: “Is there a way for books to connect hearts, not divide them?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a cozy study filled with a mix of Dutch and Japanese books, illuminated by candlelight. The townsgirl is engrossed in reading an imported philosophical text and a scroll titled "Introduction to Reading Circles." The spirit of the author, imagined as a gentle middle-aged man named Yamamoto Tatsuya, appears, explaining the essence of reading circles.
**Dialogue**: He gestures kindly, saying, “A reading circle isn’t about opinions, but about connection.” 

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl hosts a small reading circle at a teahouse, surrounded by townspeople including a carpenter, a courtesan, and a monk. They share insights on the same book, creating an atmosphere of laughter and understanding. The townsgirl smiles, taking notes as she realizes the richness that different perspectives bring to the story.
**Dialogue**: The overlapping dialogue bubbles convey the warmth of their shared discovery.

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the evening, the townsgirl sits by the window with a brush in hand, looking out at the moonlit street where the reading circle has just dispersed. She writes a waka on a small tanzaku paper, capturing the essence of the book’s message. The scene is tranquil and poetic, with flickering candlelight illuminating her serene expression.
**Tanka (translation)**:  
When pages turn,  
Hearts unfold and connect,  
Voices intertwine,  
Learning together at dusk,  
A night of shared wisdom.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
本の頁  
ひらけばひらく  
人の心  
声を交わして  
学びあう宵
