### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a lively Edo street, the townsgirl observes merchants engaged in polite arguments, avoiding real conflict. She tilts her head, sensing something unusual — everyone appears harmonious, yet progress is stagnant. The background features paper lanterns, shop signs, and townsfolk bowing excessively to maintain peace.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a candle-lit study, the townsgirl opens a Dutch book on “merchant governance” next to a Japanese scroll. A calm, middle-aged scholar with short black hair, gray strands, glasses, and a refined face appears, dressed in a formal haori. He gestures thoughtfully, explaining how harmony can become a “silent killer” of growth. The girl listens intently, with her brush poised above her notebook.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, the townsgirl gathers villagers to discuss ways to improve market fairness. When disagreements arise, instead of silencing them, she smiles and encourages open dialogue. The composition conveys dynamic energy, with scrolls, an abacus, and waves of dialogue flowing through the frame, symbolizing constructive conflict leading to progress.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the evening, the townsgirl kneels by lamplight, writing a poem on a tanzaku. Her expression is serene and enlightened. The short poem reads vertically on the tanzaku:  
「和を守り  
声を潜めて  
時は過ぐ  
変わる勇気が  
命をつなぐ」  
The final frame captures the quiet glow of realization — with her ink brush poised, the girl gazes out toward the dawn of new understanding.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: "Protecting harmony, / Silencing voices, / Time passes by. / The courage to change / Connects our lives."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「和を守り  
声を潜めて  
時は過ぐ  
変わる勇気が  
命をつなぐ」
