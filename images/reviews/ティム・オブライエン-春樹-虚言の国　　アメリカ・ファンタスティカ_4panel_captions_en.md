### Panel 1
**Scene**: At dusk on an Edo street, the young townsgirl walks with her abacus and notebook in hand. A storyteller’s voice drifts through the air, describing a distant land “where truth and lies mingle.” She pauses under a swaying lantern, her expression thoughtful as the light flickers across her face.  
**Dialogue**: “Can a whole nation be ill with lies…?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study, surrounded by scrolls on Dutch learning and brushes, she opens an imported book. Inside are two portraits—one of a calm Western scholar with introspective eyes, the other of a serene Japanese translator in simple kimono. Their gazes seem to whisper lessons about truth and illusion as she reads by the soft glow of a candle.  
**Dialogue**: *(None)*

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, she experiments with a polished mirror, using it as a metaphor to test the reflections of townsfolk spreading rumors. Their faces warp in the mirror’s surface, revealing how easily appearances deceive. She takes notes, half amused, as even her cat’s reflection seems to “lie,” making her laugh despite the serious theme.  
**Dialogue**: *(Laughter)*

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Night deepens. Sitting by the window, she holds her brush poised over paper as moonlight spills across her desk. Her expression is calm yet determined as she writes a waka that captures the essence of what she has learned. The poem appears vertically on a tanzaku slip beside her.  
**Dialogue**: *(None)*  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
Even knowing we cling to falsehoods, still we live;  
When truth is spoken, light will return.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
「  
虚言にも　すがるを知りて　人は生く  
　真を語らば　光は戻る  
」
