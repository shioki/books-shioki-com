### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a busy Edo street, holding a basket and scrolls. She hears merchants giving long, confusing explanations to customers, and her expression shifts to curiosity as she wonders, “Why can’t they say it simply?” The background features wooden shop signs, lanterns, and townsfolk engaged in conversation.
**Dialogue**: “Why can’t they say it simply?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a small study filled with scrolls and Dutch books, the townsgirl opens a peculiar Western-style book adorned with intricate diagrams. A gentle middle-aged man in scholarly robes, envisioned as the spirit of “Ito Yōichi,” appears through the sliding door. He has a calm demeanor, short hair neatly tied back, and wears round spectacles. He gestures towards a three-tier pyramid drawn on parchment, explaining the significance of “structure and one phrase that moves hearts.” The girl listens intently, her eyes sparkling with understanding.

### Panel 3
**Scene**: In a lively town marketplace, the townsgirl confidently stands on a crate, summarizing a complex math problem in just one minute to an amused crowd. Her gestures are animated, and her words are concise. The people nod eagerly, with a fishmonger repeating her “super one phrase” aloud, clearly impressed. A humorous touch: even a passing cat pauses to listen.

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the evening, in her small room illuminated by a single lantern, the townsgirl dips her brush in ink and writes a waka on a hanging scroll. Her face reflects serenity as she gazes at the moon through the paper window, her words flowing with quiet conviction.
**Tanka (translation)**: “Knowing the art to move hearts with a single phrase, words become a bridge to cross.”
**Tanka (original)**: 「一言で  
　心を動かす  
　術（すべ）を知り  
　言葉は橋と  
　なりて渡せり」
