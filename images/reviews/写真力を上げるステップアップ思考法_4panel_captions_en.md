### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through a lively Edo street, holding a small wooden camera-like box made by her tinkerer friend. She observes the bustling market filled with vendors, children, and passing samurai, lost in thought about what she truly wishes to capture. The background features noren curtains, tiled roofs, and sunlight filtering through, emphasizing her curious expression.
**Dialogue**: “What am I truly trying to capture?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a quiet terakoya, the townsgirl unrolls a Dutch book on optics alongside a Japanese manual on painting. A hanging scroll on the shelf depicts a calm, middle-aged scholar with gentle eyes and a thin mustache, embodying a serene Edo intellectual. She studies his writings intently, her brush poised over the paper, reflecting her admiration and focus.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Outside, she experiments with a polished mirror and lens, attempting to reflect the street scene onto paper. The image appears inverted, drawing the curiosity of townsfolk around her. She laughs, realizing that “seeing” involves more than just the eyes; it’s about intention. The dynamic composition captures swirling light and shadow as she steps back, her reflection blending with the world.
**Dialogue**: “Seeing is not just about eyes but about intent.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dusk by the riverbank, the townsgirl sits and writes a waka on a narrow tanzaku, gazing at her reflection in the water. The serene atmosphere is enhanced by fireflies and gentle ink brush tones, as her short poem encapsulates the essence of expression, humanity, and purpose in creation.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]  
**Tanka (translation)**: “Why do I capture? In moments of questioning, the wind reflects, revealing myself in this mirror-like world.”  
**Tanka (original)**: 「なぜ撮ると　問ひしひととき　風映る　己を映す　鏡なる世に」
