### Panel 1
**Scene**: In the bustling streets of Edo at dusk, the townsgirl stands by a riverside bridge, observing merchants rushing past. She listens to two samurai debating the value of usefulness in society, her gentle face reflecting curiosity as she questions if a person's worth is truly based on their contributions. The warm lantern light dances on the water, capturing the lively essence of the town.
**Dialogue**: "Does a person's worth really depend on what they produce?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a cozy study illuminated by candlelight, the townsgirl is engrossed in a Dutch book and a scroll on moral philosophy. A portrait of a serene middle-aged scholar with round glasses hangs on the wall, exuding a calm presence reminiscent of philosopher Ichiro Kishimi. As she takes notes in the margins, she contemplates the idea that “to give is to live.”
**Dialogue**: "To give is to live..."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl steps into a rainy alley, offering her umbrella to a stranger with groceries. The spontaneous act radiates warmth, and the stranger's smile brightens the scene. Raindrops fall dynamically, with ink strokes emphasizing the movement and the kindness shared between them.
**Dialogue**: "Here, take my umbrella!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Back at her writing desk, the townsgirl dips her brush into ink, writing a waka on a slender tanzaku paper. Her serene expression is illuminated by the soft glow of a paper lantern, as she captures her thoughts in poetry.
**Tanka (translation)**: "To give is not a loss; when I understand this, the strength to live fills my heart."
**Tanka (original)**: 「与ふるは  
　損にあらずと  
　知るときぞ  
　生きるちからは  
　胸に満ちゆく」
