### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl stands beside a creaking wooden waterwheel on the edge of Edo, observing it turn in the morning mist. She overhears two washerwomen discussing how “helping each other is all that keeps us going.” The girl tilts her head thoughtfully, sensing a deeper truth about dependence and dignity. The background features a quiet watermill and a flowing stream with rippling reflections.
**Dialogue**: "Helping each other is what keeps us going."

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit temple library, the townsgirl reads a tattered rangaku manuscript. Opposite her sits an imagined mentor resembling author Tsumura Kikuko — a serene woman with a calm, intelligent expression, soft tied-back hair, and wearing plain scholar’s robes. She gestures gently toward a Dutch text about “sympathia humana.” The scene radiates quiet empathy and intellectual focus.
**Dialogue**: "This text speaks of 'sympathia humana.'"

### Panel 3
**Scene**: In a bustling Edo marketplace, the girl helps an elderly vendor lift a fallen basket of vegetables. Townspeople around her begin to help one another spontaneously, reflecting the “chain of goodwill.” The waterwheel motif subtly reappears behind them, turning faster as if powered by their shared kindness. The composition is dynamic, filled with movement and warm human expression.
**Dialogue**: "Let's help each other!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the evening by the watermill, the townsgirl sits on the wooden deck, brush in hand, composing a waka on a small tanzaku. Fireflies drift around her as she smiles softly, gazing at the turning wheel — now a symbol of mutual support and rebirth.
**Dialogue**: "This wheel turns with our support."
**Tanka (translation)**: Supporting each other,  
In the cycle of water,  
The moon reflects,  
Not alone we live,  
A testament of our lives.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
支え合う  
水の輪廻に  
映る月  
ひとりにあらず  
生きるあかしを
