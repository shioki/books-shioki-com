### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through an Edo market street at dusk, observing merchants selling sweet dumplings and white rice. She pauses with a slight frown, pondering why people are so attracted to sugary foods even though they often feel sluggish afterward. Lanterns and a teahouse sign reading "甘味処" are visible in the background.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a dimly lit study room, the townsgirl sits surrounded by scrolls of Dutch medical texts and anatomical diagrams. One scroll features a portrait of a wise scholar resembling Makita Zenji, depicted as a calm, middle-aged physician with kind eyes and neatly tied hair, dressed in a simple scholar’s robe. He appears to instruct from the page, stating that “it is sugar, not fat, that harms health.”  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl experiments in her small kitchen, substituting sweet rice cakes with grilled fish and vegetables, opting for steaming instead of frying. She carefully arranges the dishes and tastes them with curiosity. Her cat looks disappointed at the lack of mochi, adding a humorous touch. Steam and motion lines convey a sense of lively experimentation and discovery.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In a candlelit night scene, the townsgirl gazes serenely at her meal, brush in hand, composing a waka on a hanging scroll.  
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: Sweet things confuse the heart, like white snow; the light of oil illuminates life.

心を惑わす  
白き雪  
油の光
