### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through a lively Edo street, observing merchants in a hurry and townspeople discussing their fortunes. She stops to gaze at the cloudy sky, reflecting on the restlessness of everyone around her. A gentle breeze flutters the paper she holds. 
**Dialogue**: “Why do hearts chase storms not yet come?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a cozy study filled with scrolls and Dutch books, the townsgirl kneels at a low desk. A portrait of an older man, imagined as the author Ōhara Henri, hangs on the wall. He appears calm and thoughtful, with soft, slightly wavy hair and a serene expression. The phrase “Let go of borrowed worries” captures her attention in the open book before her, while candlelight flickers gently.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At dawn, the townsgirl stands by the riverbank, observing ripples on the still water. A young neighbor rushes over, panicked by rumors of economic trouble. She smiles warmly, holds up a small umbrella, and lightheartedly reassures him.
**Dialogue**: “It’s just cloudy — not the end of the season.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In her tranquil room, the townsgirl kneels by the window, writing a waka on a strip of paper. Morning light streams through the shōji paper, illuminating her peaceful face.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: "Even in my heart, the weather changes. While waiting for clear skies, my own sky remains."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「心にも  
　天気のうつろひ  
　ありにけり  
　晴れを待つ間も  
　わたしの空は」
