### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a lively Edo street, the townsgirl listens intently as cruel rumors about a distant village in disaster circulate around her. She appears troubled, with ink-like shapes representing "anonymous whispers" floating in the air, indicating hidden malice. The background features longhouses and townsfolk murmuring among themselves.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside her small study illuminated by an oil lamp, the townsgirl opens a Dutch-language book focused on human nature and ethics. A calm, middle-aged male scholar, resembling Hideo Aiba, appears as her mentor or as a portrait within the book. He speaks softly about “justice clouded by anger” and “the unseen wounds words can cause.”  
**Dialogue**: "Justice clouded by anger... the unseen wounds words can cause."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl approaches the riverside where travelers from the afflicted region are being shunned. With a warm smile, she offers them tea, breaking the barrier of prejudice. The ink lines swirl dynamically, illustrating the tension dissolving into gentle water ripples, evoking a sense of moral awakening.  
**Dialogue**: "Please, have some tea."

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, she sits by her window, brush in hand, composing a waka on a tanzaku slip as moonlight streams in. Her expression is a blend of serenity and sorrow, embodying hope and reflection. The short poem written vertically reads:  
**Tanka (translation)**: "The blade of words / Does not let unseen blood / Flow freely. / To speak face to face / Is where true hearts lie."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「  
言の刃　  
見えぬ血潮を　  
流すなり　  
顔見て語る　  
心こそ真  
」
