### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a bustling Edo street at dusk, holding a brush and small notebook. She pauses before a merchant shouting about “happiness talismans that bring success,” tilting her head in doubt. Her expression shows quiet skepticism as she wonders if happiness can truly be “bought.” Background: shop lanterns, paper charms fluttering.
**Dialogue**: "Can happiness really be bought?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit temple study room, the townsgirl reads a foreign-looking book with Dutch letters beside her wasansho. A translucent image of two authors appears as guiding spirits: one, a calm Western scholar with thoughtful eyes and a beard (representing Oliver Burkeman); the other, a serene Japanese teacher with gentle eyes and tied hair (representing Shimo Takamasa). They seem to whisper: “Passion is cultivated, not found.” The girl listens, eyes wide in realization.
**Dialogue**: "Passion is cultivated, not found."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl now applies the lesson—helping a child struggling with arithmetic, smiling as she scribbles simple sums on the ground with a stick. She gently says, “Let’s learn together,” embodying acceptance rather than pressure. A breeze stirs fallen cherry petals; the earlier “happiness charms” from the first scene drift past her feet, unnoticed.
**Dialogue**: "Let’s learn together."

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Nighttime in her small room. The townsgirl sits by candlelight, writing a tanka on a narrow strip of paper, her face serene. The short poem reads vertically on a tanzaku beside her hand.
**Dialogue**: None
**Tanka (translation)**: Rather than searching, let us nurture the flame of emotion; even anxiety can be a friend, sharing the light together.
**Tanka (original)**:  
探すより  
育ててゆかん  
情の火を  
不安も友と  
灯を分け合う
