### Panel 1
**Scene**: In an Edo street bustling with merchant shops and illuminated by paper lanterns, the townsgirl pauses while overhearing two townsmen arguing about the fairness of rice prices after a flood. She holds her abacus thoughtfully, contemplating the nature of justice. The background depicts a flooded rice market and anxious townsfolk.
**Dialogue**: “Is justice merely what brings happiness… or is it something deeper?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a candle-lit terakoya, the townsgirl is engrossed in a Dutch-bound philosophy book adorned with portraits. From the pages, two scholarly figures emerge in her imagination: one resembling Michael Sandel, an elderly Western man with calm, thoughtful eyes; the other, Onizawa Shinobu, a stern yet kind Japanese teacher in simple robes. They appear to be engaged in a debate about happiness, freedom, and virtue.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl stands in a lively market where a merchant and a customer are arguing over price increases. She steps in between them, calmly asserting that justice is found not just in profit, but in what is deemed good. The crowd pauses to reflect on her words, her expression radiating conviction, with bold sumi ink lines capturing the dynamic moment.
**Dialogue**: “Justice is not only in gain, but in what we praise as good.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At night, the townsgirl kneels by the light of a lamp, brush in hand, as she composes a tanka on a small piece of tanzaku paper. Her face is serene, illuminated by the flickering candlelight.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]
**Tanka (translation)**: "Happiness, freedom, and virtue intertwine; together they question, this is the beginning of justice."
