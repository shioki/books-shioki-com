### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a lively Edo street, carrying scrolls and an abacus. She pauses at a local office where clerks are busy stamping papers with seals. With a puzzled expression, she wonders how "efficiency" and "trust" could be better balanced. The background features an Edo townscape with scattered paper scrolls and a clerk bowing behind a counter.
**Dialogue**: "Isn't there a better balance between efficiency and trust?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her candlelit study, the townsgirl reads a mysterious new Dutch book. Suddenly, the spirit of the author Yukio Noguchi appears in a cloud of ink smoke from the pages. He has a calm demeanor, a thin face, round glasses, and tied-back hair, dressed in simple robes. He gestures towards two diagrams: one labeled “centralized control” and the other “distributed trust.” The townsgirl listens with astonishment and curiosity.
**Dialogue**: "These concepts could change everything!"

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, the townsgirl experiments in her neighborhood. She gathers townsfolk in a circle, each holding their own record tablet, exchanging information directly without a central keeper. The villagers smile, realizing the process is smoother and fairer. The composition is dynamic, with the townsgirl at the center, her sleeves fluttering as she explains the “good digitalization” of shared trust.
**Dialogue**: "Look how easy it is when we share!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At twilight, the townsgirl sits by a stream, writing a waka on a strip of paper, her brush poised gracefully. Her expression is serene yet determined. The short poem appears beside her as vertical Japanese text on a tanzaku.
**Tanka (translation)**: "The circle of trust connects people, becoming the wind; the invisible threads change the world."
**Tanka (original)**: 「  
信の環  
人ぞつなぎて  
風となる  
見えぬ糸こそ  
世を変へにけり  
」
