### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl stands in front of a lively Edo marketplace, observing merchants and scholars exchanging greetings. She reflects on the nature of introductions, pondering why people only share their names and trades. A gentle breeze lifts her paper fan, and the wooden signs and tiled roofs of Edo create a picturesque backdrop.
**Dialogue**: “Why do people introduce themselves only by name and trade?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a candle-lit study filled with scrolls and foreign books, the townsgirl opens a newly imported book titled "Volume Two of Self-Introduction." From the pages, a faint image of a wise scholar resembling Takashi Yokoi appears, depicted as a calm and intellectual man in simple robes, with neatly tied hair and soft eyes. He gestures toward her with a brush, conveying a message of future possibilities.
**Dialogue**: “Speak not of what was, but of what will be.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl stands on a small stage at a local salon, introducing herself to the townsfolk and artisans. Rather than listing her skills, she shares her dream of weaving trust and knowledge across worlds. The captivated audience leans in, and a child whispers that she sounds like the future. The scene is illuminated by lantern light, highlighting her confidence.
**Dialogue**: “To weave trust and knowledge across worlds.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Under the moonlight at her writing desk, the townsgirl composes a waka poem with brush and ink. The verse appears on a tanzaku beside her, surrounded by the quiet night air and the distant sound of temple bells.
**Tanka (translation)**: “Speaking of the future, weaving threads of trust, becoming a voice, deeper than names, a path that connects people.”
**Tanka (original)**: 「未来語り　信の糸編む　声となり  
　名よりも深く　人つなぐ道」
