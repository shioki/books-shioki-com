### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a quiet Edo street at dusk, the townsgirl overhears a couple quarreling — the man retreats into silence while the woman pleads for him to talk. She pauses, tilting her head with curiosity as she wonders why hearts so close can drift apart. The background features wooden eaves and paper lanterns typical of a merchant district.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a candle-lit study, the townsgirl is engrossed in a foreign-bound book about the balance of humors and the nature of men and women. Above her, a hanging scroll portrait depicts two imagined scholars: a Western sage with gentle eyes and wavy hair, and a calm Japanese thinker in traditional attire holding a brush. Their serene expressions seem to guide her understanding as she takes notes in the margin.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, she observes a neighbor couple again — this time, she gently suggests the husband take solitary time to calm his mind, while advising the wife to share her heart through gentle words. The couple shows humorous surprise as they nod in relief, realizing each other's needs. The townsgirl smiles, holding her fan like a teacher's baton.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, under the moonlight by the window, she writes a waka poem onto a small tanzaku strip, her brush moving softly as she reflects on human hearts.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: Even if we differ, if we acknowledge each other, harmony will arise, and love will grow into skill.  
**Tanka (original)**: 「違ふとも  
　認め合へば  
　和となりて  
　愛は育つる  
　技となるなり」
