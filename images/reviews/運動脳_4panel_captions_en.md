### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a temple library, the townsgirl is engrossed in a rare imported text that discusses the connection between body and mind. An illustration in the margin depicts a calm, scholarly foreign man, imagined as Anders Hansen, with neat short hair and gentle eyes, dressed in European scholar robes. Next to him, a graceful Japanese woman resembling Yumiko Mifune translates the text with a serene smile. The townsgirl's eyes light up with realization as she absorbs their insights about how movement can awaken the brain.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At dawn along the Sumida River, the townsgirl runs lightly in her kimono, her sleeves tied back and hair flowing behind her. She counts her breaths rhythmically, inspired by her reading. Fishermen and vendors watch her in surprise as she laughs, feeling her thoughts become clear and vibrant. The ink lines illustrate her motion and energy, with her footsteps splashing in puddles like brushstrokes of life.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That evening, the townsgirl sits by the light of a lamp, joyfully writing a waka on a hanging scroll. Her cheeks are flushed with happiness as her brush moves gracefully across the paper. In her short poem, she captures the essence of the book’s message—that movement awakens the mind and harmonizes emotion.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: With every movement, both heart and brain become clear, the waves of thought settle quietly.
