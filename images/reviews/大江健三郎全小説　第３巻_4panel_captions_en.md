### Panel 1
**Scene**: In the quiet streets of Edo at dusk, the townsgirl observes townspeople bowing deeply before an official's banner, pondering the need for comfort in authority. Lantern-lit shopfronts and wooden houses create a backdrop of social order and constraint.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside her small study, the townsgirl opens a heavy Western book filled with strange marks. From the shadows, the faint image of an elderly scholar resembling Ōe Kenzaburō appears, whispering about "the gaze of others" and "the rebirth of the self." The girl listens intently, her ink brush trembling.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, she stands before a polished copper mirror, seeing the faces of townspeople instead of her own reflection. Boldly smiling, she grabs her abacus and notebook, stepping into the street to teach children arithmetic, declaring her freedom from judgment. The dynamic composition suggests liberation with swirling ink strokes.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, under a paper lantern, she writes a waka on a narrow tanzaku, her expression serene. The final panel radiates quiet luminosity, with a soft halo of ink wash surrounding her as she completes her verse.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: She sheds her image reflected in others' eyes, lighting a flame within her own shadow.

映るわが身を  
脱ぎすてて
