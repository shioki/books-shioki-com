### Panel 1
**Scene**: At dusk on a lively Edo street, the young townsgirl walks among merchants and lanterns, holding her abacus and lost in thought. The chatter of traders about profits and time fills the air, blending with the glow of paper lanterns and wooden storefronts.  
**Dialogue**: “Is happiness also a calculation?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a quiet temple study lined with scrolls and foreign books, she opens a Dutch-bound volume titled *Jinsei Sekkei*. Beside her appears the calm figure of an older scholar, imagined from her thoughts — his expression thoughtful, fan in hand. He points to a diagram of three circles labeled “financial,” “human,” and “social.” The townsgirl listens intently, eyes shining with curiosity.  
**Dialogue**: (No spoken words, only silent understanding between teacher and student.)

### Panel 3
**Scene**: That night, by the warm glow of a lantern in her small room, she experiments with her own “capital balance.” She arranges coins, study scrolls, and letters from friends into piles marked “money,” “skill,” and “people.” Her cat watches as she laughs, realizing that happiness grows not from abundance but from harmony. Ink splashes as she tips one pile to even them out.  
**Dialogue**: “Balance… that’s the true wealth.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dawn, kneeling by the window, she writes a tanka on a hanging scroll. Morning light filters through the shōji, illuminating her serene face as she finishes her brushstroke.  
**Dialogue**: (She whispers the poem softly.)  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
“When counted, the sources of joy are three —  
wealth, heart, and the bonds of friends.”  
**Tanka (original)**:  
「かぞえれば　幸の資本　三つなり  
　金と心と　友のえにしを」
