### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a lively Edo marketplace, a young townsgirl in kimono walks among merchants and shoppers. The air is filled with talk of money troubles and scarcity. She pauses, holding her abacus close, her gaze thoughtful as she wonders whether it is truly coins that are lacking—or something deeper. Wooden storefronts and busy stalls frame the tension between wealth and daily life.  
**Dialogue**: “Is it really coin that is scarce—or something else?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her dimly lit study, the townsgirl unrolls a Dutch economics manuscript beside a Japanese arithmetic text. A hanging scroll shows the portrait of a middle-aged scholar—Manabu Tauchi—whose calm, intelligent eyes seem to speak softly of shifting values: that the limits of money are moving toward people themselves. She listens in awe, realization dawning in her expression.  
**Dialogue**: (Imagined voice) “Money’s limits are shifting toward people.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At dawn, she joins her neighbors in the communal rice-milling yard. Instead of competing, she shows them how to share the work. Laughter rises as they take turns, rice husks flying in the morning light. Her flowing sleeves and bright face capture the moment she understands that abundance grows from cooperation, not hoarding.  
**Dialogue**: (Neighbors laughing) “Working together makes it lighter!”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Evening falls. By the glow of a paper lantern, she sits quietly with brush in hand, writing a tanka on a slender strip of paper. The room is still; her expression serene, filled with calm insight.  
**Dialogue**: (None)  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
“The shadow of coin—  
a mirror reflecting human ties;  
sharing our unease,  
we also share the hope  
that binds us together.”  
**Tanka (original)**:  
「貨の影　人のつながり　映す鏡  
　不安をわかち　希望ともなう」
