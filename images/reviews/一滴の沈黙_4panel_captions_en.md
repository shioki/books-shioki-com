### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a narrow Edo alley, the townsgirl walks past a bustling market filled with arguing merchants and a silent, bowed student. She pauses, feeling a divide between the lively atmosphere and the weary presence. The mood is quiet yet tense, as if “unheard voices” linger in the air. 
**Dialogue**: “Why do some toil and never bloom?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a candle-lit study, the townsgirl studies a Dutch book and a scroll of social codes. A hanging scroll features a wise man resembling the author, depicted as a calm, middle-aged scholar with thoughtful eyes and a stern expression. She reflects on his teachings about justice and empathy.
**Dialogue**: “To see the unseen pain… that is true knowledge.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At dawn, she visits a riverside where laborers from various lands work side by side. Edo citizens and foreign sailors exchange words. She steps forward, offering a bamboo cup of water to a weary man with a smile. The sunlight reflecting on the river resembles “a single drop of silence.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In her quiet room, she dips her brush in ink and writes a waka on a hanging scroll. Her face radiates understanding and compassion. The short poem appears in vertical Japanese calligraphy on the scroll.
**Tanka (translation)**: In silence, a drop seeps, bringing salvation; listen to the light of the voiceless.
**Tanka (original)**: 沈黙に  
滲むひとしずく  
救いあり  
声なき者の  
光を聴けり
