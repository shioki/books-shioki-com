### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through an Edo street at dawn, observing two samurai crossing swords in the distance. She pauses, feeling a mix of unease and fascination by their intense movements, as the atmosphere is thick with the weight of "life and death." She quietly wonders about the meaning of "true strength" beyond just winning or losing.
**Dialogue**: "What does true strength mean?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit study filled with scrolls and Dutch books, the townsgirl reads a newly arrived manuscript titled "Chambara." A wise scholar, resembling Sato Kenichi, appears beside her as a spirit within the pages. He is depicted as a calm, thoughtful man in simple robes, with intelligent eyes and a philosophical presence. He gestures with a brush, illustrating the interconnectedness of fear, creation, and ethics like flowing ink.
**Dialogue**: "Fear, creation, and ethics intertwine..."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At the riverside, the townsgirl practices sword forms with a wooden stick, trying to replicate what she read. Her stance wavers but steadies as she takes a deep breath, embracing her fear instead of denying it. In the water's reflection, she sees not an opponent but herself, smiling with newfound realization as the wind disturbs the surface.
**Dialogue**: "I see myself, not an opponent."

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, illuminated by the light of a paper lantern, she kneels and writes a waka on a narrow tanzaku, her brush trembling gently. The ink glistens as she whispers the poem.
**Tanka (translation)**: "Knowing fear, I transcend; the sword becomes my essence, breaking tradition, creating myself."
**Tanka (original)**: 「恐れ知り  
　越えてゆく身の  
　剣（つるぎ）かな  
　伝統破り  
　己を創（つく）る」
