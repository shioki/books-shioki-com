### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through a bustling Edo street market at dusk, observing merchants negotiating prices. She stops, intrigued, as she sees a beggar sharing his rice ball with a stray cat. A gentle breeze lifts her sleeve while she quietly wonders, “Why do gifts that expect nothing feel warmer than trade?” The background features wooden stalls, paper lanterns, and townspeople exchanging goods.
**Dialogue**: “Why do gifts that expect nothing feel warmer than trade?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her dimly lit room illuminated by candlelight, the townsgirl studies a Dutch book alongside a philosophical scroll. An imagined portrait of the author “近内悠太” appears in the scroll's margin, depicting a calm, thoughtful man in simple robes with kind eyes and tied-back hair, exuding quiet wisdom. The portrait seems to communicate with her, hinting at the unseen connections of generosity. Her face lights up with understanding.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The following morning, the townsgirl discreetly leaves a small handmade abacus at the door of a struggling child’s home, smiling gently as she walks away unnoticed. A neighbor misinterprets it as a forgotten item, leading to a humorous misunderstanding. The scene captures her movement — her kimono sleeve fluttering and the abacus sparkling in the sunlight — symbolizing an act of “gift without name.”
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, she sits by a paper window with a brush in hand, composing a waka on a tanzaku. The flickering candlelight casts shadows as she contemplates the invisible kindness that sustains the world. The short poem she writes beside her reads:
**Tanka (translation)**: “The gifts received,  
Unnoticed, breathe life into  
A world that shines bright,  
Illuminated by love,  
A nameless act of giving.”
**Tanka (original)**: 「受けしもの  
気づかぬままに  
息をして  
名なき贈りの  
世を照らすかな」
