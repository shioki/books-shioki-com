### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a lively Edo street, observing a wealthy merchant scolding a group of women for their "lack of respect." She pauses, deep in thought, sensing that something about this demand feels off. The background features wooden townhouses and fluttering merchant banners.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a quiet study, the townsgirl studies a foreign book about justice and dignity. Next to her, the imagined figure of the author "Brady Mikako" appears, a calm androgynous scholar with soft, intelligent eyes, dressed in a simple robe that blends Japanese and Western styles. She gestures toward the book, teaching that "respect" must come from equality, not hierarchy.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl helps her neighbors repair a dilapidated tenement. Instead of waiting for officials, they share tools and rice, laughing together. She realizes their acts of "giving" and "supporting each other" create a new kind of respect. The ink lines capture the lively movement of hands passing wooden planks and the smiles of mutual aid.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dusk, the townsgirl sits on the engawa, gazing at the lanterns lit across the alley. She writes a waka on a small tanzaku, her brushstroke firm yet tender.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: Raising our voices,  
Hands supporting each other,  
Lighting the lanterns,  
Beyond material goods,  
A precious bond we share.  
**Tanka (original)**: 「声あげて  
　支えあう手に  
　灯をともす  
　貨の外にも  
　尊き絆」
