### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through the serene evening streets of Edo, pausing in front of a small sake shop. She gazes curiously at the laughing customers inside, her eyes reflecting a desire to understand the meaning of "sake" to them. A lantern sways gently, creating a tender yet introspective atmosphere.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit study, the townsgirl is engrossed in reading a mysterious imported essay titled "Reading Sake." Two imagined figures appear on the pages as her teachers: one is a calm scholar with short black hair and glasses, and the other is a gentle figure with slightly tousled hair and a warm smile. They discuss how sake connects hearts and reveals human fragility, while she listens in awe, her brush hovering in midair.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, the townsgirl visits a small night stall selling amazake. Instead of drinking, she listens to the conversations of the townsfolk—the lonely merchant and the laughing craftsman. She sketches notes gently, realizing that "to read sake" means to read people. The scene is lively yet intimate, with reflections of faces in the sake cups.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Back in her room, moonlight spills over her paper and inkstone. With a soft smile, she holds her brush poised to write a waka on a hanging scroll. The poem appears in elegant Japanese calligraphy:  
「酔うひとも　読むひともまた　ひとつなり　こころの盃　かわすあかとき」  
She gazes out the window, serene, as if savoring both words and life itself.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: Both the drinker and the reader become one, exchanging heartfelt cups at dawn.  
**Tanka (original)**: 酔うひとも　読むひともまた　ひとつなり　こころの盃　かわすあかとき
