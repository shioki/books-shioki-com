### Panel 1
**Scene**: In the lively Edo marketplace, the townsgirl observes two neighbors in a heated argument about what constitutes “proper” behavior. She tilts her head, feeling a subtle discomfort — the invisible line between “normal” and “strange.” The background is filled with shop signs, paper lanterns, and bustling townspeople, all depicted in monochrome ink strokes.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a dimly lit study filled with scrolls and Dutch books, the townsgirl consults her mentor, a man with short blond hair and glasses, who has a gentle, slightly apologetic demeanor reminiscent of author Ryo Asai. He points at a diagram in a book, explaining that even “rightness” can cause harm. The ink shading highlights his calm yet penetrating gaze.
**Dialogue**: "Even 'rightness' can wound."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At dusk by the riverbank, the townsgirl experiments by drawing two circles in the sand, one labeled “normal” and the other “different.” A group of children and workers gathers, looking puzzled as she allows the waves to blur both circles away. Her face lights up with a mix of realization and humor — perhaps “rightness” disappears when touched by the tide.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In her small room illuminated by a single lantern, the townsgirl holds her brush and writes a waka on a hanging scroll, her expression calm yet determined. The poem is written vertically.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: "Rightness dissolves like shadows in the waves; even without understanding, I wish to share this."
**Tanka (original)**:  
「  
正しさは  
波にとけゆく  
影のごと  
理解(わか)らずとも  
共にありたし  
」
