### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a lively Edo marketplace, observing merchants shouting prices and customers haggling. She notices a wealthy merchant greedily counting his coins, feeling a sense of unease. In the background, paper lanterns sway and children play. 
**Dialogue**: She murmurs to herself, questioning if money could be used for kindness instead of pride.

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study filled with scrolls and Dutch books, she unrolls a parchment that depicts three philosophers in Edo-style portraits. One is an elderly man with a wise face, another is a youthful, mischievous figure, and the last is a tall, contemplative person. Their drawn faces seem to convey messages about “money as a gift” and “a society without humiliation.” Candlelight flickers, creating deep shadows.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl stands in a neighborhood scene, giving a few coins to an elderly neighbor who has lost her pouch. The neighbor smiles gratefully, and soon other townsfolk begin to share goods and food. The coins appear midair like glowing circles, symbolizing a “circulating ball.” The scene radiates warmth and movement amidst the simplicity of Edo streets.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Evening descends. The townsgirl kneels by the window, brush in hand, writing a waka on a small tanzaku slip. Moonlight filters through the paper shoji, illuminating her peaceful face.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: Even if humble, a heart that circulates is strong, bringing no shame; such a world is truly abundant.

めぐるこころは
