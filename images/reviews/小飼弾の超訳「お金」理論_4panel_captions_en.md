### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through the bustling Edo marketplace, observing merchants exchanging coins and goods. She pauses, slightly frowning as she contemplates, “What truly gives silver its worth?” The background is filled with lively stalls and glinting abacuses.  
**Dialogue**: “What truly gives silver its worth?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study filled with Dutch and Japanese texts, she opens a book titled "The Theory of Money." A spectral mentor emerges from the pages — an imagined figure inspired by the author Dan Kogai: an intelligent-looking man with calm eyes and a scholarly expression. He gestures toward a chalkboard displaying the kanji "Trust = Value."  
**Dialogue**: “Trust = Value.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl conducts an experiment in her neighborhood, issuing handwritten “trust notes” among children and artisans, observing how they trade them like currency. The scene is filled with lively expressions of surprise and laughter as her experiment proves successful, showcasing the invisible power of shared belief.  
**Dialogue**: “Look! They’re trading them!”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As evening falls, the townsgirl sits by lamplight with a brush in hand, gazing at her notes with a soft smile. Beside her, she writes a waka that encapsulates the essence of her findings.  
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue in this panel]  
**Tanka (translation)**: "Threads of trust, woven by human hands, wealth and knowledge too, circulate like light."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「信の糸  
人の手により  
紡がれて  
富も知もまた  
巡るひかりよ」
