### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through a lively Edo marketplace, observing merchants shouting prices and customers haggling. She stops to watch the exchange of coins and goods, pondering aloud—“Is wealth the same as happiness?” The background features wooden stalls, hanging lanterns, and townsfolk sharing smiles and sighs.
**Dialogue**: “Is wealth the same as happiness?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a tranquil terakoya at dusk, the townsgirl studies an imported Dutch text on economics next to a thoughtful scholar. He has a serene expression, round spectacles, and a calm demeanor, inspired by the essence of Maruyama Shunichi. He gestures toward a diagram of gears and waves, explaining that “the flow of gold is also the flow of desire.” Scrolls and an abacus are scattered across the tatami.
**Dialogue**: “The flow of gold is also the flow of desire.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At home, the townsgirl experiments by arranging pebbles and rice grains to simulate a market, attempting to balance “growth” and “well-being.” The pebbles topple over and spill; she chuckles softly, realizing that achieving perfect balance is unattainable. Her playful experiment illustrates the conflict between rational calculation and human emotion.

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At night, illuminated by the glow of a paper lantern, the townsgirl writes a waka on a small tanzaku. Her face reflects serenity and contemplation as she gazes over the rooftops of Edo. The short poem reads:  
“The wind of wealth reflects the heart like a mirror; not in fear, but with reverence, we shall live together.”  
**Tanka (translation)**: The wind of wealth reflects the heart like a mirror; not in fear, but with reverence, we shall live together.  
**Tanka (original)**: 「富の風　心を映す　鏡なり　恐れず畏れ　共に生きなん」
