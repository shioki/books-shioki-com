### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a bustling Edo marketplace, observing a group of workers arguing over unfair pay near a rice storehouse. She tilts her head thoughtfully, holding her abacus.
**Dialogue**: “If everyone could live without worry, what would become of work?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study filled with scrolls and Dutch books, she reads a translated treatise imagined as “Minna ni Okane wo Kubuttara.” Two ethereal teacher figures appear from the pages — one a calm Western woman and the other a composed Japanese woman in kimono, explaining dignity, equality, and freedom through faint calligraphic diagrams of coins.

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl stages a playful “experiment” in the alley, giving small paper money tokens to children and vendors, inviting them to “choose freely.” The scene becomes lively as people engage in painting, resting, sharing food, and smiling, while she looks surprised at the transformative power of generosity.

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Night falls. The townsgirl sits by lamplight with a brush and inkstone, gazing at the quiet street beyond the shōji, writing a tanka on a hanging scroll.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]
**Tanka (translation)**: Distributing money, I think of its preciousness, like life itself. In the wind of freedom, my heart opens.
**Tanka (original)**: 配る金  
　思ふは尊き  
　いのちかな  
　自由の風に  
　心ひらけり
