### Panel 1
**Scene**: At dusk on an Edo street, the young townsgirl walks past a clockmaker adjusting a large Dutch-style clock. She pauses, intrigued and slightly puzzled by the notion that “time” can be measured so precisely. Lanterns flicker to life among narrow wooden houses, the quiet street flowing gently with the rhythm of evening.  
**Dialogue**: *“Can something as fleeting as time truly be captured?”*

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study, she leans over a Dutch scientific book describing devices said to “preserve” or “freeze” time. On the wall hangs a portrait of a calm scholar—sharp-eyed, unkempt hair, holding a quill—whose gaze seems to follow her, silently guiding her through the invisible layers of time and existence.  
**Dialogue**: *“If time can be held still… what becomes of the self that moves within it?”*

### Panel 3
**Scene**: She experiments with a sundial, marking its shadow every few breaths. Suddenly, she notices the marks overlap—the past and present coexist. Startled, then amused, she laughs softly as a breeze scatters her notes. A cat leaps through the frame, a fleeting symbol of time’s circular dance.  
**Dialogue**: *“So even moments can meet again…”*

### Panel 4
**Scene**: By candlelight, she writes a waka on thin paper, her expression serene. She reflects that to “disappear” might be another form of freedom, as the flame trembles beside her.  
**Dialogue**: *“To vanish is also to live, perhaps.”*  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
Layers of time—  
memory and town dissolve,  
mingling as one;  
to fade away is yet  
another shape of life.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
時の層  
記憶と街と  
溶けあいて  
消ゆるもまたは  
生のかたちに
