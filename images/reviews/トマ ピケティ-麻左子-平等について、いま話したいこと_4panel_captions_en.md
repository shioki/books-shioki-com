### Panel 1
**Scene**: The young Edo townsgirl walks through a lively street filled with merchants bargaining and children playing go. She pauses, frowning slightly as she overhears two men boasting about their wealth while ignoring a beggar nearby. Wooden storefronts and paper lanterns line the background, capturing the bustle of Edo life.  
**Dialogue**: (Thought) “Is value only what money can measure?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study lit by an oil lamp, she opens a Dutch book and scrolls of arithmetic. On the wall hang sketches of three thinkers: a calm European scholar with intelligent eyes, an elder philosopher with a kind, reflective face, and a composed Japanese woman scholar in kimono. Their portraits seem to speak from the scrolls, debating equality, dignity, and merit. The townsgirl listens intently, eyes wide with inspiration.  
**Dialogue**: (Silent contemplation as the portraits “speak” in her imagination)

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, she gathers townspeople—fishmongers, children, and an elderly teacher—in the temple courtyard for an impromptu meeting. Drawing a circle in the dirt, she explains that everyone’s role holds meaning beyond coins or rank. Laughter rises as even a cat joins the “discussion.” The scene is viewed from above, the circle forming a symbol of equality.  
**Dialogue**: (Townsgirl) “Each of us gives value to the world in our own way.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Evening. The townsgirl sits by a paper window, brush in hand, composing a waka on a hanging tanzaku. The candlelight flickers beside her calm face as she reflects on the day’s thoughts.  
**Dialogue**: (Softly) “True worth lies in the heart that honors others.”  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
The sound of gold—  
Do not weigh human worth by it;  
In hearts that revere,  
There alone does equality  
Truly find its home.

人の価値を
