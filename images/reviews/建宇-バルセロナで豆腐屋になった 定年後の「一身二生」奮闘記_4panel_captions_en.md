### Panel 1
**Scene**: On a quiet Edo morning, the young townsgirl pauses before a tofu vendor steaming fresh curds. She gazes at the humble tofu, wondering how something so simple could cross oceans and touch hearts. The market bustles with merchants and townsfolk; her face glows with curiosity and a longing for distant lands.  
**Dialogue**: “How can something so modest travel across seas and hearts?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a dimly lit study, she pores over Dutch books and scrolls about trade, culture, and life abroad. Behind her, the imagined figure of a wise scholar with silver hair and gentle eyes appears—his presence calm and instructive, like a memory guiding her. He tells her stories of “a man who made tofu in Barcelona.” The ink lines radiate warmth and quiet wisdom.  
**Dialogue**: Scholar: “Even tofu can find new life in foreign lands.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, the townsgirl experiments in her courtyard, pressing tofu with a wooden mold as curious neighbors peek over the fence. She laughs as the tofu crumbles apart, soy milk splashing like ink drops. The scene is lively and humorous, full of motion and spirit.  
**Dialogue**: “Failure is but a step toward flavor!”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Evening falls. By the soft glow of a paper lantern, she sits with brush in hand, gazing at her imperfect tofu. On a hanging tanzaku, she writes a reflective waka that captures her resolve to begin anew. Her expression is serene, her spirit quietly determined.  
**Dialogue**: (none)  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
“Unaging heart,  
kneading tofu, I set forth—  
on a new journey,  
body and spirit both  
renewed in living.”  
**Tanka (original)**:  
「老い知らず  
豆腐をこねて  
旅に出る  
身もこころも  
あらたに生くる」
