### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a bustling Edo town street, a young townsgirl pauses with a basket of scrolls, troubled by whispers from neighbors about someone who "does not read the air." The lively alley is adorned with laundry and paper lanterns, reflecting her mixed feelings of curiosity and unease as she contemplates the meaning of living under societal scrutiny.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a candle-lit study, the townsgirl is engrossed in a rare imported book and a handwritten scroll titled "A Cheerful Life Consultation." The illustration in the book depicts a calm middle-aged man resembling the author, imagined as a thoughtful Edo scholar. His composed demeanor and gentle eyes convey wisdom as he seems to advise her: “To live freely, understand both structure and heart.” The townsgirl listens intently, her quill trembling with inspiration.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At a neighborhood meeting, the townsgirl faces a group of gossiping townspeople. Instead of engaging in conflict, she bows politely and speaks calmly, leaving with quiet dignity. By the riverbank, she writes in her notebook, a faint smile on her face as she finds peace in self-understanding rather than seeking approval. Cherry petals float on the water, symbolizing her realization that "escaping" can be an act of courage.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the quiet of night, the townsgirl kneels by the lamplight, brush in hand, as she composes a tanka on a small scroll. Her serene expression is illuminated by the soft glow of the lamp.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: To escape the world, the heart is what I protect;  
Without pretense, I embrace the spring moon's shadow.  
**Tanka (original)**: 世間より　逃げて守れる　心こそ  
　構えず澄ます　春の月影
