### Panel 1
**Scene**: In the lively market street of Edo, the townsgirl stops to listen to a storyteller narrating a tale about a boy who lived four different lives. She tilts her head in curiosity, pondering the question, “Can one life truly have many paths?” Merchants can be heard shouting in the background, and paper lanterns sway gently over the canal bridge.
**Dialogue**: “Can one life truly have many paths?”

Beside her, the ghostly figures of two mentors appear: a tall Western man with deep eyes and a calm demeanor, and a gentle scholar with a thoughtful gaze and refined presence. They guide her through the mysterious symbols of fate and choice written in Dutch and English.

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, the townsgirl draws diagrams in the dust of the street, demonstrating to children how one choice can branch into four, then eight. A sudden gust of wind scatters her chalk lines, and she laughs, realizing that even her lesson is subject to chance. The children clap in amazement as she bows to the wind, smiling.

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dusk, sitting by the riverbank, the townsgirl opens her notebook, dips her brush, and writes a tanka that captures the essence of the book’s themes of impermanence, choice, and rebirth. The river sparkles with fragments of light, symbolizing the merging of four lives into one.
