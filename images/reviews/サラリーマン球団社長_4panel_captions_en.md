### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a bustling Edo street, surrounded by shops and the sounds of townspeople arguing about who truly runs the baseball team of merchants. She tilts her head, intrigued by the concept of unseen masters controlling the lives of ordinary people. The background is filled with vibrant signboards and a lively longhouse scene, reflecting the energy of the town. Her eyes sparkle with curiosity as she contemplates whether ordinary workers could alter the existing structure.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her dimly lit study, illuminated by the warm glow of candlelight, the townsgirl unrolls a Dutch arithmetic scroll alongside a mysterious imported book. From the book's illustration, a serene older man appears, resembling the author Kiyotake Hidetoshi, with calm eyes and neatly tied hair, dressed in a scholar's robe. He points with a brush at a diagram labeled “BOS” (Baseball Operation System) written in Edo calligraphy. The townsgirl listens intently, her eyes wide with excitement as she absorbs the lesson on rational reform through data and patience.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl stands confidently in the town square, gathering local merchants and artisans around her. With her abacus and chalk, she introduces a “data board” to fairly track rice sales and deliveries. The crowd reacts with surprise; one elder scoffs while another nods in admiration. The dynamic composition features bold ink strokes that highlight the contrast between tradition and innovation, capturing the essence of quiet rebellion and humor as the young woman takes the lead.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As dusk settles, the townsgirl sits by the riverside, brush in hand, gazing at the rippling water that reflects the soft glow of lantern light. She writes her waka on a tanzaku, embodying the spirit of reform and perseverance. The elegant poem is inscribed beside her in Japanese vertical text, expressing her thoughts and aspirations.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: It is the outliers who bind reason and emotion, becoming the wind that endures and blooms into the flower of transformation.  
**Tanka (original)**: 異端こそ  
　理と情（こころ）結ぶ  
　風となり  
　耐えて咲かせる  
　変革の花
