### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through an Edo street filled with storytellers sharing tales of heroic battles. She pauses with a slight frown, contemplating whether wars truly shape nations or simply lead to their destruction. The background features paper lanterns and banners depicting medieval knights and castles, merging Edo and European imagery.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit temple study room, the townsgirl is engrossed in a Dutch history book next to a scroll titled "英仏百年戦争". A teacherly figure, imagined as author Sato Kenichi, appears beside her. He is a calm, thoughtful man with composed eyes and slightly wavy hair tied back, dressed in scholarly robes. He gestures toward a map of Europe, explaining how feudal houses evolved into nations through war and reform.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl is experimenting with her abacus and inkbrush, sketching diagrams of armies transforming into organized "国家 (kokka)" structures, drawing parallels with her town's rice-tax system. Suddenly, she bursts into laughter, realizing that even the rules of a small village reflect the same principle — structure arising from conflict. Her imagination creatively overlays images of European knights with Edo bureaucrats.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In quiet reflection by lamplight, the townsgirl writes a waka on a small tanzaku slip. Her expression is serene yet inspired as the brush glides across the paper.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: The flames of war shape the nation, casting shadows that rise in the hearts of the people, where flags stand tall.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
戦の火　  
国をかたどる　  
影となり　  
民のこころに　  
旗は立つなり
