### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a lively Edo neighborhood next to a nagaya tenement, a young townsgirl draws water from a communal wooden well. She notices dead tiny fish in the drainage channel and an oily ripple, while a charcoal dealer quietly removes used filter sacks at dawn. Despite the official notice claiming the water is safe, her curious expression reflects her doubts as she studies the scene, with her face mirrored upside down in the clear water.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a cramped study resembling a terakoya, the townsgirl calculates contamination routes surrounded by wasan books, an abacus, Dutch-language volumes, and charcoal samples. Her rangaku mentor, a fictional scholarly figure, points from the well to a charcoal sack and a disposal-site map, highlighting the chain of contamination. Dramatic ink shadows suggest hidden information and competing interests.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: A dynamic bird’s-eye view shows the townsgirl racing across rooftops and leaping onto a moving handcart, chasing the supposedly "purified" used charcoal. An alley cat mischievously tears open a sack, spilling contaminated charcoal. Merchants, an official, and a waste hauler point fingers at each other, while her hand-drawn map and evidence expose their secret route. The visual pun highlights the difference between legality and accountability.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dusk beside the well, the townsgirl sits quietly with a brush and a narrow tanzaku poetry strip. The water reflects tangled silhouettes of warehouses and officials, hinting at unseen power structures. Her expression is solemn yet determined, embodying the idea that knowledge and the right to ask questions are vital for protecting life.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: Even clear water  
Hides poison in the dark.  
Tracing the path,  
With the right to know,  
I will protect my life.  
**Tanka (original)**: 澄む水も  
闇に毒あり  
跡を追い  
知る権利もて  
命守らん
