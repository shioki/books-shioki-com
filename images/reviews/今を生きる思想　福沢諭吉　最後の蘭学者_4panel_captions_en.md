### Panel 1
**Scene**: In an Edo street at dusk, the townsgirl pauses by a bookstall, intrigued by scholars debating civilization and freedom. The warm lantern light flickers over woodblock prints and Dutch books.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a modest study filled with scrolls and Dutch texts, the townsgirl reads intently. A calm, middle-aged scholar, resembling Okubo Takeharu, gestures towards a Dutch book, explaining the power of translation.
**Dialogue**: "Through translation, new worlds are born."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: By a riverside, the townsgirl debates with townsfolk, using chalk to illustrate “freedom through discussion.” The crowd listens, surprised yet inspired, as cherry petals scatter in the wind.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At night, the townsgirl writes by candlelight, brush poised gracefully. A short waka appears beside her on a tanzaku slip, encapsulating the book's essence.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: To translate is to give birth to reason; it is the power to stir the winds of civilization.
