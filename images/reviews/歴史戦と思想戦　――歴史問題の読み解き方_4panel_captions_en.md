### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a lively Edo street near a temple school, a young townsgirl overhears two elders arguing about history. One elder passionately claims that "the old empire was glorious," while the other insists on the importance of learning from the past. The townsgirl tilts her head, caught in a mix of pride and reflection, surrounded by wooden signposts and children practicing their writing. The atmosphere is bright yet filled with questions.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a serene study illuminated by shoji light, the townsgirl is engrossed in reading a foreign book with Dutch letters and another titled "History Wars and Ideological Battles." A calm scholar resembling Yamazaki Masahiro appears beside her, dressed in a simple scholar's kimono, holding a brush over scrolls. He points to a diagram that separates the words "Empire" and "Nation" with a line, symbolizing clarity of thought. The girl listens intently, her eyes sparkling with understanding.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: In a bustling market, the townsgirl enthusiastically explains her thoughts to her friends using an abacus and chalk on a board. She demonstrates that "mixing the old empire and today's Japan is like adding different measures and calling them equal." The crowd reacts with laughter, surprised yet understanding. A child exclaims, "Then truth is in the measure itself!" The scene is dynamic, filled with movement and humor.  
**Dialogue**: "Then truth is in the measure itself!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the evening, the townsgirl sits on the engawa under the warm glow of lantern light, brush in hand, gazing at the moon reflected in an inkstone. She writes a waka on a small tanzaku, her expression serene yet determined. The poem is elegantly written in vertical script.  
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: "When the moon reflects in the mirror of mistakes, the shadows of the heart become clear and bright."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「  
過ちを  
映す鏡に  
月すめば  
心の影も  
澄みて明らむ  
」
