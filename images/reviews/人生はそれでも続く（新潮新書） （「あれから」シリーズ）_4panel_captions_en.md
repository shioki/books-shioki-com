### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through an Edo street after a sudden downpour, observing townspeople quietly rebuilding their market stalls damaged by the rain. She pauses, sensing the quiet strength in their movements. An old signboard in the background reads “Will it be sunny tomorrow?” Her expression reflects curiosity and empathy as she contemplates how people continue after hardship.

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a candle-lit study filled with scrolls and Dutch books, the townsgirl reads a bound journal titled “After That Record.” An imagined figure emerges from the pages — a calm, collective presence representing the “Yomiuri Shimbun Social Affairs ‘Arekara’ reporting team.” They appear as shadowy scholars in plain robes and round spectacles, embodying a chorus of witnesses. The townsgirl listens intently as they discuss people's lives beyond loss.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl applies the lesson to her own life by helping a neighbor who lost her store restart a small math class for children in the alley. The scene is lively, with children counting on abacuses and laughter filling the air. The townsgirl smiles, realizing that "continuing" is an act of quiet courage. The brushwork becomes dynamic, emphasizing renewal.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Night falls. The townsgirl sits by a paper lantern, brush in hand, writing a waka on a tanzaku. Her expression is serene yet resolute, reflecting compassion and understanding. The tanzaku reads:
**Tanka (translation)**: After loss, a single leaf can sprout, connecting the days, and living on.
