### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through an Edo street at dusk, pausing thoughtfully with her bamboo arithmetic case as she hears laughter from a family inside a longhouse. The flickering lantern light highlights her introspective question: “What makes a family?” The background depicts a lively Edo neighborhood filled with narrow alleys and paper lanterns.
**Dialogue**: “What makes a family?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small room, the townsgirl sits with a Dutch-bound book and a handwritten scroll under dim candlelight. A serene woman, resembling a calm middle-aged teacher with soft eyes and simple hair, appears in her imagination, smiling kindly. The imagined mentor conveys the message that “Family is not bound by blood, but by shared time.” The townsgirl listens earnestly, brush poised.
**Dialogue**: “Family is not bound by blood, but by shared time.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, the townsgirl helps a neighbor’s child fix a broken toy while the child’s grandmother watches, touched. Sakura petals are blown across the scene by the wind, symbolizing the love and care that travel between people, like passing a baton. The townsgirl's eyes shine with discovery.
**Dialogue**: (none)

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In her quiet room, the townsgirl writes a waka on a hanging scroll, her pen trembling slightly with emotion. The flickering candlelight reflects in her eyes as she smiles softly. The tanzaku features her short poem written in brushstroke Japanese vertical text.
**Dialogue**: (none)
**Tanka (translation)**: Bonds are not merely of blood; they carry the light, blooming in the heart even after parting.
