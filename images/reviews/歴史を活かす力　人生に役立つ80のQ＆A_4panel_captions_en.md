### Panel 1
**Scene**: In an Edo town street at dusk, the townsgirl stands by a temple bulletin, observing elders debating the nature of history. She tilts her head in curiosity, holding her writing brush, pondering the question, “Can the past truly guide the future?” The background features wooden houses, lanterns, and townsfolk engaged in conversation.  
**Dialogue**: “Can the past truly guide the future?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a candle-lit study filled with scrolls and Dutch books, the townsgirl reads a newly copied manuscript titled "The Power of Utilizing History." A wise scholarly figure resembling the author Deguchi Haruaki appears from the pages, an elderly man with calm eyes and a gentle expression, dressed in simple scholar’s robes. He gestures as if teaching, explaining that history is not merely memory but a training for thought. The townsgirl listens intently, her eyes shining with interest.  
**Dialogue**: “History is not memory, but training for thought.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Morning light fills the narrow street as the townsgirl assists her neighbors in resolving a dispute over market prices. She uses “past examples” she learned, referencing a case from ancient China mentioned in the manuscript. The neighbors nod in surprise, while the girl smiles, swiftly moving her brush on a wooden tablet, connecting the past with the present. The scene is filled with a sense of humor and insight.  
**Dialogue**: “Let me share a past example!”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In a tranquil moment by the riverbank, the townsgirl kneels, writing a waka poem on a tanzaku. The wind gently ripples the water, and her expression is serene, illuminated by a sense of understanding. The tanzaku displays the poem in Japanese vertical script.  
**Tanka (translation)**: "Embracing past mistakes, I envision the future, blending them together, becoming a light of knowledge."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「過ぎし日の  
　過ち抱き  
　未来描く  
　混ぜて強まる  
　知の灯となれ」
