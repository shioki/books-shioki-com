### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through a lively Edo street, intrigued by whispers of "karakuri that can write poems and paintings by itself." She pauses, tilting her head in curiosity while holding a bamboo abacus. In the background, a bookshop displays illustrated scrolls of strange mechanical devices labeled "jidō e-utsushi karakuri." Her expression reflects both wonder and unease.

**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit terakoya, the townsgirl is engrossed in a Dutch book about "artificial intelligence" and economic philosophy. A portrait of a calm, scholarly man appears on the screen or scroll, representing author Inoue Tomohiro, depicted as a thoughtful Edo scholar with tied-back hair and a plain kimono. He gestures as if explaining that machines can think without heart, his presence ethereal, resembling a teacher emerging from the book.

**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl experiments with a wooden karakuri automaton that writes letters. It quickly produces elegant sentences but halts when she asks, "Why do you write?" She chuckles softly, recognizing its brilliance yet emptiness. Ink splashes dynamically as she adjusts the brush herself, illustrating human spontaneity and imperfection. The scene is humorous and expressive, reflecting on creativity and control.

**Dialogue**: "Why do you write?"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: The townsgirl sits by lamplight, brush in hand, gazing out the window at the moonlit street. A short waka appears on a hanging tanzaku beside her, written in delicate vertical script.

**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: "The craft of weaving, / Pausing to see the moon's shadow, / Reflects the heart; / The art is human."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「  
機（はた）織る  
手を休め見ゆ　月の影  
心を写す　わざは人なり  
」
