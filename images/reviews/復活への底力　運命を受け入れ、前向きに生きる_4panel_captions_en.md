### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through an Edo street after a sudden spring rain, carrying a small abacus and a Western-style notebook. She overhears an elder lamenting his fate after illness and pauses, gazing at the puddle’s reflection with quiet curiosity, pondering what “accepting fate” truly means. Merchant houses and glowing paper lanterns are in the background.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study lined with scrolls and Dutch books, she reads a newly arrived rangaku text. A portrait of an elderly scholar appears within it, imagined as author Haruaki Deguchi: serene, thoughtful eyes, neatly tied gray hair, scholarly posture in plain robes, exuding calm intellect. His figure seems to speak through the page, teaching that "knowledge and acceptance awaken strength." Candlelight flickers across her face as she listens intently.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The girl visits a local temple school to teach children arithmetic using pebbles and bamboo rods, encouraging a boy frustrated by his mistakes. She smiles and says, “Let’s accept the error first—then learn anew!” The children laugh as she turns failure into play, embodying the lesson of resilience. The composition shows dynamic diagonal energy, with ink splashes symbolizing renewal.  
**Dialogue**: “Let’s accept the error first—then learn anew!”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At night, she sits by lamplight, gazing at the moon reflected in an inkstone, brush poised. On a hanging tanzaku, she writes a waka poem summarizing the book’s essence, her face calm and luminous.  
**Dialogue**: None
