### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through an Edo street at dusk, observing two neighbors engaged in conversation—one is animatedly speaking while the other merely nods with a forced smile. She stops, tilts her head in contemplation, sensing a deeper meaning behind the silence. The flickering light of a paper lantern highlights her curiosity about the true essence of “listening.”
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her dimly lit study room, illuminated by candlelight, the townsgirl reads a newly imported scroll titled “Kiku no Gijutsu.” The scroll features an illustration of a calm scholarly figure, imagined as the author, Kaijin Tohata—a serene man in his forties with gentle eyes and tied-back hair, exuding quiet intellect. His presence conveys that true listening is about having the courage to share silence rather than filling it. The townsgirl listens intently, her brush poised in midair, moved by this realization.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The following day, she sits by the river with a friend who appears troubled. Instead of speaking, she remains quietly by her side, allowing the sounds of the wind and water to fill the silence. Gradually, the friend begins to open up on her own. Their reflections tremble on the water’s surface, symbolizing their mutual presence and unspoken understanding.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As night falls, the townsgirl writes a waka on a hanging scroll, her expression serene yet glowing with empathy. The soft candlelight envelops her brush as she recites a poem that encapsulates the essence of the book.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: If we share silence together, the wind is gentle; even loneliness becomes one heart that listens.  
**Tanka (original)**: 沈黙を  
ともに渡れば  
風やさし  
孤独もひとつ  
聞くこころかな
