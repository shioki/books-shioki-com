### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a bustling Edo town street, the townsgirl observes merchants haggling over prices, her expression one of deep contemplation about the nature of value. A gentle breeze ruffles her sleeve as she ponders aloud, questioning if worth is solely determined by money.
**Dialogue**: “Is worth only counted by coin?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a serene study filled with scrolls and Dutch literature, the townsgirl engages in a thoughtful discussion with an elder scholar, who resembles the calm and gentle Ebanato Tetsuo. He gestures toward a book discussing the philosophy of value, emphasizing that ideals should guide pricing rather than mere costs. She listens with reverence, poised to take notes.
**Dialogue**: “Price should be guided by ideals, not cost.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At a riverside stall, the townsgirl showcases her handmade abacus. When a customer offers a lower price, she responds with a smile, explaining the true value of her work. A crowd gathers, intrigued by her perspective, while a passing monk humorously attempts to calculate the "value of enlightenment," eliciting laughter from the onlookers.
**Dialogue**: “Its price is in the precision it brings, not the wood it’s made of.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As twilight descends, the townsgirl sits by a window, peacefully writing a waka on a narrow strip of paper. Her serene expression reflects the essence of her thoughts, encapsulating the story's themes of sincerity and self-contentment.
**Tanka (translation)**: "When sincerity builds trust, the wind becomes clear; true value lies in the heart's understanding."
**Tanka (original)**: 「誠実に  
　信を築けば  
　風は澄む  
　価(あたい)は心の  
　納得にあり」
