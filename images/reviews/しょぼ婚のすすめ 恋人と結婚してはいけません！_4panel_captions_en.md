### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through the lively Edo marketplace, observing a couple arguing about household expenses. She tilts her head in contemplation, pondering if love alone can support a household. The background features merchant stalls, paper lanterns, and traditional wooden houses.
**Dialogue**: "Can affection truly sustain a home?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her cozy study illuminated by an oil lamp, the townsgirl opens a Dutch book titled “Marriage and Reason.” A mysterious teacher figure, inspired by the author えらいてんちょう, appears—dressed in simple robes, with slightly tousled hair and thin round glasses. He explains through a scroll diagram that marriage is about “mutual cooperation, not passion.” The townsgirl listens intently, her eyes sparkling with curiosity.
**Dialogue**: "Marriage is a system of mutual cooperation, not just passion."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl visits a neighboring couple who are bickering over household chores. She gently advises, “Instead of guessing, try asking and thanking.” The couple shares awkward smiles and bows in appreciation, their tension easing. A light-hearted touch is added with a cat observing them, tilting its head in approval.
**Dialogue**: "Why not ask and express gratitude instead?"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, beneath the moonlight, the townsgirl writes a waka on a hanging scroll, her brush strokes soft yet intentional. Her expression radiates serenity, enlightened by the notion that harmony flourishes from reason and gentle hearts.
**Dialogue**: "Harmony grows from reason and gentle hearts."
**Tanka (translation)**: Love is like fire, reason is the light; when we live together, gratitude and requests ignite our spirits.
**Tanka (original)**: 恋は火よ  
理(ことわり)は灯(ともしび)  
共に住めば  
感謝と依頼  
機嫌を灯す
