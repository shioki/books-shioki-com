### Panel 2

The woman is depicted as a serene, middle-aged figure with gentle eyes and tied-back hair, exuding a calm presence in a simple kimono. The portrait within the book seems almost alive, whispering about strength found in acceptance rather than resistance.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next day, a sudden gust of wind knocks over laundry lines in the alley. The townsgirl rushes to assist an elderly neighbor in gathering the fallen clothes, both laughing as the wind tousles their hair. She realizes that simply being present and sharing the moment is a form of understanding that requires no words. The composition is dynamic, with fluttering cloth and expressive brushstrokes suggesting movement and renewal.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, under the moonlight, the townsgirl writes a waka on a hanging scroll beside her candle. The ink glows softly against the paper, and her expression is calm and resolute as she composes her poem.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: Standing in the wind, I embrace a heart that does not fall, even if we pass by each other, I know the warmth of the shadows that draw near.  
**Tanka (original)**: 風に立ち  
倒れぬ心　受けとめて  
すれちがうとも
