### Panel 1
**Scene**: In an Edo street at dusk, the townsgirl kneels by a news board where couriers are posting official notices. She observes hurried scribes competing to copy the notices first, her curious eyes reflecting both wonder and unease about the concept of “speed over truth.” The wooden buildings and lanterns around her glow with a quiet tension.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit study filled with scrolls and Dutch books, the townsgirl consults her mentor, a calm and thoughtful middle-aged man resembling the author. He has an intelligent gaze, neat hair tied back, and a refined yet firm demeanor. He gestures toward an open scroll titled “Justice in Reporting,” explaining the dangers of haste in acquiring knowledge. Ink diagrams illustrate the flow of information between power and truth.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl applies her learning by writing a public notice carefully, while others rush to post theirs first. Her deliberate brushstrokes stand in stark contrast to the blurred motion of the others. A gust of wind scatters papers labeled “速報,” symbolizing fleeting and unchecked information. Her calm focus hints at a quiet rebellion against the culture of speed.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Night falls, and the townsgirl sits by her paper lantern, brush in hand, gazing at the moon reflected in her inkstone. In a short vertical poem panel, she writes her waka that captures the essence of the book.  
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: "More than speed, / The path of the brush conveys / The truth within. / Shining in solitude, / The light of the heart."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「  
速さより  
真をつたえる  
筆の道  
孤にて光る  
心のあかり  
」
