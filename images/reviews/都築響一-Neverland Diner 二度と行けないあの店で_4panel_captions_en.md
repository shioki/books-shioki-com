### Panel 1
**Scene**: Evening settles over an Edo street lined with wooden eateries and glowing lanterns. The young townsgirl pauses before a shuttered teahouse, its sign faded and half removed. She gazes at it in quiet surprise, the air filled with drifting smoke from nearby food stalls.  
**Dialogue**: “I used to come here for sweet dumplings with my friend…”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her dimly lit room, the townsgirl reads an imported book titled *Neverland Diner*. From the inked pages, the image of an older man materializes — a serene, thoughtful figure with slightly disheveled hair, round spectacles, and a scholar’s robe. He appears like a spirit mentor emerging from the book, speaking gently about how vanished places live on in memory. Scrolls, Dutch studies texts, and abacuses fill the shelves around her.  
**Dialogue**: “Even when a place disappears, its warmth remains in those who remember.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: In her courtyard, the townsgirl sets up a small makeshift diner, serving tea and sweets to neighbors. She writes their names and laughter in a notebook, saying she wants to preserve these moments. A child looks up and asks what happens if this place disappears too. She smiles softly, surrounded by the lively warmth of the gathering.  
**Dialogue**:  
Child: “But what if this place disappears too?”  
Townsgirl: “Then memory itself will be our true storehouse.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Nighttime. By candlelight, the townsgirl sits quietly, gazing at her closed notebook. She dips her brush and writes a tanka on a hanging paper slip, her expression calm yet wistful. The scene is hushed, with soft paper texture and deep ink shadows.  
**Dialogue**: (none)  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
A vanished shop—  
its light still glows within my heart;  
the warmth of taste  
crosses the flow of time,  
living on beyond the years.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
消えし店　心に灯す　記憶あり  
味のぬくもり　時を越えゆく
