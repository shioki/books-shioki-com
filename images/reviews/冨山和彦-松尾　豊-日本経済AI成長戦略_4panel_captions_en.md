### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a lively Edo marketplace, a young townsgirl in a kimono walks among merchants and craftsmen. She overhears a group discussing the idea that “machines may one day think like people.” Pausing, she looks curiously at an abacus and a strange imported gear mechanism. Wonder and unease mix in her expression as she reflects on how knowledge can transform labor. The background shows wooden stalls, paper lanterns, and bustling townsfolk.  
**Dialogue**: “Machines... that think like people?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her candlelit study, the townsgirl pores over Dutch and Japanese scrolls. On the wall hang two imagined portraits: one of a calm, strategic thinker in scholar’s robes, and another of a gentle, analytical mind with a soft smile. Their serene gazes seem to guide her as she studies a scroll labeled with archaic characters meaning “Artificial Intelligence.”  
**Dialogue**: “What kind of wisdom lies within these foreign studies?”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, she experiments in her small workshop. Using an abacus and a wooden automaton she built herself, she tries to make it “assist” her calculations. The automaton clumsily moves the beads, and she laughs, realizing that true mastery lies not in the machine’s answers but in the questions she asks. Bold ink strokes capture her lively energy and sense of discovery.  
**Dialogue**: “So it’s not the answers... but the questions that matter!”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At night, under the soft glow of a candle, she kneels by a paper window, brush in hand, writing a waka poem on a hanging tanzaku. Her serene face reflects harmony between human insight and mechanical aid.  
**Dialogue**: (none)  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
“The power to question—  
that alone opens the path ahead.  
With wisdom and craft,  
side by side we shall advance.”  
**Tanka (original)**:  
問うことの　力こそ今　道を開く　知恵と機巧（はかり）　共に進まん
