### Panel 1
**Scene**: In the bustling streets of Edo, the townsgirl gazes at a peculiar device brought by a traveling merchant — a wooden box with a lens that claims to “see all.” Her expression is a mix of wonder and unease, sensing its hidden power. The background is alive with merchant stalls and townsfolk engaged in lively conversation.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her candle-lit room, the townsgirl studies Dutch books and scrolls about “artificial eyes” and “calculating machines.” Two imagined mentors appear in her mind: one resembling a calm Western scholar, and the other a sharp-eyed Japanese intellectual in scholar’s robes. They discuss the dual nature of knowledge as a tool for freedom or control, with shadows of watchful eyes looming in the background.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl experiments with her own invention made from abacus beads and magnifying lenses, realizing how easily learning tools can turn into instruments of surveillance. While testing it on the street, she accidentally startles a cat, and humor lightens the moment as she laughs, reflecting on the double-edged nature of innovation.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Under the moonlight at night, the townsgirl writes a waka on a hanging scroll, her face a blend of serenity and solemnity. The ink flows with deep emotion as she reflects on knowledge, freedom, and the unseen watchers. The short poem is inscribed on the scroll.  
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: The power to know, for whom does it serve, a mirror indeed. Chains unseen, ringing in the wind.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
知る力　誰のためなる　鏡かな  
目に見ぬ鎖　風の中鳴る
