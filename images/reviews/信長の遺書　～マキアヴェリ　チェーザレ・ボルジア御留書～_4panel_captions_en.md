### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a lively Edo street at dusk, listening to a merchant arguing about power and faith. She pauses, intrigued, holding a small notebook as temple bells faintly ring in the background. She wonders if "belief can rule people better than gold," her expression thoughtful amidst the ink wash shadows that suggest her inner curiosity.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside her small study, illuminated by an oil lamp, she studies a worn Dutch text and a Japanese chronicle about Oda Nobunaga. A portrait of the author Yamamoto Otoya is visible on the shelf, depicted as a calm, scholarly man with soft features and contemplative eyes, surrounded by scrolls. His serene presence seems to guide her as she reads about "Machiavelli's realism," creating an atmosphere of intellectual tension.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning at a riverside marketplace, she applies her newfound ideas by organizing the vendors' stalls based on logic instead of seniority, surprising everyone and bringing order. The townspeople marvel at her efficiency while she smiles mischievously, realizing that "governing" can be an art of reason. The brushwork captures movement and humor, with the river breeze lifting her sleeve.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, she sits by candlelight, writing a waka on a hanging scroll. The atmosphere is quiet and shadowy, with her face reflecting awe and melancholy as she contemplates the loneliness of power and creation. The short poem (tanka) appears vertically beside her brush.  
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: "Is it wisdom to break and rebuild this world? Even at the end of dreams, there are shadows of people."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「壊しては　また築く世の　理知かな  
　夢の果ても　人の影あり」
