### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through a tranquil Edo street at dusk, holding a small lantern while reflecting on life’s pivotal moments after overhearing older merchants discussing “midlife struggles.” Her expression is a mix of curiosity and contemplation, with wooden houses and paper lanterns swaying gently in the background.

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a candle-lit study room, the townsgirl opens a Dutch-bound book with brush-written characters resembling “人生の分かれ道.” A hanging scroll beside her features a portrait of an imagined mentor, inspired by author Egami Go, depicted as a calm, middle-aged scholar with gentle eyes and neatly tied hair. He seems to whisper advice about “letting go of the ‘but’ and flowing like water,” while the townsgirl listens earnestly, her brush poised over her notes.

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning by a riverside, the townsgirl pours water into various vessels — a bowl, a bamboo tube, a gourd — observing how the water changes form. She smiles with realization, reflecting, “柔らかく、けれど強く.” A passerby looks on, puzzled, as she joyfully experiments, her kimono sleeves tucked up and sunlight glinting off the water’s ripples.

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Once again in the evening, the townsgirl kneels by her low writing desk, illuminated by a small oil lamp, as she writes a tanka on a narrow tanzaku strip. Her serene face shows a mature understanding while the candle flickers. 
**Tanka (translation)**: "To live like water, discarding the 'but'; loneliness reflects the heart."
**Tanka (original)**: 「  
のに捨てて　  
水のごとくに　生きる道　  
孤独は鏡　  
心を映す　  
」
