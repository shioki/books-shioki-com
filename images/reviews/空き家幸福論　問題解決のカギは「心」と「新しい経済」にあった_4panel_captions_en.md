### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through an old Edo neighborhood at dusk, captivated by an abandoned machiya with a half-broken shoji and moss-covered roof. She pauses, tilting her head in curiosity, reflecting on how "even a silent house may hold stories untold." The background features narrow streets, paper lanterns, and neighbors gossiping about “wasted houses.”
**Dialogue**: "Even a silent house may hold stories untold."

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study illuminated by candlelight, the townsgirl reads a Dutch-bound book discussing new concepts of “value” and “connection.” Beside her, a vision of the imagined scholar **Tetsuya Fujiki** appears — a calm, thoughtful middle-aged man in Edo-style scholar robes, with neatly tied hair and gentle eyes, radiating quiet intellect and innovation. He points to a scroll diagram illustrating the connection between “心” (heart) and “経済” (economy) through a circle.
**Dialogue**: "Value and connection are intertwined."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, the townsgirl restores the abandoned house with her own hands, sweeping, repairing tatami, and inviting townsfolk to share tea. A humorous twist occurs when a stray cat moves in first, causing laughter among the neighbors as the house comes alive again. The composition conveys dynamic motion, with ink splashes symbolizing renewal and connection among people.
**Dialogue**: "Look, even the cat loves it here!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Evening sunlight streams through the restored window. The townsgirl sits by the engawa with a brush and paper, crafting a tanka that encapsulates the essence of the book’s message. The poem appears on a vertical strip of paper, written in elegant brushstrokes.
**Tanka (translation)**: "Abandoned house, / when hearts are connected, / the light will shine. / In things without value, / happiness can dwell."
**Tanka (original)**: 「  
捨てし家  
心つなげば  
灯ともる  
値なきものに  
幸は宿れり  
」
