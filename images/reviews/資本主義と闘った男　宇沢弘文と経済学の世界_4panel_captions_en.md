### Panel 1
**Scene**: In an Edo town street at dusk, the townsgirl pauses, overhearing merchants arguing about profit and loss. Her curious eyes reflect both wonder and unease, surrounded by wooden shops, paper lanterns, and a temple bell tower.  
**Dialogue**: She murmurs to herself, contemplating whether “wealth” can truly measure human worth.

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit terakoya, the townsgirl studies a Dutch economics scroll. A scholarly figure resembling the author Sasaki Minoru appears as a mentor spirit—middle-aged, calm, with thoughtful eyes and tied-back hair, dressed in simple robes that suggest intellect and humility. He gestures toward a chalkboard-like surface illustrating “shared capital of society.”  
**Dialogue**: His presence is serene and reflective, embodying ethical wisdom rather than authority.

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl steps into the riverside marketplace, intervening in a dispute between fishmongers by proposing they share the well water and maintain it together for everyone’s benefit. Her lively expression and bold pose contrast with the surprised crowd.  
**Dialogue**: She realizes that theory can guide daily life and fairness.

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Night falls. The townsgirl kneels by a lantern, brush in hand, writing a waka on a hanging scroll. Her calm smile glows in the ink-wash shadows.  
**Dialogue**: The short poem reads on the scroll:  
**Tanka (translation)**: "Do not measure wealth for others; it is the heart that is the true capital for living together."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「人のため  
　富を量るな  
　心こそ  
　共に生きゆく  
　資本なるもの」
