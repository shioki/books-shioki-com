### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a quiet Edo backstreet at dusk, the townsgirl sits outside a sake shop, observing drunken townsmen staggering by, their laughter echoing in the night. She gazes at her untouched sake cup, a faint frown forming as she ponders whether "cheer" can truly be poured from a bottle.

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside her small study illuminated by an oil lamp, the townsgirl reads an imported essay scroll featuring a portrait of a thoughtful middle-aged man, imagined as the author Odajima Takashi. He is depicted with gentle features, short hair tied loosely, round spectacles, and a calm yet piercing gaze. In the portrait, he holds a brush mid-thought, surrounded by lines about illusion, control, and the human heart.
**Dialogue**: “So… the true intoxication lies not in the sake, but in the mind.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, she experiments by pouring clear water into a sake cup. At the teahouse, she joins her friends' lively conversation, listening intently and laughing freely instead of drinking. Her friends are surprised to see her glowing brighter without alcohol, as her laughter flows like wind through the lively room.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, under the moon by the canal, the townsgirl writes a waka on a small tanzaku slip, her brush poised over reflection and self-discovery. The final panel radiates quiet resolve and gentle humor.
