### Panel 1
**Scene**: In the lively streets of Edo, the townsgirl stops next to a noticeboard about a new "regional revitalization" project from the shogunate. She tilts her head, sensing something wrong as merchants murmur about taxes and profits of officials. The scene is vibrant yet tinged with quiet dissatisfaction, surrounded by wooden signboards, lanterns, and bustling townsfolk.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a small study illuminated by light filtering through shoji screens, the townsgirl studies a Dutch text and a scroll on governance. A wise scholar, resembling the author Hiroshi Aiba, appears as her mentor. He gestures towards a diagram labeled "Power and Ethics," his expression a mix of sternness and compassion.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: By the riverside, the townsgirl showcases her new understanding. She draws a chart in the sand illustrating how money flows from villages to the capital. Townspeople gather, amazed, as she explains with humor and clarity. A fishmonger laughs, remarking on the revelation about their silver.  
**Dialogue**: Fishmonger: “So that’s where our silver goes!”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As night descends, the townsgirl sits by the light of a lamp, brush in hand, composing a waka on a hanging scroll. Her eyes reflect a mix of sorrow and determination.  
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: In the land of power, flowers do not bloom; it is the voice questioning the truth that sways in the wind.  
**Tanka (original)**: 権の蔓　  
根を張る里に　  
花は咲かず　  
真を問う声ぞ　  
風に揺らめく
