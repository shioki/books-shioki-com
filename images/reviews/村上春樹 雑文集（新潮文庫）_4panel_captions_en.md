### Panel 1
**Scene**: At dusk, the townsgirl walks through a quiet Edo alley, carrying her abacus and notebook. She pauses by a lantern shop where people are debating “truth” and “judgment.” Lantern light flickers on her thoughtful face as she wonders how one might see truth without rushing to decide.  
**Dialogue**: (Thinking) “To see clearly… perhaps one must wait before judging.”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study lined with Dutch and Japanese books, she reads an imported essay. From the ink mist, a calm apparition of a middle-aged man appears — gentle-eyed, serene, and quietly wise. He tells her that a writer observes much but judges little. She listens, brush poised in midair.  
**Dialogue**: Apparition: “A writer observes much, judges little.”  
Townsgirl: “To observe… without deciding…”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, she strolls through the lively marketplace — merchants bargaining, children laughing, a cat stealing a fish. She simply watches and takes notes, refraining from judgment. When a gust of wind scatters her papers, she laughs, realizing the beauty of not knowing. Her hair and sleeves flow with the wind’s motion.  
**Dialogue**: (Laughing) “So even the wind teaches me!”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, by candlelight, she writes a waka on a slender tanzaku strip. The room is silent except for the sound of her brush. Her expression is serene — enlightened yet humble.  
**Dialogue**: (Softly) “To see, to wait… that is enough.”  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
When I simply watch and wait,  
answers melt into the wind.  
More than judgment,  
it is the gaze itself  
that lights the world.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
観（み）て待てば  
答えは風に  
溶けてゆく  
判断よりも  
灯すまなざし
