### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through a serene Edo alley at dusk, observing a poor vendor sharing a rice ball with a stray cat. A hint of curiosity flickers in her eyes, pondering why kindness seems so insignificant in a vast, divided world. The townscape conveys a gentle loneliness, with swaying paper lanterns and a heavy yet tender atmosphere.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her cozy study, the townsgirl reads a Dutch book on human society by candlelight. An imagined portrait of a middle-aged woman scholar appears on the page — calm, intelligent, with short hair and warm, thoughtful eyes, embodying the spirit of Brady Mikako. The figure seems to softly encourage the girl to seek “the jewels within ordinary people.”  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The following morning, as townsfolk argue at the well over water rights, the townsgirl steps in, offering tea to both sides with a gentle smile. The tension eases, leading to a moment of shared laughter, a small act of “A cup of kindness.” The scene is depicted with bold brushstrokes, light breaking through the ink shadows.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, beneath a paper lantern, the townsgirl writes a waka on a hanging strip of paper, her brush moving with quiet determination.  
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: When offering tea to unseen people, it becomes a jewel, rolling hearts connect, and the world unites.  
**Tanka (original)**: 「見ぬ人に  
　茶をささぐれば  
　珠となり  
　転がるこころ  
　つながる世界」
