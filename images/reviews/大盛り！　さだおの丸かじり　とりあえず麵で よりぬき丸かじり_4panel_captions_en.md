### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a bustling Edo street filled with food stalls, a young townsgirl stands captivated by a noodle stand, watching the steam rise. Her expression shows a mix of hunger and philosophical curiosity as she wonders why noodles attract people so irresistibly. The background features lanterns and the warm glow of evening, accompanied by the sounds of merchants chatting.
**Dialogue**: "Why are people so drawn to noodles?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a serene study adorned with scrolls and Dutch literature, the townsgirl opens a newly arrived essay titled “丸かじり.” From the pages, the spirit of an elderly man appears, resembling the author Shoji Sadao, with thin glasses and soft white hair. He holds a ladle and explains how noodles reflect both human folly and sincerity. The townsgirl listens intently, her brush hovering in the air.
**Dialogue**: "Noodles reveal our folly and sincerity."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, the townsgirl sets up her own noodle stall by the riverbank. She attempts to cook using a water clock for precise timing but ends up laughing as the noodles overboil. An elderly customer praises her for the "human flavor" of her noodles, leading her to realize that intuition and warmth are more important than perfection. The steam around her swirls like calligraphy.
**Dialogue**: "Your noodles have a wonderful human flavor!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As night falls, the townsgirl sits beside her extinguished pot, writing a tanka on a hanging scroll. The steam dissipates into the starry sky as she smiles gently, her brush trembling with emotion.
**Tanka (translation)**: In the steam, reason melts away, and the chopsticks move; the flavor of humanity seeps into smiles.
**Tanka (original)**: 湯気のなか　理性もとけて　箸すすむ  
人のあじわい　笑みにしみゆく
