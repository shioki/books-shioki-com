### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a lively Edo marketplace filled with paper stalls and print sellers. She pauses at a shop showcasing hand-drawn picture scrolls, pondering, “Can art truly capture life’s movement?” Her eyes sparkle with curiosity as smoke from a brazier curls in the air like ink lines, reflecting the theme of “capturing motion in stillness.”
**Dialogue**: “Can art truly capture life’s movement?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a dimly lit study, the townsgirl studies scrolls and Dutch diagrams. Above her desk hangs a scroll depicting a thoughtful man—imagined as author Yoshimori Daisuke, with calm eyes and tied-back hair. His gaze seems to guide her as she learns brushwork and composition, whispering the lesson: “Draw first, think after.”
**Dialogue**: “Draw first, think after.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Kneeling in the courtyard with a brush in hand, she sketches sparrows as they scatter. Her strokes are wild yet full of life. A neighbor looks over, confused by her “messy” lines, but the birds in her drawing appear to flutter off the page—she laughs, realizing that creation arises from imperfection and persistence.
**Dialogue**: [None]

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Evening light streams through paper screens. The townsgirl sits at her low desk, writing a waka on a small tanzaku slip, embodying the book’s essence. Her expression is calm yet determined as she writes.
**Dialogue**: [None]
**Tanka (translation)**: To draw is to hold the brush without fear of not selling; it embodies movement, just as my heart desires.
**Tanka (original)**: 「描くとは  
　売れぬを恐れず  
　筆を執る  
　動きを宿す
