### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a bustling Edo town street, a curious townsgirl observes two merchants arguing about their late father's shop inheritance. She tilts her head in thought, holding an abacus and a bamboo scroll, while a sign in the background reads “Consulting about inheritance.” The atmosphere is tense yet humorous, with one merchant clutching a ledger and the other waving a fan in protest.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her candle-lit study, the townsgirl opens a Dutch-bound book titled “The Logic of Inheritance.” An illustration appears on the parchment of a calm, scholarly man with gentle eyes and short tied hair, embodying a wise teacher named “Keita Tachibana.” He gestures as if explaining concepts clearly, his expression kind yet firm, radiating expertise and fairness.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl gathers her neighbors for a mock family meeting in the courtyard of a longhouse. She uses pebbles on the ground to demonstrate how assets can be divided flexibly if everyone agrees. The onlookers laugh as she jokes about the “arithmetic of peace.” A cat playfully bats at one of the pebbles, adding humor and movement to the scene.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As night falls, the townsgirl kneels by a paper lantern with a brush in hand, gazing softly at the moonlight filtering through shōji. On a tanzaku, she writes a waka that encapsulates the essence of the book — that harmony, not numbers, preserves wealth across generations.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: The bonds of home,  
Deeper than mere numbers,  
Must be preserved well.  
The heart's scale,  
Lightly layered together.  
**Tanka (original)**: 家の絆  
数より深く  
守るべし  
心の秤  
軽く重ねて
