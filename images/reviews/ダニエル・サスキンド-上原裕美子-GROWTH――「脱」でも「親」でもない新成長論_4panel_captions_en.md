### Panel 1
**Scene**: At dusk in a lively Edo street, the young townsgirl walks among merchants and lanterns, carrying an abacus and a Dutch book. She overhears traders arguing over profits and quietly wonders if more silver truly means more happiness. The glow of lanterns reflects in her thoughtful eyes.  
**Dialogue**: “Is more silver always better…?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her candle-lit study, she reads a foreign book intently. From its pages emerge two ethereal mentors — a calm Western scholar with wavy hair and a composed Japanese woman with tied hair. They seem to discuss the balance between prosperity and morality, their ghostly light illuminating her face.  
**Dialogue**: (The mentors’ gentle voices echo) “True wealth lies in harmony, not excess.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: In the town square, she teaches neighbors using a chalkboard, drawing circles labeled “wealth,” “harmony,” and “knowledge.” Children and artisans watch curiously. As her abacus beads spill and scatter like seeds, she laughs softly, calling it a good omen.  
**Dialogue**: “Look — even the abacus agrees! Growth can take many forms.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: By the moonlit riverbank, she writes a waka poem on a slender tanzaku. Her calm expression reflects quiet conviction — a wish to seek balance between progress and purpose.  
**Dialogue**: (softly) “May this light guide our path beyond measure.”  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
When we question growth,  
a new path opens before us —  
beyond mere measure,  
let us kindle the small flame  
of quality over quantity.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
「  
成長を  
　問えばひらける  
　道の果て  
量より質の  
　灯をともさん  
」
