### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a quiet Edo town street at dusk, the townsgirl observes an old constable returning to patrol after retirement. She tilts her head, pondering the meaning of “serving again after one’s time.” The background features old wooden houses and a softly glowing police patrol lantern. Her expression is one of curiosity and contemplation.
**Dialogue**: "What does it mean to serve again after one's time?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a small temple school, the townsgirl studies a foreign-bound scroll about ethics and duty. A scholarly elder appears beside her, embodying a calm and reflective presence. He is depicted as a middle-aged man with kind eyes and tidy hair tied back, dressed in simple robes. He explains how “aging can be a second spring.” Books and abacuses are scattered across the tatami.
**Dialogue**: "Aging can be a second spring."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl stands in the marketplace, mediating a quarrel between two merchants. She applies her learned skills, listening earnestly and choosing fairness over strict rules. Her gesture mirrors that of the old constable from the first panel. The scene is dynamic, with wind swirling paper slips around her, symbolizing renewal and human warmth beyond bureaucracy.
**Dialogue**: "Let's find a fair solution together."

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Night falls. The townsgirl kneels at her writing desk by candlelight, brush poised. She smiles gently as she writes a waka on a small tanzaku, capturing the essence of the book — that age and experience can bloom again as “old spring.” The candle’s flame flickers softly.
**Dialogue**: "Old spring blooms anew."
**Tanka (translation)**: Though aging, I rise again, spring haze glimmers in the wind, not just in numbers, but in the heart of people.
**Tanka (original)**: 老いながら  
再び立ちぬ　春霞  
数にあらねど  
人の志（こころ）  
風に光りぬ
