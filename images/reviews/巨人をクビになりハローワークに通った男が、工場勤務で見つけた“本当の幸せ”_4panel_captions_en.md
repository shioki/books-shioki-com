### Panel 1
**Scene**: The young Edo-period townsgirl walks through a lively riverside street, where strong men unload goods beside a warehouse. Lanterns sway above, and an old sign reads “Employment Consultation Office.” She gazes thoughtfully at the scene, wondering why some people lose their place in life even after achieving success.  
**Dialogue**: “Why do some lose their footing even after they’ve done so well…?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dim temple library, the townsgirl studies a Dutch book and a scroll about work ethics. From the pages, two ghostly mentors appear — one a calm, broad-shouldered craftsman with kind eyes, the other a scholarly man with a keen, gentle gaze. They share their wisdom about finding true happiness through being needed by others.  
**Dialogue**: “True happiness is found when your efforts are needed by someone else.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: In the bustling marketplace, the townsgirl helps a neighbor repair a broken abacus. Applying what she has learned, she smiles as she fixes it with cleverness rather than strength. The surrounding townsfolk cheer, celebrating the quiet pride and connection born from small acts of help.  
**Dialogue**: “There—see? It works again! Sometimes, a gentle touch solves more than force.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dusk beneath a willow tree, the townsgirl kneels with brush and paper. Her expression is serene as she writes a waka poem on a tanzaku strip, the ink softly bleeding into the paper.  
**Dialogue**: (none)  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
As places change,  
I leave my heart behind—  
and through each smile,  
I come to understand  
what true happiness means.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
変わりゆく  
場所に心を  
置きながら  
笑顔こそ知る  
真のしあわせ
