### Panel 1
**Scene**: In an Edo street at dawn, the townsgirl observes a group of palanquin bearers waiting for customers. One of them expresses frustration about the “dispatch tablet” from the magistrate that decides who will work today. The girl tilts her head, sensing something peculiar about this invisible “selector” that dictates labor. The background features wooden houses shrouded in early morning mist.
**Dialogue**: "Why does the dispatch tablet choose for us?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit terakoya, the townsgirl studies a Dutch manuscript alongside two imagined mentors inspired by the authors — one, Alex Rosenblat, appears as a composed Western scholar with short hair and round spectacles, while the other, Takako Iijima, is depicted as a dignified Japanese woman with a calm gaze and tied-back hair. Both mentors are illustrated as figures in the scroll, pointing to diagrams of gears and flow lines that symbolize the “algorithms” controlling unseen workers.
**Dialogue**: "These diagrams show how systems control labor."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl, curious and bold, constructs her own “dispatch box” using an abacus and wooden cards, allowing her friends to draw work orders. However, they soon argue as the device favors some over others. Shocked, she realizes her invention has turned into a tiny “automated boss.” Ink splashes illustrate her inner turmoil as she stares at the abacus, which glows ominously.
**Dialogue**: "This isn't what I intended..."

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That evening, under a paper lantern, she writes a waka in her notebook, gazing at the sky where invisible threads seem to connect all workers. The composition is serene yet thought-provoking. The short poem appears vertically on a hanging tanzaku:
**Tanka (translation)**: "Though I work under unseen hands, I labor in the name of freedom, like the wind, with no master."
**Tanka (original)**: 「見えぬ手に  
　操られつつ　働けど  
　自由の名のみ  
　風のごとくに  
　形なき主（あるじ）」
