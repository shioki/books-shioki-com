### Panel 1
**Scene**: On a bustling Edo street, a young townsgirl in a kimono walks home, holding a wasan book and a Dutch studies volume. She suddenly stumbles due to a knee ache, glancing between her leg and a broken karakuri puppet nearby, contemplating the nature of bodies and machines. 
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a modest terakoya study filled with diagrams, an abacus, and various books, the townsgirl eagerly compares an illustration of human anatomy with the puppet's gears. A middle-aged imagined version of author Suzuki Nao observes her, demonstrating the importance of caring for worn gears rather than scolding them.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: In a humorous low-angle view, the townsgirl imagines piloting a giant translucent karakuri body through Edo. Instead of pushing it forward, she performs a “maintenance sequence,” enjoying yudofu, taking a nap, and walking with a friend, showcasing cooperation with limitations.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dawn by a river, the townsgirl sits with her repaired karakuri puppet and books, accepting the coexistence of loss and joy. She writes a tanka on a narrow strip of paper, with the silhouette of her imagined giant karakuri body walking beside her.
**Tanka (translation)**: This body,  
breaking little by little,  
I care for it,  
eating and resting,  
heading toward tomorrow again.  
**Tanka (original)**: この身体  
壊れつつゆく  
いたわりて  
食いて眠りて  
明日へまた行く
