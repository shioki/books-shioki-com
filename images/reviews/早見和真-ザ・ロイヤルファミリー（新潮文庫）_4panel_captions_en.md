### Panel 1
**Scene**: On a lively Edo street lined with teahouses and bookmakers, the young townsgirl pauses before a crowd cheering at a painted horse race screen. She looks puzzled, her ribbon fluttering in the wind as she wonders why people are so passionate about horses they do not own. The air is filled with the bustle of commoners placing bets.  
**Dialogue**: “Why do they cheer so fervently for horses that aren’t theirs…?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a dim study cluttered with scrolls and imported Dutch books, the townsgirl meets a calm, scholarly man resembling Hayami Kazuma. With neatly tied hair and composed eyes, he gestures toward a Dutch text, the candlelight casting deep ink shadows around them. The atmosphere is quiet and thoughtful.  
**Dialogue**: “True investment lies not in horses, but in people.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At dawn by the riverbank, the townsgirl releases a small wooden horse she carved into the flowing water, whispering her resolve to trust in others. The toy horse drifts away under the rising sun. Nearby, townsfolk work together to repair a bridge — a scene of shared hope and renewal, rendered in expressive brushstrokes of rippling light.  
**Dialogue**: “I will trust in the hearts of others.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Kneeling at her writing desk, the townsgirl lifts her brush above a tanzaku slip. Candlelight flickers as she writes a waka about trust, inheritance, and hope. Her serene expression reflects quiet conviction as the poem appears vertically on the paper.  
**Dialogue**: (none)  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
“Trusting in people,  
Feelings surpass the bloodline—  
Every ending births  
A beginning once again,  
A prayer carried onward.”  
**Tanka (original)**:  
人を信じ  
血を越えゆく  
想いあり  
終わりは始め  
祈りつなげり
