### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through the lively streets of Edo at dusk, observing the bustling crowd and merchants. She stops by the Sumida River, looking at her reflection and reflecting on how “time in the city never stops, but people do.” The background features lanterns, wooden bridges, and the soft glow of evening, capturing the essence of a vibrant city.
**Dialogue**: "Time in the city never stops, but people do."

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a dimly lit study, the townsgirl studies a mysterious imported book titled “四次元紀行.” Inside, a portrait of an elderly scholar resembling the author catches her attention—an older man with short, thinning white hair, glasses, and a calm demeanor, dressed in a neat haori. He seems to gaze back at her knowingly, as if whispering, “Even solitude has its geometry.” Ink bottles, Dutch scrolls, and abacuses are scattered around her.
**Dialogue**: "Even solitude has its geometry."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, the townsgirl steps into a crowded Edo street, using her fan to draw geometric shapes in the air to illustrate “the invisible paths of human hearts.” Bystanders watch her with a mix of confusion and amusement as she likens the city's vibrant flow to intersecting dimensions of time and loneliness. In a humorous twist, her chalk drawing accidentally obscures a shop sign, surprising the owner.
**Dialogue**: "These are the invisible paths of human hearts!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As night descends, the townsgirl sits beside the soft light of a paper lantern, brush in hand, composing a waka on a small hanging tanzaku. Her expression is calm and contemplative. The city sleeps quietly beyond the shoji.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]
**Tanka (translation)**: In the river of time, I sway with the flow, knowing solitude.  
Voiceless thoughts become light.
**Tanka (original)**: 時の川　流れに身ゆれ　孤を知る  
声なき想い　光となりて
