### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through the bustling Edo market at dusk, carrying a small abacus and notebook. She overhears merchants arguing over profits and prices, their words echoing like mechanical clinks of coins. She pauses, puzzled by the scene around her.
**Dialogue**: “If all chase silver, who tends the heart?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her candle-lit study within a narrow longhouse, the townsgirl is engrossed in Dutch and Japanese texts. A hanging scroll behind her features a portrait of a calm, scholarly man, imagined as Kohei Saito, with neat short hair and gentle yet intense eyes behind round spectacles.
**Dialogue**: She reads his words about “the automatic movement of value,” her expression reflecting awe and quiet rebellion.

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Dawn light spills into the street as she sets up a small “common well” for neighbors, offering free water and books. Children and elders gather joyfully, while a confused merchant observes, realizing there is no profit here, yet joy abounds.
**Dialogue**: The girl laughs, sleeves rolled up, embodying the idea of “reclaiming the commons.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the calm of evening, she kneels by the well with a brush in hand, composing a waka on a narrow strip of paper. The well’s reflection shimmers softly in the twilight.
**Dialogue**: The short poem reads:
**Tanka (translation)**: "In a world bound by the threads of profit, to live together is what we call wealth."
**Tanka (original)**: 「  
利の糸に  
縛られし世を  
解き放ち  
共に生くるを  
富と呼びけり  
」
