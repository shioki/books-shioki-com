### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a lively Edo street at twilight, the townsgirl stands before a bulletin board where townspeople are engaged in a heated discussion. She tilts her head in curiosity, pondering why the loudest voices always seem to prevail. Lanterns and a soba stall can be seen in the background, along with murmuring townsfolk. Her expression conveys a mix of curiosity and unease.
**Dialogue**: “Why do the loudest voices always win?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside her modest study, the townsgirl is deeply focused on scrolls and Dutch books. A portrait of a scholarly man resembling the author Uno Shigeki hangs on the wall. He appears as a calm, middle-aged Edo scholar with thoughtful eyes and neatly tied short hair, dressed in plain robes, radiating a gentle yet intellectual presence. The portrait seems to convey a message about the ethics of democracy, emphasizing that true governance arises from shared responsibility rather than mere numbers. The townsgirl listens intently, holding a brush.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At dawn, the townsgirl gathers her neighbors around a communal well. Instead of allowing arguments to arise, she encourages everyone to express their thoughts, mediating their voices with quiet confidence. The composition captures their faces illuminated by the morning light as she smiles, symbolizing “participation and responsibility.” A humorous touch is added with a cat mimicking the act of raising its paw as if voting.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That evening, under a paper lantern, the townsgirl writes a waka on a tanzaku, her eyes calm yet bright. The short poem reads:  
「声あげて　数に埋もるな　人の意を  
　きく耳こそが　世をつくる礎」  
She places the tanzaku beside her inkstone, gazing out at the tranquil town, reflecting on democracy as a way of living harmoniously together.
**Tanka (translation)**: "Raise your voice, do not be buried by numbers; it is the listening ear that lays the foundation for the world."
**Tanka (original)**: 「声あげて　数に埋もるな　人の意を  
　きく耳こそが　世をつくる礎」
