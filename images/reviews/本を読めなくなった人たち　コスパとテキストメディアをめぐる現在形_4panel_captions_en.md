### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a busy Edo street at dusk, observing young students who are only reading brief flyers and gossip scrolls instead of books. She tilts her head in thought, holding a worn paper book to her chest, pondering why people have stopped reading deeply. The background features wooden signboards and a paper lantern that reads "速読指南所" (quick reading school).
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a candle-lit study, the townsgirl is deeply engrossed in a Dutch science book and a thick Japanese essay on "the art of reading." A portrait of a scholarly man, imagined as author Inada Toyofumi, hangs on the wall. He appears middle-aged with a calm expression, short hair tied back neatly, dressed in simple robes, exuding a scholarly air with sharp, intelligent eyes. His gaze seems to teach her about the loss of thought in an age of effortless words.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl attempts to copy a passage by hand with her brush, while a friend nearby showcases a wooden contraption that spits out endless slips of printed text, symbolizing AI-generated writings. The slips pile up around them without meaning. She laughs in surprise, then resolutely closes her eyes, holding her handwritten page to her heart as the slips scatter around her.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dawn, the townsgirl sits by the riverbank, holding her brush and notebook. She writes a waka on a small tanzaku, capturing the essence of the book. The wind blows, sending one of the printed slips into the water as she gazes at the sunrise.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: To read is to navigate a boat across time, rowing alone through the sea of contemplation.  
**Tanka (original)**: 読むとは  
　時を超えゆく  
　舟なりて  
　思索の海を  
　ひとり漕ぎゆく
