### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl stands in a lively Edo marketplace, holding a few coins in her palm while observing merchants and craftsmen exchanging goods. She ponders aloud why people seem to pursue money instead of happiness. The background features wooden stalls, hanging lanterns, and townsfolk bargaining.
**Dialogue**: "Why do they chase after money instead of happiness?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a serene terakoya (temple school), the townsgirl is engrossed in a Dutch economics book. An imagined mentor appears beside her — a scholarly man with calm eyes and a gentle smile, dressed in simple robes, embodying the author Manabu Tanouchi. He gestures towards a diagram illustrating connections through cooperation, explaining that "money is but a tool between hearts."
**Dialogue**: "Money is merely a tool that connects our hearts."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl assists a neighbor who is struggling to buy rice, offering her help and organizing a small local exchange of goods and labor. The panel captures a humorous moment as townsfolk join in, creating a joyful chain of barter and gratitude, realizing that “economy” is truly about mutual support.
**Dialogue**: "Let’s help each other out!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: The townsgirl kneels by the light of a lamp, brush in hand, writing a waka poem on a tanzaku strip. Her expression is calm and inspired as she gazes at the coins next to the candle flame, symbolizing “money beyond money.”
**Tanka (translation)**: "Beyond money, the warmth of people can be seen; helping each other is the true wealth of the world."
**Tanka (original)**: 「銭の先　人のぬくもり　見えにけり  
　助け合ふこそ　世の富なりけり」
