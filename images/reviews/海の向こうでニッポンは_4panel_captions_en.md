### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through an Edo port town, observing foreign sailors admiring Japanese fans and porcelain. She looks puzzled, murmuring to herself about how “Nippon” appears so different when seen from the sea. The background features ships with foreign flags, crates marked with “長崎,” and curious townsfolk watching her with intrigue.
**Dialogue**: "Nippon looks so different from the sea..."

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study room, the townsgirl reads a Dutch book and an illustrated scroll titled "Beyond the Sea, Nippon." Beside her is the imagined scholar, inspired by author Inoue Shoichi — an older intellectual with a calm yet keen expression, wearing scholarly robes and small round spectacles. He gestures toward a map showing arrows looping between Japan and foreign lands, explaining the exchange of culture.
**Dialogue**: "Culture travels back and forth, changing shape each time."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl hosts a small gathering by the riverside, presenting her version of “foreign-style sushi” — round, colorful, and slightly odd. The onlookers laugh with delight and confusion, while she beams with pride, declaring that “misunderstandings can be beautiful.” The scene is lively, with bold ink strokes capturing the motion of laughter and waves.
**Dialogue**: "Misunderstandings can be beautiful!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In a night scene, the townsgirl sits by a paper lantern, gazing at the sea beyond the town, brush in hand. In a soft, reflective atmosphere, she writes a waka on a hanging tanzaku, conveying the beauty of cultural circulation and creative misunderstanding.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]
