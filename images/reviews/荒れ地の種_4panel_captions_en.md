### Panel 1
**Scene**: In an Edo town street after the evening rain, the townsgirl stands thoughtfully before a broken sake barrel outside a closed sake shop, reflecting on how people and places recover after being "washed away." The background features wooden shop signs, puddles that reflect lantern light, and townsfolk quietly cleaning debris.  
**Dialogue**: "It’s interesting how everything can be washed away, yet still find a way to recover."

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a small study illuminated by candlelight, the townsgirl reads an imported Dutch science book next to a portrait scroll of 江上剛, depicted as a calm, middle-aged scholar with a composed face and serene eyes. She traces the kanji “荒れ地の種” on the page, contemplating the meaning of rebirth through story.  
**Dialogue**: "What does it mean to plant seeds in barren land?"

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl is actively participating in rebuilding a sake brewery, measuring rice with a wooden box, writing formulas on a wooden board, and explaining fermentation as “a living story.” The townsfolk are surprised and amused by her integration of learning into their work. The wind carries the scent of new beginnings through the open doors.  
**Dialogue**: "Fermentation is like a story that breathes life into our work!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dusk, she sits by the riverbank with a brush in hand, observing a small sprout growing from cracked soil beside her. She writes a waka poem on a tanzaku, encapsulating the essence of the book — that story and compassion can revive the land and its people.  
**Dialogue**: "With every word, we nurture the seeds of hope."  
**Tanka (translation)**: "Even in barren land, the seeds of the heart sprout, voices continue to tell stories, becoming the wind."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「荒れ地にも  
　こころの種は  
　芽吹きけり  
　語りつぐ声  
　風となりゆく」
