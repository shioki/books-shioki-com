### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through a lively Edo street, holding a bundle of papers and an abacus. She overhears merchants arguing about who works harder and tilts her head in curiosity, pondering the meaning of work. The background is filled with bustling shops and craftsmen, creating a warm and noisy atmosphere.
**Dialogue**: "What does it mean to work?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her dimly lit study, the townsgirl reads a thick leather-bound book titled “Company Days” in kanji. A wise-looking scholar, inspired by Yamamoto Yukihisa, appears beside her, pointing at the text. He has gentle eyes, slightly messy hair, and wears simple robes, embodying a calm demeanor.
**Dialogue**: "Even imperfect masters help their apprentices grow."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning at a local workshop, the townsgirl applies her knowledge as she mediates a quarrel between two apprentices over a failed project. She encourages them to appreciate the process rather than the outcome. A spilled pot of ink adds humor to the situation, and everyone laughs, showcasing the warmth of human connection.
**Dialogue**: "Remember to value the process, not just the result."

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At sunset by the riverbank, the townsgirl writes a waka on a small scroll. The wind gently ripples the water as she reflects on the day's lessons, capturing the essence of her experiences in her poem.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]
