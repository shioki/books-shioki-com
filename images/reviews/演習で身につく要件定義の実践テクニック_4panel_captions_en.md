### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a bustling Edo street, listening to merchants and officials arguing over conflicting plans for a new town registry system. She tilts her head thoughtfully, sensing that the confusion stems not from the scrolls or tools, but from unclear intentions. The background features wooden signboards, merchants with abacuses, and a temple bell in the distance.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a candle-lit study, the townsgirl pores over Dutch and Japanese books about logic and governance. A hanging scroll depicts a calm, intellectual man resembling the author Tetsurou Mizuta, portrayed with a measured, thoughtful expression, neat hair tied back, slender build, and scholar’s robes. His spirit seems to guide her with invisible ink diagrams showing “logic,” “visualization,” and “consensus” as three flowing pillars.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl gathers townsfolk around a low table and uses a brush and paper to draw a branching “logic tree” of their problems—linking “tools,” “tasks,” and “management” in three layers. The crowd’s expressions shift from confusion to clarity, and even the neighborhood cat seems to nod wisely at her diagram.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the evening, the townsgirl kneels by lamplight, composing a waka on a thin strip of paper, her face serene and reflective. The paper glows softly with ink as she writes.
**Dialogue**: None
**Tanka (translation)**: Through the structure, connecting people and aspirations, weaving words to envision the future.
**Tanka (original)**:  
仕組みにて  
人と志  
つなぐ道  
言の葉まとい  
未来を描く
