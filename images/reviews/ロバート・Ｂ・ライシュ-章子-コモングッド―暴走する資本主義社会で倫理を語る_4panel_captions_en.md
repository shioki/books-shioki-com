### Panel 1
**Scene**: On a lively Edo street at dusk, a young townsgirl in kimono pauses as a merchant argues over profits. Her gaze sharpens with quiet doubt, wondering if wealth alone defines a person’s worth. Wooden storefronts glow under paper lanterns, and townsfolk trade coins — a scene of prosperity tinged with unease.  
**Dialogue**: “Is gain alone the measure of worth?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a candlelit study, the townsgirl pores over Dutch and Chinese texts. On the wall hangs a portrait of three imagined scholars — a gentle Western elder with round spectacles, a calm Japanese man in scholar’s robes, and a serene woman with a quill in hand. Their painted faces seem to speak softly of “the common good” and “ethics beyond profit.”  
**Dialogue**: (none)

### Panel 3
**Scene**: In a temple courtyard, the townsgirl teaches children to share ink and paper. One mischievous child hides his brush, but she smiles kindly, showing that learning is a treasure meant to be shared. Plum petals drift through the air, symbolizing truth and education as gifts for all. Bold brushstrokes convey warmth and motion.  
**Dialogue**: “Knowledge grows brighter when shared.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At night, she kneels by the window, moonlight spilling across her desk. With calm resolve, she dips her brush and writes a waka poem on a slender tanzaku, her face serene yet determined.  
**Dialogue**: (none)  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
True virtue becomes a path when guarded by all;  
Deeper than profit — the light of living together.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
真（まこと）とは　みなで守りて　道となる  
利より深し　共に生くる灯（ひ）
