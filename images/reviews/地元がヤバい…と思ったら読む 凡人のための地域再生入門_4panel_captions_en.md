### Panel 1
**Scene**: The young townsgirl walks through a quiet Edo street, surrounded by closed shops and an abandoned market square. She looks around with a puzzled expression as neighbors sigh, lamenting the lack of customers. Her face reflects both concern and curiosity about how to revive her town, hinting at the need for change.
**Dialogue**: “No customers these days…”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a small temple library, the townsgirl is engrossed in studying a Dutch book alongside a scroll about local trade. A portrait of a thoughtful man, imagined as Kinoshita Hitoshi, hangs nearby, exuding a scholarly vibe. His calm demeanor and simple robes suggest he is a reformist merchant or teacher, serving as an unseen mentor to her. The girl’s eyes widen as she reads about “ordinary people starting small.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At the riverside market, the townsgirl sets up a modest tea stall with her own savings. She serves cheerful customers, transforming an old, unused corner into a vibrant gathering place. Some townsfolk watch with skepticism, while others come to lend a hand. The dynamic composition, marked by bold ink strokes, captures the lively spirit of “starting before perfect.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As night falls, the lantern at the stall glows softly. The townsgirl kneels by the river, writing a waka on a tanzaku, reflecting on her day. The scene is quiet and emotional, with gentle ripples on the water. Her poem is clearly visible in vertical Japanese text:  
「  
補助金に　頼らず始め　手を汚し  
人の輪生むや　町の灯戻る  
」  
**Tanka (translation)**: "Starting without relying on subsidies, getting our hands dirty, does it create a circle of people? The town's light returns."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「補助金に　頼らず始め　手を汚し　人の輪生むや　町の灯戻る」
