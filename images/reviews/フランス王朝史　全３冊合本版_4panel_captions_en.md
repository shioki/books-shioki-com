### Panel 1
**Scene**: The young Edo townsgirl walks through a lively street market, where merchants argue over which lord’s coinage carries greater “authority.” She tilts her head thoughtfully, wondering what truly makes power legitimate. The bustling stalls and signboards capture the vibrant rhythm of Edo commerce.  
**Dialogue**: (Thinking) “What gives a coin—or a lord—its weight of truth?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her candlelit study, she reads a thick scroll titled “History of the French Dynasties.” From the page, a spectral scholar appears—calm, bespectacled, his hair tied back. He gestures toward illustrations of medieval kings, his voice like a whisper of ink and memory. Shelves of Dutch and Chinese books line the walls.  
**Dialogue**: Scholar: “Continuity is mightier than genius.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At a temple school, the townsgirl teaches children how to keep ledgers. When one asks who the “lord” of the numbers is, she smiles and shows how each page builds upon the last—turning arithmetic into a lesson on governance. The children gasp as the abacus before them shimmers into a tiny castle made of counting beads.  
**Dialogue**: Townsgirl: “See? Each number rules only by the order that came before.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Night falls. By lamplight, she sits with brush and ink, gazing at the moon reflected in her inkstone. On a hanging slip of paper, she writes a tanka that distills the day’s thoughts into verse.  
**Dialogue**: (Softly) “Even the ordinary, layered with time, becomes a nation…”  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
Even the commonplace,  
layered through time, becomes a realm—  
Threads of bloodline weave  
the fabric of institutions,  
binding generations into form.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
凡庸にも　時を積みてぞ　国となる  
血脈の糸は　制度を織りぬ
