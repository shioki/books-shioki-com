### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through an Edo street at dusk, holding a foreign-bound book she borrowed from a rangaku scholar. She pauses beneath a lantern, gazing at the sky, pondering how a single person could change the fate of a nation. The background features paper lanterns, wooden houses, and a nearby temple school where students study by candlelight.
**Dialogue**: "How can one person change the fate of a nation?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside her small room, she unrolls a scroll depicting a Western soldier—her imagined portrait of the author Sato Kenichi as an Edo-style scholar: middle-aged, with stern eyes and tied-back hair, exuding a calm yet intense presence. She studies his ideas as if he were her unseen teacher, surrounded by Dutch texts and arithmetic diagrams. His ink-drawn image seems to “speak” about knowledge as the weapon that transcends birth and circumstance.
**Dialogue**: "His ideas guide me like a hidden teacher."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl stands in the rain-swept street, using a bamboo stick to sketch geometric lines in the puddles, explaining to curious children how “reason and resolve” can shape destiny. Her expression is bright and confident, embodying the transformation of learning into action. The composition is dynamic, with raindrops and ink strokes blending together.
**Dialogue**: "With reason and resolve, we can shape our destiny!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At her writing desk, candlelight flickers as she finishes a waka on a vertical tanzaku slip. Her face is serene, reflecting both solitude and strength. The tanzaku bears her handwritten poem in delicate brush calligraphy.
**Tanka (translation)**: Fate is not for lamenting; with pen in hand, I illuminate the darkness, lighting my path.

知の灯（ひ）かざして
