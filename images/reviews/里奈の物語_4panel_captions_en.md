### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a dimly lit Edo backstreet at dusk, observing a struggling mother and child outside a tenement. She pauses, her eyes filled with empathy, reflecting on what it means to “live with dignity.” The background features flickering lantern light against wooden eaves, creating an atmosphere of quiet tension and compassion.
**Dialogue**: "What does it mean to live with dignity?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small room, the townsgirl studies a worn rangaku book alongside a mysterious new scroll labeled "鈴木大介." The scroll depicts a scholarly middle-aged man with calm yet intense eyes, thoughtful brow, and simple robes. His silent presence conveys the essence of “practical ethics of survival.” The girl listens in awe, brush poised over her notes.
**Dialogue**: "This wisdom speaks volumes."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl applies her newfound insight by teaching a destitute widow basic arithmetic to help manage her meager earnings. The scene is lively, with coins scattering as the girl confidently explains calculations on a wooden tablet. The widow’s face lights up with a spark of hope amidst her struggles.
**Dialogue**: "See? It’s simple! Just add these together."

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As night falls, the townsgirl kneels by lamplight, brush in hand, writing a waka on a hanging scroll. Her expression is serene yet determined, embodying the essence of survival, dignity, and the bonds that transcend despair.
**Dialogue**: "This poem captures it all."
**Tanka (translation)**: To live is to reach beyond shame, hand in hand, sharing light in the night of learning.
**Tanka (original)**: 生きるとは　恥を越えつつ　手を取りて  
灯（ひ）を分かち合う　夜の学びや
