### Panel 1
**Scene**: In the lively streets of Edo, the townsgirl takes a moment to pause from her work of copying merchant ledgers, sighing softly as she contemplates the nature of her duties. Cherry blossoms float through the air, and the bustling townsfolk move with purpose, while she gazes at her brush, seeking answers.
**Dialogue**: “Is work merely duty, or could it be life itself?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small room at night, illuminated by a flickering oil lamp, the townsgirl studies a Dutch philosophy book. An imagined portrait of the author, Kondo Kotaro, appears on the page, depicting him as a calm, middle-aged scholar with thoughtful eyes and a faint smile. His ghostly figure seems to impart wisdom.
**Dialogue**: “There are no interesting jobs—only people who make them so.” 

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The following morning, the townsgirl enthusiastically applies her newfound knowledge, transforming her monotonous work into a creative endeavor. She arranges the merchant's accounts like a puzzle, balancing numbers joyfully while humming, inspiring her fellow workers who watch her with amusement.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At twilight, she sits peacefully by the riverbank, brush in hand, composing a waka on a hanging tanzaku. Her expression is serene, illuminated by the soft, fading light of dusk.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]
**Tanka (translation)**: "When I work,  
   play and learning meet,  
   in a heart that toils,  
   flowers bloom in the soil."  
**Tanka (original)**: **「働けば  
　遊びも学びも  
　めぐりあう  
　耕す心に  
　花の咲くなり」**
