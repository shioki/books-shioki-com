### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a bustling Edo marketplace at dusk, observing laborers unloading heavy goods while merchants shout about efficiency and profit. She pauses, feeling a quiet unease — “Are people valued less than the wares they carry?” Wooden signboards in the background promote “new management methods” in kanji, hinting at the modern theme of efficiency over humanity.
**Dialogue**: “Are people valued less than the wares they carry?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit study, the townsgirl studies Dutch texts and moral philosophy scrolls. A portrait of her mentor hangs on the wall — a middle-aged scholar resembling author Hideo Aiba, with a calm expression, thoughtful eyes, modest scholar’s robes, and slightly graying hair tied neatly. His ghostly figure whispers about “ethics” and “virtue” as the true measure of civilization.

### Panel 3
**Scene**: In the town square, she applies her learning, using an abacus and reasoning from her books to mediate a dispute between merchants and laborers. Her logic reveals the unfairness of their contract, surprising both sides. The composition is dynamic, with scrolls fluttering, townspeople’s shocked faces, and her figure illuminated by sunlight breaking through clouds — symbolizing moral clarity amidst corruption.

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, the townsgirl sits by candlelight, brush in hand, composing a waka on a small tanzaku slip. Her expression is serene yet resolute.
**Dialogue**: (The short poem in Japanese)  
「徳の糸  
　断たれし世にも  
　人はなお  
　誠を織りて  
　明日を生くべし」
**Tanka (translation)**: Even in a world where the thread of virtue is severed, people must still weave sincerity and live for tomorrow.  
**Tanka (original)**: 「徳の糸  
　断たれし世にも  
　人はなお  
　誠を織りて  
　明日を生くべし」
