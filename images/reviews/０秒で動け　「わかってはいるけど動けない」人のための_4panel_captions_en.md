### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a lively Edo street filled with merchants and scholars, holding a scroll of her unfinished poem. She stops in front of a notice board, frowning slightly as she contemplates how people often hesitate before taking action. In the background, a quiet longhouse and the distant sound of a temple bell can be seen. Her inner thoughts reveal her frustration: she knows what to do but feels unable to act.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit study filled with Dutch books and abacuses, the townsgirl opens a newly imported book on "quick thinking." A portrait scroll on the wall depicts a wise middle-aged scholar, imagined as Ito Yoichi, portrayed as a calm and thoughtful man with short hair, no glasses, and wearing simple robes. His warm and logical presence seems to inspire her, and she imagines him saying, "It is good to draw a conclusion and take action." The flickering candlelight illuminates her eager note-taking.
**Dialogue**: "仮に結論を出して動くがよい."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning in the town square, the townsgirl confidently steps forward to test her new idea by organizing a spontaneous counting game for children using abacus beads. She laughs and adjusts her plan in real time, while townspeople watch in surprise as her quick thinking turns confusion into lively learning. The dynamic brush strokes convey the rhythm of immediate action.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the evening, the townsgirl sits by the riverbank under the soft glow of lantern light, brush in hand, feeling serene. She writes her reflection as a waka poem on a small tanzaku, capturing the essence of acting before achieving perfection. The ink flows gently into the night breeze.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: "When I think and cannot act, I wait for the wind; a temporary conclusion will open the way."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「考えて  
　動けぬ時に  
　風を待つ  
　仮の結論  
　道を開かん」
