### Panel 1
**Scene**: On a lively Edo street lined with food stalls and teahouses, the young townsgirl pauses to watch people eating in haste. Their meals seem mechanical, lacking joy. She tilts her head slightly, curiosity and unease flickering in her eyes, as if pondering what “true nourishment” means. A soft wisp of miso soup aroma drifts through the ink-washed air.  
**Dialogue**: (thought) “Why does everyone eat as if chasing time itself…?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a dim study filled with scrolls and foreign books, she pores over a thick Dutch science text. Beside her hangs a portrait scroll of an older scholar with calm, kind eyes — Yamamoto Yukihisa — imagined as the book’s author. Though separated by centuries, their gazes meet through the inked page, teacher and student bound by curiosity and sincerity.  
**Dialogue**: (softly) “Sensei… what does it mean to truly study life?”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl opens a small communal dining space in her neighborhood. Steam rises from bowls of rice and soup lined along a long wooden table. Merchants, children, and laborers gather, sharing laughter and chopsticks. Warmth and connection fill the air — as if Edo itself awakens to a new flavor of togetherness.  
**Dialogue**: (smiling) “Let’s eat slowly… and taste the day together.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Night falls. By the glow of a paper lamp, she writes with quiet focus. A tanzaku rests beside her, bearing a freshly brushed waka that captures the spirit of her journey — the light of morning, the shared spoonful, the star of flavor that binds lives.  
**Dialogue**: (whispering) “May this feeling linger beyond the meal…”  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
Morning meal—  
a single spoonful shines,  
lighting the heart;  
together we live on,  
beneath a star of flavor.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
朝の膳  
心を照らす  
ひと匙に  
共に生きゆく  
味の星あり
