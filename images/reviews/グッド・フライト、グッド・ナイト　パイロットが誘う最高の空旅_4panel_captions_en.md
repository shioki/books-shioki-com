### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through the serene evening streets of Edo, gazing up at the stars twinkling above the tiled rooftops. She expresses her wonder about what it would feel like to soar above the clouds, free from worldly burdens. The flickering lantern light and a gentle breeze play with her hair, igniting a sense of longing and curiosity for the "kingdom of the sky."
**Dialogue**: "What would it be like to fly above the clouds?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a dimly lit study filled with scrolls and Dutch books, she unrolls a newly arrived foreign text—an illustrated treatise on flight. The pages reveal an engraving of a tall, muscular Western man with a calm, reflective demeanor, reminiscent of Mark Vanhoenacker, imagined as a scholarly navigator. Next to him stands a graceful Japanese translator resembling Yuko Okamoto, guiding her finger along the text. The girl’s eyes widen in awe as she reads about flight as a poetic act of connection.
**Dialogue**: "This is incredible! Flight as poetry..."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, she climbs a nearby hill overlooking Edo Bay at dawn. With her arms stretched wide, she feels the wind rushing past her sleeves. She envisions herself as a bird or an airship pilot, tracing invisible paths through the sky that connect distant lands. The bold composition features her silhouette against sweeping clouds, rendered in dynamic sumi-e brushwork, evoking both solitude and freedom.
**Dialogue**: "I can almost feel the sky calling me!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Back in her small room, moonlight spills across the tatami mat as she quietly writes a waka on a small tanzaku slip, her brush trembling with quiet awe. The short poem encapsulates the essence of the book—the union of science and poetry, freedom and belonging.
**Tanka (translation)**: "Above the clouds, my heart soars on a connecting map; even the place to return shines with light."
**Tanka (original)**: 「雲の上　つながる地図に　心飛び  
帰る場所さえ　光に見ゆる」
