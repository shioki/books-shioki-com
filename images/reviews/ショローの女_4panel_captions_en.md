### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a quiet Edo alley at dusk, carrying a bundle of paper and brushes. She overhears elderly women chatting about aging and loss, her expression turning thoughtful as she murmurs, “Does growing older mean merely fading away?” The atmosphere is serene yet filled with quiet curiosity, with wooden houses and hanging lanterns in the background.
**Dialogue**: “Does growing older mean merely fading away?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a small temple library illuminated by candlelight, the townsgirl reads an imported Dutch book and a Japanese essay scroll named “伊藤比呂美.” From the scroll’s illustration, a gentle female scholar appears in spirit form—calm and wise, without defined physical traits but radiating poetic depth and warmth. She teaches the girl that aging is not decline but transformation, blending Eastern and Western elements with Dutch diagrams beside hand-copied waka.
**Dialogue**: “Aging is not decline, but transformation.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, the townsgirl tends to an elderly neighbor’s garden. When the old woman apologizes for her weakness, the girl laughs softly, showing her how new buds sprout on old branches. Ink splashes emphasize vitality and movement, symbolizing acceptance of change, creating a quietly humorous and lively scene.
**Dialogue**: “Look, new buds sprout on old branches!”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In her small room by lamplight, the townsgirl writes a tanka on a hanging scroll. She smiles as she reads it aloud, brush poised midair, embodying a moment of creative joy.
**Dialogue**: “Old age is not the end, but a beginning.”
**Tanka (translation)**: Aging is not the end, but a beginning; both body and words await the spring breeze.
**Tanka (original)**: 「老いとはね  
終わりでなくて  
始まりよ  
身も言の葉も  
春を待つ風」
