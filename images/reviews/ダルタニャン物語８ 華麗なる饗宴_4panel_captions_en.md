### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a lively Edo street at dusk, the townsgirl stands thoughtfully in front of a teahouse, where a samurai is boasting about “honor and love.” The lantern light flickers across her curious eyes as she contemplates the tension between pride and affection.
**Dialogue**: “Can love be power, too?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside her small study, the townsgirl opens a Dutch book next to a Japanese translation of “D’Artagnan Monogatari.” A faint portrait of two imagined authors hangs on the wall, one resembling a broad-shouldered man with curly hair and lively eyes, and the other a calm, bespectacled scholar in Edo attire. Their ghostly figures seem to debate about passion and reason as she eagerly takes notes with a brush.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, the townsgirl mediates a quarrel between two merchants arguing over reputation and affection. With a gentle smile and a scroll of calculations, she explains how “balance” sustains both honor and harmony, turning their pride into cooperation. Their astonished faces reflect a sudden insight as sunlight streams through the shōji.
**Dialogue**: “Balance sustains both honor and harmony.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That evening, under the moonlight by a quiet canal, the townsgirl writes a tanka on a narrow strip of paper, her expression serene yet wistful. A gentle breeze stirs as she whispers her poem.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: Love is also a mirror reflecting strength; pride and vanity fade, only sincerity remains in the fleeting banquet.  
**Tanka (original)**: 愛はまた  
力を映す鏡なり  
誇りと虚栄  
うつろう宴に  
誠のみ残る
