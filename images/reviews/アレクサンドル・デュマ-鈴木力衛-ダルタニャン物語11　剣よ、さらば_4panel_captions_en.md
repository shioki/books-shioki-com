### Panel 1
**Scene**: On a quiet Edo street at dusk, a young townsgirl in kimono watches a group of retired samurai walk slowly by, their swords sheathed and their steps heavy. Long shadows stretch across the alley, and a paper lantern flickers in the evening breeze.  
**Dialogue**: She murmurs softly, “When the time comes to lay down one’s blade…”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study filled with Dutch books and abacuses, the townsgirl unrolls a translation scroll showing two Western scholars—one imagined as Alexandre Dumas, broad-shouldered and expressive, the other as Riki-e Suzuki, a calm Japanese scholar in late Edo attire with spectacles. Their ink-drawn faces seem to debate “glory and soul.” The girl listens as though hearing their voices across time.  
**Dialogue**: (She listens silently, eyes shining with curiosity.)

### Panel 3
**Scene**: In the morning mist outside a temple dojo, the townsgirl holds a wooden practice sword. She helps an elderly ronin hang his scabbard on the wall. They bow deeply to each other, and she smiles with quiet serenity. A single leaf drifts between them, symbolizing that the courage to end is also a form of strength.  
**Dialogue**: (Softly) “To stop is also to be brave.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Sitting by a window, the townsgirl writes a tanka on a small tanzaku card with her brush. Beside her, the sword she once practiced with rests quietly next to a vase of plum blossoms. Her expression is calm and luminous as she recites her poem.  
**Dialogue**: (Reciting gently)  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
“Laying down the sword,  
the stillness of its shadow  
fills my heart—  
in a friend’s gentle smile  
shines an eternal light.”  
**Tanka (original)**:  
「剣おきて  
　影の静けさ  
　胸に満ち  
　友の笑みこそ  
　永遠の光」
