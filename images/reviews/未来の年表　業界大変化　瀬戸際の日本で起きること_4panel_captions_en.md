### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a lively Edo street market, the townsgirl stops to listen as merchants express their concerns about the declining number of visitors each year. She tilts her head, sensing a deeper unease about the town's shrinking population. The background features wooden shopfronts, paper lanterns, and townspeople exchanging goods.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside her study, illuminated by a paper lantern, the townsgirl is engrossed in a thick treatise on Dutch studies. An illustration shows a wise scholar resembling the author Kawai Masashi, depicted as a calm, middle-aged man with a scholarly presence, slender frame, and thoughtful eyes, dressed in simple robes and holding a scroll. His spectral image conveys: “Population is not prediction—it is destiny.” The girl listens intently, her brush poised over her notes.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, she stands before her neighbors, drawing a diagram on the ground with a bamboo stick. She illustrates how to “strategically gather” homes and shops instead of spreading them thin. The townspeople appear surprised yet intrigued. The composition is dynamic, with her kimono fluttering as she gestures, sunlight streaming through the gaps between buildings.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That evening, under the moonlight, she writes a waka on a small tanzaku paper, her expression serene yet determined. The poem reads:  
「減る町に　知恵を寄せ合う　灯ともす　縮むを生きる　未来のしるべ」  
The brushstrokes are expressive, with soft ink shading that evokes both melancholy and hope.  
**Tanka (translation)**: In a shrinking town, we share our wisdom, lighting the way—a guide to living in a diminished future.  
**Tanka (original)**: 減る町に　知恵を寄せ合う　灯ともす　縮むを生きる　未来のしるべ
