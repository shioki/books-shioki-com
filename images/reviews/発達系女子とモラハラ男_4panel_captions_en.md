### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a quiet Edo backstreet, carrying a basket of scrolls. She overhears a couple arguing, one accusing the other of being "lazy" and "not listening." She pauses, her brows knit in thought, sensing a deeper misunderstanding. Wooden houses and paper lanterns sway in the background.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small candlelit room, the townsgirl studies a Dutch medical text beside a scroll of wasan formulas. A portrait of two scholars hangs on the wall—one man with calm eyes and scholarly features, and one woman with gentle but determined eyes and tied-up hair. They watch over her studies as she murmurs about fairness.
**Dialogue**: “Fairness… not in time or tasks, but in the mind’s fatigue…”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, she helps neighbors divide chores, presenting a hand-drawn chart showing “mental energy” measured with abacus-like beads. The others look astonished as she explains that fairness means balancing invisible burdens. A humorous moment occurs when one man scratches his head, questioning her.
**Dialogue**: “So even thinking counts as work?”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In moonlight by the riverbank, the townsgirl kneels with brush and paper, writing a tanka that captures her realization. Her expression is serene and strong, with the wind rippling her sleeves. The short poem appears elegantly beside her on a vertical tanzaku.
**Tanka (translation)**: "To share the unseen burdens is the path we walk together; in the balance of our hearts, a world in harmony."
**Tanka (original)**: 「見えぬ荷を  
　分かちあうこそ  
　共の道  
　心の秤に  
　釣り合う世界」
