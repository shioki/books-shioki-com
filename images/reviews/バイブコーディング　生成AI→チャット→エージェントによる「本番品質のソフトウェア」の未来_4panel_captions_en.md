### Panel 1
**Scene**: On a bustling Edo street next to a nagaya row house, the townsgirl observes a remarkable karakuri scribe swiftly producing calculation scrolls for a merchant. The machine, proud of its work, gestures triumphantly, but its scrolls yield the same result for different piles of rice bales. The merchant celebrates, oblivious to the contradiction, while the heroine leans forward, curiosity piqued, holding a soroban in one hand. The setting features period-accurate shops, paper lanterns, wooden buildings, townspeople, and distant temple roofs, all depicted with dynamic diagonal composition and lively brushwork.

**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a cluttered terakoya study, the townsgirl immerses herself in Dutch books, wasan manuals, a soroban, geometric sangaku diagrams, and a disassembled karakuri mechanism. She realizes that the human role extends beyond merely making the machine write; it involves defining goals, dividing tasks, testing results, comparing options, and accepting responsibility. She is surrounded by small task slips, modular wooden parts, branching scroll paths, and rapid feedback loops illustrated by repeated test weights and abacus checks. Three imagined Edo scholars represent authors Gene Kim, Steve Yegge, and Takahiro Nagao, portrayed as fictional intellectual avatars.

**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: A humorous action scene unfolds from an exaggerated low angle as the townsgirl applies her lessons at a busy riverside rice warehouse. Instead of assigning one large task to the karakuri scribe, she feeds it a series of small illustrated task slips, tests each wooden module, and inspects every scroll. Several alternative mechanisms operate side by side for comparison. The overconfident automaton prematurely poses in victory, but she tests an extreme case with a tiny rice bag and a large rice bale, revealing a hidden fixed peg that caused the same answer each time. The machine humorously sprays soroban beads into the air as she catches the error and replaces the rigid peg with a modular gear.

**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In a quiet, emotionally resonant scene at dusk, the townsgirl sits on a wooden veranda, watching the now-reliable modular karakuri mechanism work beside the warehouse. Neatly tied tested scrolls and stored alternative parts surround her, with a soroban resting nearby. She holds a brush, writing a vertical tanka on a plain hanging poetry strip. Her expression is serene yet resolute, acknowledging that judgment and responsibility remain human, even when machines perform labor. The atmosphere features spacious negative space, soft ink wash, drifting paper fibers, and a distant crescent moon.

**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: From the writing hand,  
To question and ascertain,  
Wisdom is required.  
Dividing tasks so small,  
The burden rests on me.
