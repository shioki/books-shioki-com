### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a small Edo-period kitchen, the young townsgirl stands before a cluttered table filled with ingredients. She looks frustrated, holding a cookbook full of complicated recipes. Through the window, she can hear neighbors chatting about their elaborate meals, which only deepens her doubt.  
**Dialogue**: *“I’ll never be able to cook something perfect like that…”*

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Later that evening, by the gentle glow of a candle, she studies a foreign-style book. From its pages emerge faint ink portraits of two imagined thinkers — Sasaki Noriyoshi, calm and composed, and Yuka Yamaguchi, lively and warm. They seem to speak to her, teaching that cooking is not about perfection, but about finding one’s own rhythm and satisfaction.  
**Dialogue**: *“Perfection is not the goal — listen to your own rhythm.”*

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Back in her kitchen, she experiments with simplicity — grilling dumplings and warming a bowl of rice. Steam rises softly as she tastes her meal, her face lighting up with surprise and joy. Her cat watches curiously from the corner, adding a touch of humor and warmth.  
**Dialogue**: *“It’s simple… but it tastes just right!”*

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, under the gentle light of a lantern, she writes a waka poem on a small strip of paper. Her expression is serene, the moment quiet and reflective — a sense of harmony found in imperfection.  
**Dialogue**: *(none)*  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
Letting go of perfection,  
I savor this humble meal—  
a taste that feels right,  
and in that quiet contentment,  
my day finds its balance.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
「完璧を　捨てて味わう　小さき膳  
　納得の味　わが日を整う」
