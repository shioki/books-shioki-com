### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through an Edo street at night, illuminated by the full moon. She overhears two constables arguing about “justice” and “orders,” her eyes widening with curiosity as she ponders, “Is justice determined by rank or by heart?” The flickering street lanterns cast long shadows, hinting at hidden conflicts.
**Dialogue**: “Is justice decided by rank or by heart?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: At home, she studies by candlelight. An imagined mentor emerges from her Dutch and Chinese books — a composed, intellectual woman in her 50s, with a gentle yet firm expression, hair in a simple bun, dressed in scholarly robes. This figure embodies the essence of author Yuzuki Yūko: dignified, thoughtful, and quietly strong.
**Dialogue**: “True justice begins when you dare to question your own.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The following day, the townsgirl steps in to mediate a dispute between merchants and an official misusing power. She employs reason and arithmetic to uncover the truth in the accounts, astonishing everyone. The scene is dynamic, with her sleeve fluttering as she points to the ledger, the moon faintly visible through the paper windows, symbolizing moral clarity piercing through darkness.
**Dialogue**: “Let me show you the truth in the accounts!”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Later, she kneels by the riverbank under the same moon, brush in hand, composing a waka on a tanzaku slip. The wind rustles the reeds; her expression is calm yet determined.
**Tanka (translation)**: Justice is not merely a voice,  
I understand this on a moonlit night.  
Embracing the weak,  
Let it become a light.  
**Tanka (original)**: 正義とは  
声にあらずと  
知る月夜  
弱きを抱きて  
光となれり
