### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a lively Edo street near a temple school, observing children reciting their lessons. She pauses, her face reflecting curiosity and quiet concern as she wonders why some children struggle while others excel. In the background, a wooden schoolhouse stands with children writing with brushes, and paper lanterns sway gently in the breeze.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside her small study illuminated by an oil lamp, the townsgirl opens a mysterious scroll about foreign learning. The scroll features two portraits: one of a Western woman with shoulder-length dark brown hair and gentle eyes, and another of a Japanese woman with short bobbed black hair and glasses. They appear as spirit-like mentors emerging from the pages, sharing insights about trust in teachers, fairness through support, and the cultural roots of learning. The townsgirl listens intently, brush poised, her face glowing with awe.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl teaches local children arithmetic under a cherry tree, adapting her teaching methods so every child can follow along. One child drops his abacus beads, and she laughs warmly, demonstrating that learning can be a joyful experience. Above them, the spirits of the two authors watch approvingly from a faint cloud, smiling gently.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the evening, the townsgirl sits by the river with a brush in hand, gazing at the reflections of lanterns on the water. She writes a tanka on a small tanzaku paper, capturing the essence of the book’s message. The atmosphere is serene, with the ink stroke of her brush forming the poem.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: Learning is a mirror reflecting the heart of people, weaving the future with threads of culture.  
**Tanka (original)**: 学びとは  
人のこころを  
映す鏡  
文化の糸で  
未来を紡ぐ
