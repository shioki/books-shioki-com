### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a bustling Edo street filled with merchant stalls and longhouses, the townsgirl sits beside a paper lantern, gazing at a pile of unfinished abacus ledgers. She sighs and reflects on how she spends her days tallying others' accounts, leaving no time to think or dream. The background depicts townsfolk hurrying about, symbolizing the monotony of daily life.
**Dialogue**: "I spend all day counting others' accounts... no time to think or dream."

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit study room, the townsgirl opens a Dutch-bound book titled “Before DX.” From its pages, a serene figure resembling the author Tsuzuma Chiyonori appears—a scholarly man in simple robes, with thoughtful eyes and a gentle smile, holding a brush and scroll. He gestures to a diagram of gears and flowing water, explaining the importance of creating time to think and building lasting systems.
**Dialogue**: "To create time to think and build systems that last..."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Back at her workplace, the townsgirl rearranges her tasks using clever wooden automata and a pulley-driven writing device she designed. Townsfolk watch in amazement as the contraption automatically copies receipts, freeing her hands. Humorously, her cat naps on the machine, symbolizing the peace that comes from efficiency. She smiles, realizing she has created "a system that thinks for her."
**Dialogue**: "I've built a system that thinks for me!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As night falls, the townsgirl sits by candlelight, brush poised over a small tanzaku. She softly recites a waka that encapsulates the essence of the book. The panel is tranquil, with a breeze flowing through paper screens and the silhouette of the author's figure fading like ink mist behind her.
**Dialogue**: "Thinking time is treasure, anew... systems open paths like the wind."
**Tanka (translation)**: Thinking time is treasure, anew; systems open paths like the wind.
