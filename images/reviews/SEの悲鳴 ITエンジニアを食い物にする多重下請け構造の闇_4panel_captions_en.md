### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a lively Edo street filled with merchants and craftsmen. She overhears two artisans expressing their frustration about their work being “passed down” through many intermediaries, leaving them with little reward. She tilts her head thoughtfully, sensing an injustice in the hidden structure of commerce.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study illuminated by a paper lantern, the townsgirl studies a Dutch book on trade systems and a wasan scroll. Behind her stands a gentle, scholarly man, her imagined teacher inspired by Tanaka Hiroaki. He has a calm expression, round glasses, neat short hair, and wears a modest kimono. He gestures to a diagram showing how value diminishes with too many middlemen, while she listens intently, her eyes sparkling with understanding.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl draws a diagram in the dirt of the street, demonstrating to her neighbors how direct exchange could benefit everyone. She uses an abacus and sketches lines connecting people directly, while the crowd reacts in astonishment. A humorous moment occurs as a merchant exclaims, “So fewer hands means more rice for us all!” while she smiles knowingly.
**Dialogue**: “So fewer hands means more rice for us all!”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dusk, the townsgirl kneels by the riverbank, brush in hand, writing a waka on a hanging scroll. Her expression is serene yet resolute, with the lantern light reflecting on her ink.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: The unseen threads bind us with human wisdom; let the light shine through, even in the path of skill.  
**Tanka (original)**: 見えぬ糸　結ぶは人の　知恵なれば　光を通せ　技の道にも
