### Panel 1
**Scene**: In the bustling Edo marketplace, the townsgirl observes a group of workers in a crowded delivery yard moving boxes endlessly under a merchant’s orders. She tilts her head, sensing something unsettling — everyone moves like clockwork, yet no one smiles. The background shows warehouses and narrow alleys; she murmurs, “Is efficiency always kind to the heart?”
**Dialogue**: “Is efficiency always kind to the heart?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study lit by candlelight, she pores over a Dutch mechanical treatise and a borrowed manuscript by a scholar resembling the author Yokota Masuo — a middle-aged man with calm, piercing eyes, neatly tied hair, scholarly robes, and a thoughtful air. His portrait on the scroll seems to speak: “To see the empire within the marketplace, one must enter its gears.” She listens intently, eyes gleaming with curiosity.
**Dialogue**: “To see the empire within the marketplace, one must enter its gears.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next day, she experiments with an abacus-driven contraption for sorting goods. The mechanism works flawlessly — but her friend toils beside it, exhausted. Startled, she realizes her invention mirrors the same dehumanizing efficiency she pondered. She stops the device mid-motion, ink splattering as she gasps, “Even progress can bind the hands that build it…”
**Dialogue**: “Even progress can bind the hands that build it…”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At twilight, seated by the riverbank, she writes a waka on a hanging scroll, the reflection of lanterns shimmering on the water. Her expression is serene yet contemplative as she recites softly.
**Dialogue**:  
「  
人の手を  
機のごとくに  
使うとも  
心の灯は  
消ゆることなし  
」
**Tanka (translation)**: Even if we use hands like machines, the light of the heart will never extinguish.
**Tanka (original)**:  
人の手を  
機のごとくに  
使うとも  
心の灯は
