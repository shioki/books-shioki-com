### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a bustling Edo street at dusk, observing carpenters and merchants working late by lantern light. She tilts her head, feeling a sense of unease — “Why do they work even after the bells of yūgure?” The scene conveys the exhaustion of endless labor amidst the lively townscape.
**Dialogue**: “Why do they work even after the bells of yūgure?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a quiet temple study, the townsgirl is engrossed in a Dutch-bound book and a scroll of moral philosophy. A hanging scroll features a portrait of a calm, intelligent man, imagined as author Shinjo Ko, who appears as a mentor. He seems thoughtful, exuding quiet intellect, as if whispering, “Justice must rest upon human dignity.”
**Dialogue**: “Justice must rest upon human dignity.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: In the town square, the girl sets up a wooden notice board with the message “Rest is not sloth — it is the breath of the heart.” Curious neighbors gather around. A merchant chuckles, and a craftsman nods in relief. The girl smiles, her ink-stained hands trembling slightly, realizing that knowledge can move hearts even without authority.
**Dialogue**: “Rest is not sloth — it is the breath of the heart.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Night has returned. The townsgirl writes by candlelight, her brush gliding over paper as she composes a tanka that encapsulates her learnings. The flickering candlelight dances beside her calm face, reflecting both sorrow and hope.  
**Tanka (translation)**: Working is like a blade that carves the heart, yet in hands that trust each other, the light will shine.  
**Tanka (original)**: 働くとは　心を削る　刃なれど  
信じ合う手に　灯はともるや
