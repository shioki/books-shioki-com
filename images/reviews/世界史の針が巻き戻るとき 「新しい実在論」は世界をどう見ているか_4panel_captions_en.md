### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a bustling Edo street filled with merchants and children, the townsgirl gazes at a paper lantern displaying distorted news headlines, reflecting the rumors spreading rapidly around her. She frowns slightly, contemplating how “truth” seems obscured by layers of illusion, creating an uneasy atmosphere filled with smoke and chatter.
**Dialogue**: "It seems the truth is hidden beneath these illusions..."

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit study, the townsgirl is engrossed in a newly arrived Dutch book on philosophy. Two imagined figures appear in an ink-like haze; one is a calm Western philosopher with sharp eyes and wavy hair, while the other is a composed Japanese scholar in traditional robes. They seem to engage in a silent debate about “reality” and “ethics,” while the townsgirl listens with wide eyes, filled with curiosity.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl steps outside to the riverside, where townsfolk are arguing over a rumor. She confidently spreads a piece of parchment inscribed with diagrams and Dutch words, explaining that “facts” must be perceived as they truly are, not as they are presented. The crowd pauses, beginning to realize their misunderstanding, as the composition contrasts illusion and clarity through strong diagonal brushstrokes.
**Dialogue**: "We must see the facts as they are, not as they appear!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As night falls, the townsgirl sits beside a lantern, her ink brush poised as she composes a waka on a small tanzaku. Her expression is serene, softly illuminated by the lantern's glow.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]
**Tanka (translation)**: "Without drowning in the facade of the world, I take up my brush; the light of truth illuminates others."
**Tanka (original)**: 「見せかけの  
世に溺れずに  
筆をとる  
事実の光  
人を照らして」
