### Panel 1
**Scene**: On a lively Edo street at dusk, a young townsgirl pauses as she overhears merchants arguing about who should take responsibility when things go wrong. Lantern light flickers across her thoughtful face; a scroll is tucked under her arm.  
**Dialogue**: “I wonder… is ignorance itself a kind of sin?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study, lit by a single candle, she sits surrounded by rangaku scrolls and arithmetic tablets. On the wall hangs a portrait of the imagined scholar Hayami Kazuma — calm, plain, yet with eyes that seem to pierce silence. The portrait almost seems to whisper to her.  
**Dialogue**: “To know or not to know — that is the weight of responsibility.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At the local temple school, children quarrel over a broken abacus. The townsgirl steps in, gently guiding them to share responsibility and learn together. With her finger, she draws circles in the sand to show how all things are connected. The children watch in awe as she compares their quarrel to “a world that hides truth behind silence.”  
**Dialogue**: “When we share our mistakes, we uncover what silence conceals.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At night by the riverbank, moonlight ripples on the water. The townsgirl kneels, brush in hand, writing a tanka on a strip of paper that drifts downstream. Her expression holds both sorrow and calm acceptance.  
**Dialogue**: (none)  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
I ask the moon —  
Is it sin not to seek truth?  
Even silence, too,  
may take the shape of mercy,  
softly forgiving all.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
知ろうとも  
せぬを罪とは  
月に問う  
沈黙もまた  
赦しのかたち
