### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through an Edo street filled with scholars and merchants discussing a “miracle discovery” noted on scrolls. She appears puzzled, holding her abacus and notebook, pondering whether “truth in learning” can be fabricated. The atmosphere is curious yet uneasy, with a temple school and fluttering papers in the background.  
**Dialogue**: "Can truth in learning really be fabricated?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study at night, the townsgirl reads a thick Dutch science book next to a portrait scroll of her imagined mentor, “Suda Momoko.” The portrait depicts a composed female scholar with tied-back hair and gentle eyes, dressed in simple robes, symbolizing honesty and intellectual rigor. The flickering candlelight highlights her realization that scientific truth relies on faithful methods and verification rather than mere brilliance.  
**Dialogue**: "Truth in science must be built on verification."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next day, she conducts a small experiment at the well, testing water purity with simple reagents from her rangaku notes. Neighbors gather, intrigued. When one praises her as a prodigy, she laughs and responds, “No, it’s not magic—only careful proof!” The scene captures her joy in discovery and the humor of applying deep science in a humble Edo setting.  
**Dialogue**: "It's not magic—only careful proof!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At her writing desk, with moonlight streaming in, she dips her brush and writes a waka on a hanging tanzaku. Her expression is serene yet determined, embodying the essence of the book's message.  
**Dialogue**:  
**Tanka (translation)**: "Sincerity is the foundation of learning; falsehoods cloud the light, the mirror of knowledge."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「誠こそ　学びの礎　偽りは  
　光を曇らす　知の鏡かな」
