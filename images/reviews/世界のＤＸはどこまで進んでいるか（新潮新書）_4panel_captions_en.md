### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a lively Edo street market, observing merchants shouting and clerks using abacuses. She expresses her curiosity about how the world could change if knowledge flowed like water through all homes, hinting at the concept of transformation.
**Dialogue**: "What if knowledge could flow like water through every house?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a dim study, the townsgirl studies a Dutch scientific text alongside a new scroll titled "How the World Changes." A scholarly figure resembling Kanji Amemiya appears, dressed in Edo-style robes, explaining transformation with a diagram of three circles labeled "Tools," "Processes," and "Management Transformation."
**Dialogue**: "Transformation comes from understanding these elements."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl enthusiastically experiments with her newfound knowledge, arranging wooden tablets and abacuses to create a miniature town economy. Villagers look on in confusion as she asserts that true change comes from people's thoughts and actions, illustrated by swirling ink lines representing connected minds and data.
**Dialogue**: "Change starts not with tools, but with how we think and act!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As night falls, the townsgirl kneels by the window, gazing at the moon's reflection in a basin of water, symbolizing the digital twin of the world. She gracefully writes a tanka on a hanging scroll.
**Dialogue**: 
**Tanka (translation)**: "The moment hearts change, more than tools, becomes the key to open the future."
**Tanka (original)**: 「道具より  
こころの変わる  
ときこそが  
未来をひらく  
鍵となるなり」
