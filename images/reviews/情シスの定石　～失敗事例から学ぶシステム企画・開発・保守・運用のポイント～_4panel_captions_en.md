### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a lively Edo street filled with merchants and lanterns, the townsgirl pauses to listen to two clerks arguing about a failed merchant ledger system. She tilts her head in thought, pondering why plans often go awry despite much effort. The scene captures her curiosity awakening.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a serene terakoya lit by paper lanterns, the townsgirl studies a Dutch manuscript that illustrates “system flow” diagrams. Two imagined mentors appear behind her as dreamlike sages: one is a calm, bespectacled scholar holding a scroll, while the other is a gentle, poetic figure with long hair and serene eyes. They point to a chalkboard filled with stages labeled “Planning,” “Development,” “Operation,” and “Maintenance,” as she listens intently.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Back in the town, the townsgirl applies her knowledge by organizing villagers to create a new water distribution system. She sketches a plan on the ground with a brush, explaining that “the first design decides all.” The townsfolk nod in admiration. A humorous twist occurs when a neighbor accidentally spills water on the plan, prompting her to laugh and say, “We’ll review before building again—it’s cheaper so!”
**Dialogue**: “We’ll review before building again—it’s cheaper so!”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That evening, under the moonlight, the townsgirl writes a waka on a narrow tanzaku, her brush poised with quiet determination. The focus is on her serene face and the ink flowing gracefully across the paper.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: "The first design determines the harmony among people; correcting mistakes is a path a hundred times longer."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「企ての  
　初めに定む  
　人の和よ  
　誤り直すは  
　百倍の道」
