### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through the early morning streets of Edo, with sunlight filtering through narrow alleys. She observes two children promising to stay in the light together, pausing to reflect on the power of such simple words to connect hearts. Her expression shows a mix of curiosity and quiet melancholy as she gazes at the flickering light and shadow on the shōji screen.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study, the townsgirl is engrossed in a Dutch book about optics and a treatise on human emotion. A nearby hanging scroll displays a simple portrait of a serene scholar with gentle eyes and short hair, imagined to represent the author Ichihō Michi. The calm, introspective figure radiates both tenderness and sorrow. The townsgirl murmurs, “Do light and shadow live together?”
**Dialogue**: “Do light and shadow live together?”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At dusk, the townsgirl experiments with a polished copper mirror and a piece of paper, observing how light creates both brilliance and darkness. The reflection unexpectedly splits, illuminating one side of her face while leaving the other in shadow. She laughs softly, realizing that both parts are equally her. A nearby cat chases the shifting light spot, adding humor and liveliness to the scene.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Sitting by an oil lamp, the townsgirl writes a short waka on a small tanzaku strip, her face serene yet glowing with quiet insight. The poem encapsulates the essence of her reflections — embracing both light and shadow as integral parts of life.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: Living on, embracing both light and shadow, one promise ignites the heart.  
**Tanka (original)**: 光にも　影にも寄り添い　生きてゆく  
約束ひとつ　胸の灯とす
