### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a quiet lane of Edo, the townsgirl observes her neighbors arguing about the ideal image of a "happy" family. She tilts her head, sensing something strange about their pursuit of perfection. The background features narrow townhouses and laundry swaying in the breeze.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: At her small writing desk illuminated by candlelight, the townsgirl opens a Dutch-bound book. She reads about an older scholar depicted as a calm, thoughtful man in his sixties, with gentle eyes and a composed demeanor, reminiscent of author Shigematsu Kiyoshi. His image seems to whisper to her: “Kindness may heal more than correctness.” The brushstrokes of his portrait blend softly into the candle smoke.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, she discovers two children arguing over whose arithmetic answer is correct. Instead of reprimanding them, she kneels with a smile and praises both for “not giving up halfway.” Their tension melts into laughter as a gentle breeze lifts cherry petals around them, symbolizing renewal.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dusk, the townsgirl writes a waka poem on her paper fan while gazing at the fading light over the rooftops. The poem reads:  
「正しさを　やさしさ包み　続ければ　途切れぬ絆　希望の丘に」  
The composition leaves soft space above her, with ink strokes fading like quiet hope.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: "If kindness wraps around correctness, the bonds will remain unbroken, on the hill of hope."  
**Tanka (original)**: 正しさを　やさしさ包み　続ければ　途切れぬ絆　希望の丘に
