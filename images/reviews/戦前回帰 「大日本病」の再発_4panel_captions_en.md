### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a bustling Edo street near a shrine, the townsgirl observes townsfolk enthusiastically praising a local lord's new decree, sensing an unsettling ease in their blind acceptance of "good will." Merchant banners flutter in the background as townspeople bow respectfully.

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dim terakoya, the townsgirl studies a scroll titled "The Record of Great Japan's Illness." A calm, sharp-eyed scholar resembling Yamazaki Masahiro appears in a portrait or as a spirit-like teacher, pointing to a diagram contrasting "Reason" and "Faith," warning of the dangers of blind devotion. The girl listens attentively, ready to take notes.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At a town meeting, the girl witnesses cheers for a new "moral campaign." She raises a question, challenging the crowd's logic with insights from the scholar. The atmosphere shifts to silence as a few townsfolk begin to ponder. A banner reading "Virtue Above All" flutters ironically in the background, highlighting her resolve.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Under the night sky, she sits beside a paper lantern, writing a waka on a tanzaku. Her expression is calm yet determined as the wind gently rustles the shōji. The poem reflects her thoughts on reason and virtue.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: "In the name of good, reason sleeps to the sound of the wind; when I question myself, light shines once more."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「善の名に　理性を眠らす　風の音　己を問えば　光また射す」
