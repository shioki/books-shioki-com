### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a lively Edo street adorned with paper lanterns and merchant houses, the townsgirl stands in front of a bookstore, captivated by a scroll titled "The Predictive Power of Classics." She muses about how ancient books might foresee the future, her eyes sparkling with curiosity as cherry blossoms float by, symbolizing the eternal flow of ideas.
**Dialogue**: "I wonder how these old books can predict the future..."

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit temple study filled with scrolls and foreign books, the townsgirl consults an elderly scholar inspired by Akimitsu Yoshihiko. He has a calm demeanor, short hair, and wears scholarly robes. With a warm presence, he points to an open manuscript, explaining that “a true classic reveals the future because it captures human essence.”
**Dialogue**: "A true classic reveals the future because it captures the essence of humanity."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through a bustling market, observing lively debates over prices, gossip, and news sharing. She begins to connect her readings to real life, realizing the universal nature of humanity's repetition. Holding an abacus and a Dutch book, she symbolizes the blend of logic and empathy, her expression ignited with insight.
**Dialogue**: "It's amazing how human nature repeats itself..."

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dusk by the riverbank, the townsgirl writes a waka on a hanging tanzaku. The gentle wind creates ripples in the water as she gazes at her serene reflection, inspired. The poem, elegantly written in brush calligraphy, reads:
**Tanka (translation)**: "The shadows reflected in ancient books illuminate the present, continuing thoughts to know the light of the future."
**Tanka (original)**: 「古の  
書に映る影  
今を照らす  
思ひ続けて  
未来を識る灯」
