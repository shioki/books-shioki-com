### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a lively Edo street, observing merchants and foreign sailors unloading goods at the port. She overhears discussions about "foreign workers" being "temporary helpers," her face reflecting curiosity and quiet unease as she contemplates the invisibility of those who coexist with them.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study filled with scrolls and Dutch books, the townsgirl studies a thick tome about people crossing seas and borders. An illustrated figure resembling the author, Yudai Mochizuki, appears on the page, with calm, thoughtful eyes behind round spectacles, tidy hair, and a serene yet determined presence. He seems to explain how a nation can deny the existence of what is already present.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl visits a riverside construction site where foreign craftsmen work alongside Edo laborers. She shares food and laughter with them, realizing the similarities in their lives and dreams. A gust of wind lifts her hair, and the laughter of the workers resonates, capturing a moment of shared humanity through dynamic brush strokes.  
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In a night setting, the townsgirl sits by lamplight, brush in hand, composing a waka on a small tanzaku paper. Her eyes reflect both sorrow and determination as she writes her poem.  
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: "Even in the shadows of a nation that turns a blind eye, I will support the tomorrow of those voiceless."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「見ぬふりの  
国のかげにも  
息づける  
声なき人の  
明日を支えん」
