### Panel 1
**Scene**: In the lively streets of Edo, the townsgirl listens to the townsfolk gossiping passionately about a scandal. She feels a strange unease as she wonders why everyone believes what is comfortable. The background features narrow alleys, paper lanterns, and a signboard that reads “Terakoya.”
**Dialogue**: “Why does everyone believe what feels comfortable?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her dimly lit study, the townsgirl reads a newly arrived foreign-style book on “reason and thought.” An apparition resembling the author Takashi Odajima appears, a middle-aged man with short graying hair and glasses, wearing a modest jacket. He speaks like a teacher, imparting wisdom about true intellect.
**Dialogue**: “True intellect is the courage to doubt, to pause, and to know one’s limits.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next day at the marketplace, the townsgirl faces a crowd engaged in a heated debate. Instead of conforming to the popular opinion, she quietly raises a question, causing the crowd to freeze in silence. Her calm and radiant expression shows her newfound strength.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That evening, under the moonlight, the townsgirl writes a waka poem with a brush in hand, her face serene. The poem, written vertically on a tanzaku, reflects her thoughts on courage and knowledge.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]
**Tanka (translation)**: “With courage to doubt, the tip of my brush sharpens thoughts, igniting the light of knowledge.”
**Tanka (original)**: 「  
空気より  
思ひを磨く  
筆の先  
疑ふ勇気  
知の灯をともす  
」
