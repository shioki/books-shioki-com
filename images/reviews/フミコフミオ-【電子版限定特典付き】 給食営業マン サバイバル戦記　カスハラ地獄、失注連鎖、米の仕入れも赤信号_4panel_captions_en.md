### Panel 1
**Scene**: In the lively Edo marketplace, the young townsgirl walks among merchants arguing over rice prices and customers voicing complaints. She pauses mid-step, frowning slightly as she senses an imbalance between “service” and “dignity.” Wooden rice barrels and a food stall line the street, while soft morning light filters through the narrow alleys.  
**Dialogue**: (None)

### Panel 2
**Scene**: That night, in her candlelit study, she reads a thick Dutch-bound book about trade ethics. The calm spirit of a middle-aged scholar appears beside her — composed, wearing round spectacles and a simple robe. He gestures toward a scroll that reads “The courage to say no.” The townsgirl listens intently, her eyes widening in understanding.  
**Dialogue**: Scholar (on scroll): “The courage to say no.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next day, a wealthy rice wholesaler proposes an unfair deal. The townsgirl bows politely yet firmly refuses. The merchant, shocked, drops his abacus as bystanders gasp. A breeze lifts her sleeve — she stands serene and resolute, embodying the lesson she learned.  
**Dialogue**: Townsgirl: “I must decline.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Evening falls. Sitting by the window, she writes a waka poem on a slender tanzaku card. Lantern light flickers outside as the distant bustle of merchants fades into quiet. The poem beside her expresses the essence of what she has learned.  
**Dialogue**: (None)  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
Weighing loss and gain,  
Unshaken upon the scale—  
To sever with truth,  
Knowing that even refusal  
Can be the path of sincerity.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
損と益　  
秤にかけて　  
揺るぎなく　  
断つも誠の　  
道と知りたり
