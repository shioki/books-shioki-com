### Panel 1
**Scene**: In the bustling streets of Edo, the townsgirl observes a group of merchants engaged in a heated argument over their mismatched ledgers. She tilts her head in curiosity, pondering about “systems that do not communicate with each other.” The background features wooden signboards, an abacus, and merchant houses, while her eyes sparkle with intellectual curiosity.
**Dialogue**: "Systems that do not speak to each other..."

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit terakoya, the townsgirl studies scrolls filled with Dutch mechanical diagrams and a peculiar “bank of numbers” chart. A group of scholarly elders, inspired by the authors, gathers around her. They are six figures, each embodying a calm, analytical demeanor: one with round spectacles (resembling 山端), another holding a brush (岡部), one serious and methodical (中田), one jovial and broad-shouldered (大和田), one soft-spoken yet observant (谷島), and one wearing a robe with the crest “日経.” They discuss the dangers of “leaders ignoring the underlying mechanisms,” their gestures composed and thoughtful.
**Dialogue**: "When leaders ignore the mechanism beneath..."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl sketches an intricate network on her tatami mat, connecting rice merchants, moneylenders, and temples to symbolize “system integration.” However, the threads become tangled, and she bursts into laughter, realizing that “without unity of hearts, the system collapses.” Her ink splashes create a chaotic yet elegant pattern, reflecting both failure and insight.
**Dialogue**: "Without unity of hearts, the system collapses!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: By the soft glow of lamplight, she smiles gently as she writes a waka on a hanging scroll, her brushstrokes both graceful and determined. The short poem reads:

**Tanka (translation)**: "Weaving the threads,  
Even tangled threads without hearts,  
If we tie them anew,  
Light will shine on the fabric  
Of the future."

**Tanka (original)**:  
経を織り  
心通わぬ糸もつれ  
結び直せば  
未来の布に  
光さすなり
