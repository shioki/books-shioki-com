### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a lively Edo street filled with merchant stalls, the townsgirl observes a moneylender dismissing a poor craftsman who has no collateral. She tilts her head in contemplation, pondering how "trust" could be interpreted in various ways. The background features wooden signboards, coins, and a small shrine, while her expression reflects curiosity and quiet indignation.
**Dialogue**: "Isn't trust something that can be measured differently?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a candle-lit study, the townsgirl studies several foreign scrolls and Dutch-bound books about "data" and "credit." Five spectral figures resembling the authors emerge from the pages, depicted as scholarly spirits: Ren Wei appears calm and sharp-eyed with round spectacles; Bian Hui is serene with her hair tied high; Su Xianghui has a patient, teacherly demeanor; Cao Pengcheng stands confidently with a broad forehead; and Asako Nagai exudes dignity with soft features suggesting wisdom. They gesture as if explaining how invisible numbers can reveal unseen trust, while the townsgirl listens in awe, her brush hovering above her notebook.

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At the local market, the townsgirl takes action by painting a wooden board that displays each vendor's "trust marks," based on their honesty and reliability as gathered from townsfolk stories. Shoppers begin to pay with confidence, and laughter fills the air. The scene is vibrant and dynamic, showcasing a playful Edo reinterpretation of "data-driven credit." The townsgirl beams as a vendor thanks her, holding up a basket of sweets as payment.

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the evening, the townsgirl sits by the riverbank under a willow tree, writing a waka on a small tanzaku. The moonlight reflects on the water as she smiles, brush poised. The poem is written vertically on the tanzaku:
**Tanka (translation)**: "Invisible virtues / Reflected in numbers, / Threads that connect us, / The true hearts of people / Reside in their skills."
**Tanka (original)**: 「  
見えぬ徳  
数に映して  
つなぐ糸  
人のまごころ  
技に宿りぬ  
」
