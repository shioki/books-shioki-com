### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a bustling Edo marketplace, observing a group of men debating the prices of imported goods and cheering for a “ball game” played by foreign sailors at the port. She tilts her head, curious about how a single game could evoke such wealth and enthusiasm. The background features the harbor and merchant stalls, illustrating the global exchange of goods and ideas.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study illuminated by lamplight, the townsgirl opens a Dutch-language book on economics and culture. The page reveals the portrait of a wise scholar resembling Michio Katano — depicted as a calm, middle-aged man with thoughtful eyes, neatly tied hair, and a composed scholar’s robe. His spirit seems to convey that “games become mirrors of power and ideals.” The townsgirl listens intently, her brush hovering above her notes.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, she organizes a local children's “kick-ball” match, drawing lines on the ground to create a miniature league table. A crowd gathers, cheering enthusiastically — but soon, wealthy merchants begin betting and arguing over the teams. The townsgirl freezes, realizing how swiftly ideals can be overshadowed by profit. A gust of wind scatters her paper notes into the air.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dusk, she kneels by the canal, collecting her scattered papers while gazing at the reflection of lanterns in the water. With a serene smile, she writes a waka on a small tanzaku slip, encapsulating her thoughts on ideals, power, and the essence of play. The frame is quiet and emotional, with the ink wash fading into soft shadows.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: Ideals flicker like lanterns, swayed by gold; beyond winning and losing, they reflect the heart.  
**Tanka (original)**: 理想とは  
金に揺らめく  
灯のごとし  
勝ち負け超え  
心を映す
