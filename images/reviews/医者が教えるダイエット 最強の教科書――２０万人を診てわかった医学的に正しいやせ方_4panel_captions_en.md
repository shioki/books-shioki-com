### Panel 1
**Scene**: The Edo-period townsgirl stands in front of a sweets stall in a bustling marketplace, gazing longingly at colorful dango and sweet sake. She appears puzzled, holding an abacus and muttering about why people gain weight even when they "eat little." The background is filled with a lively Edo street, featuring merchants and children playing.
**Dialogue**: "Why do people gain weight even when they eat so little?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a quiet study room illuminated by candlelight, the townsgirl reads a Dutch medical scroll. A wise older physician, inspired by author Makita Zenji, stands beside her. He is depicted as a calm elderly man with kind eyes, a thin beard, and scholarly posture, wearing simple robes. He gestures towards a diagram of the human body on a hanging scroll, explaining the role of sugar and the mind's "addiction." The background is filled with shelves of rangaku books and scrolls.
**Dialogue**: "Sugar can influence the mind's cravings."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: At her small dining table, the townsgirl puts her learning into practice by eating her meal in a careful order: vegetables first, then fish, and finally rice. A humorous detail shows a mischievous cat attempting to steal her rice bowl. She laughs, holding her chopsticks like a compass, remarking on the importance of order and balance for health.
**Dialogue**: "Order and balance are the true formulas of health!"

### Panel 4

**Dialogue**: (none)
