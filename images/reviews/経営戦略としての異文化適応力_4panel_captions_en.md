### Panel 1
**Scene**: In the lively streets of Edo, the townsgirl, holding her abacus, looks puzzled as she overhears merchants arguing about different business methods with foreign traders at the harbor. The background features a bustling marketplace with Dutch ships visible in the distance.
**Dialogue**: "Why does 'difference' cause confusion?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her candlelit study, the townsgirl studies Dutch books and scrolls, while two imagined mentors appear behind her in translucent ink forms. One is a serene woman and the other a calm man, both in Edo-style scholar robes, pointing at diagrams of overlapping circles and a large “OS” symbol that represents cultural systems.
**Dialogue**: "Culture is like an invisible operating system..."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next day at the market, the townsgirl draws a simple diagram in the dirt to mediate between two arguing merchants, illustrating how their “rules” can coexist like meshing gears. The merchants pause, surprised, and then laugh in agreement.
**Dialogue**: "See? We can work together!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At twilight by the riverbank, the townsgirl writes in her notebook under the fading light, holding a brush as she writes a waka poem on a vertical tanzaku strip.
**Dialogue**: "Let differences become our strength."
**Tanka (translation)**: "Knowing the other,  
Reflecting in the mirror,  
Differences become  
The strength we create together."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「異（こと）を知り  
　己の道を　映す鏡に  
　違いこそ  
　共に創る  
　力となれり」
