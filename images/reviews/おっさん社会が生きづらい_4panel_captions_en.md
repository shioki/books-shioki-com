### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a lively Edo street filled with wooden houses and lanterns, the townsgirl listens to a group of men boasting about their power and status. She tilts her head, feeling a sense of discomfort at their "strong must rule" mentality. In the background, a teahouse and townspeople glance at the scene awkwardly.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit study filled with scrolls and Dutch books, the townsgirl unrolls a manuscript titled "A Difficult Society for Old Men." The imagined author appears as a calm and intelligent teacher figure in the ink painting on the scroll, dressed in simple robes. The author's spirit softly conveys the message of "uninstalling the learned dominance within ourselves." The townsgirl listens intently, brush in hand.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: In a bustling market, the townsgirl applies her newfound knowledge by patiently listening to a merchant who is sharing his troubles, rather than interrupting him. The townsfolk around them look surprised as the tense atmosphere shifts, allowing humor and empathy to flourish in their conversation. Laughter drifts through the market stalls.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dusk, the townsgirl sits by the riverbank, her ink brush hovering over a small tanzaku. Her expression is peaceful, illuminated by a new understanding. She writes a waka that captures the essence of the book, the text beautifully rendered in Japanese vertical script on the tanzaku.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: Listening is the key that unbinds power, even for old men; it loosens me like the wind.  
**Tanka (original)**:  
聞くことは  
力をほどく鍵なりて  
おっさんなる  
我をもほどく  
風のごとくに
