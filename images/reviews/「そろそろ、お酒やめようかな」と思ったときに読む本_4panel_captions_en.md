### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a bustling Edo street at dusk, the townsgirl assists in closing a sake shop after a lively evening. She gazes at the empty sake cups with a soft sigh, pondering whether people truly find happiness in drinking. A flickering paper lantern casts shadows on her thoughtful face, while traditional wooden houses line a narrow alley in the background.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a candle-lit study, the townsgirl reads an intriguing imported medical scroll discussing "the mind and the body’s learning." The scroll features an illustration of a scholarly man resembling the author Kakibuchi Yoichi, depicted as a calm, bespectacled physician with gentle eyes and neatly tied hair. He guides her through diagrams of the brain, with the word "recovery" written in kanji, creating an atmosphere of learning from a distant mentor.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, she walks past a teahouse where others are already drinking sake. Pausing with a knowing smile, she turns toward a riverside to sketch arithmetic patterns in the sand while humming a tune. The rising sun glimmers on the water, symbolizing renewal and the "re-learning" of her own mind. A humorous twist occurs when a passing friend offers her sake, but she hands him a cup of tea instead.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: The townsgirl kneels under a willow tree, dipping her brush into ink as she writes a reflective waka poem on a small tanzaku. The scene is tranquil, with a light wind rustling her sleeve, creating a serene atmosphere.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: "In the morning without drinking, / The waves of my heart / Are calm, / I re-learn / My tomorrow."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「呑まぬ朝  
心の波も  
おだやかに  
学びなおせり  
われの明日を」
