### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a rainy backstreet of Edo at dusk, pausing at a lantern-lit bookstall. She picks up a rare foreign book and murmurs about “freedom that binds one’s heart.” The atmosphere is quiet yet tense, with reflections of lantern light shimmering on puddles as she gazes at the title “サーラレーオ” written in brushstroke-style kanji on the book’s cover.  
**Dialogue**: "Freedom that binds one's heart..."

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside her small study, she opens the book next to her Dutch dictionary and abacus. A mysterious scholar appears in the candlelight — a calm, middle-aged man with a scholarly posture, imagined as the author Shinjo Ko: short, neat hair, thoughtful eyes, modest robes, exuding quiet intelligence. He seems to step out from the book’s pages and softly explains how “freedom can become one’s own cage.” The townsgirl listens, eyes wide in realization.  
**Dialogue**: "Freedom can become one's own cage."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, she conducts a playful experiment: trying to “buy freedom” by trading sweets among neighborhood children using her own handwritten “freedom notes.” Chaos erupts as the children argue over fairness, demonstrating how greed and misunderstanding quickly disrupt harmony. She observes with a wry smile, realizing the bitter irony.  
**Dialogue**: "Is this really freedom?"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Twilight returns. The townsgirl sits by her window, brush in hand, gazing at the fading sky. On a hanging tanzaku, she writes a poignant waka that captures the essence of “Sārāreō” — the acceptance of one’s own shadow.  
**Dialogue**: (none)  
**Tanka (translation)**: "The more I run away, / The darkness gently embraces me, / Yet it knows no light, / Binding my freedom tight."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「逃げるほど  
　闇はやさしく  
　寄り添えど  
　光を知らず  
　自由を縛る」
