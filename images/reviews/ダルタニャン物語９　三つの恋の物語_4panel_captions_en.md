### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl strolls through the lively Edo marketplace at dusk, stopping in front of a teahouse where storytellers share tales of kings and love. She tilts her head in contemplation, pondering, “Can love truly survive power?” A gentle breeze rustles her kimono sleeve as she gazes at the temple bell tower, the light dimming around her.

**Dialogue**: “Can love truly survive power?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside her cozy room filled with scrolls and Dutch literature, she immerses herself in a foreign novel titled "The Three Loves." In her mind, a vision emerges: Alexandre Dumas appears as a dignified, middle-aged man with flowing dark hair, a round face, and a gentle yet confident demeanor, dressed in a 19th-century suit. Beside him stands a shadowy figure of a scholar, representing Suzuki Riki-ei, calm and contemplative, translating by the light of a lamp. The townsgirl listens in awe as Dumas’s spirit discusses how love and pride challenge the soul.

**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The following morning, she mediates a dispute between two neighbors—one proud and the other honest—drawing from the wisdom she gained from the book. With quiet strength, she bows and states that true dignity comes not from status, but from sincerity. The neighbors pause, taken aback, while a cat watches from the eaves, its tail flicking in amusement.

**Dialogue**: “True dignity lies not in position, but in sincerity.”

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, illuminated by the soft glow of a paper lantern, the townsgirl kneels and writes a waka on a small tanzaku. Her brush glides slowly, her expression a mix of serenity and contemplation.  
**Tanka (translation)**: Love and power,  
Even in ignorance,  
They draw close together,  
In the solitude of truth,  
Light shines forth.  
**Tanka (original)**: 愛と権  
しらぬまにさえ  
よりそいて  
誠の孤に  
光さすなり
