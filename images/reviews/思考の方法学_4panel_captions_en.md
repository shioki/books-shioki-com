### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a lively Edo marketplace, holding an abacus and a folded paper filled with notes. She pauses to thoughtfully observe the crowd, contemplating how people perceive the same world in different ways and how to “simplify the complex.” The background features wooden stalls, merchants shouting, and distant temple roofs.  
**Dialogue**: "People see the same world differently..."

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit terakoya (temple school), the townsgirl studies a Dutch book and scrolls of wasan (Japanese mathematics). A calm middle-aged scholar, inspired by Kurita Osamu, sits beside her. He has neatly tied hair, no spectacles, and a gentle yet analytical expression, wearing a simple kimono. He points to a diagram on the scroll, illustrating how to remove unnecessary details to reveal the core structure.  
**Dialogue**: "True thinking is the art of discarding."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: By the riverside, the townsgirl experiments with her learning by sketching a simple model of the town’s water flow on the ground to assist a boatman in navigating a shallow route. The success of her demonstration delights the onlookers. The scene is dynamic, with the wind stirring her sleeves and the reflection of her drawing in the rippling water.  
**Dialogue**: "This will help you find the way!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: At dusk, the townsgirl sits by her window, brush in hand, writing a waka on a narrow tanzaku scroll. The soft glow of candlelight illuminates her as she reflects with serene joy.  
**Tanka (translation)**: "In discarding,  
the essence of thought shines,  
a heart that clarifies  
the complexity,  
brings forth clarity."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「捨てること  
　思考の芯に  
　光あり  
　複雑（からくれなゐ）を  
　澄ます心よ」
