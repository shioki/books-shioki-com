### Panel 1
**Scene**: In a tranquil Edo alley at dawn, the townsgirl kneels by a washbasin, her hands submerged in the water, pausing to observe the ripples. She contemplates whether household chores are just labor or hold a deeper meaning. The background features hanging laundry, a neighbor sweeping, and the subtle activity of the morning market, creating an atmosphere of routine mixed with introspection.
**Dialogue**: "Are household chores just labor, or is there something more?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a modest study filled with scrolls and Dutch books, the townsgirl reads an essay by a visiting scholar resembling Naokōra Yamazaki. The scholar, a calm and intelligent woman in her forties with short dark hair, wears a simple yet elegant kimono. She sits at a low desk, writing gracefully and softly explaining that “housework is thinking.” The townsgirl listens with wide eyes, absorbing the realization.
**Dialogue**: "Housework is thinking."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl puts the lesson into practice, sweeping fallen sakura petals outside her home while reflecting on the balance between duty and contemplation. A neighbor boy offers to help, and she smiles as she hands him the broom. The dynamic brushstrokes depict swirling petals like thoughts, symbolizing connection and shared rhythm. A humorous moment occurs as the boy accidentally sweeps dust onto her face, causing both to laugh.
**Dialogue**: "Let’s do this together!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the evening, a single candle illuminates the room. The townsgirl sits by the open shōji with a brush in hand, gazing at the moon's reflection in the washbasin. On a small tanzaku paper, she writes a waka that encapsulates the essence of the book — that everyday labor can be a form of prayer, reflection, and connection.
**Tanka (translation)**: "As I move my hands, I also refine my heart; within the days, loving the mundane, I become human."
**Tanka (original)**: 「手を動かし  
　こころも磨く  
　日々の中  
　無駄を愛して  
　人となりゆく」
