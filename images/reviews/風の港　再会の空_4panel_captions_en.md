### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl stands on a breezy pier in Edo, watching a trading ship set sail for Nagasaki. The wind plays with her hair as she reflects on how every journey feels like both a goodbye and a new beginning. The lively harbor behind her captures the essence of departure and reunion.  
**Dialogue**: "Every voyage feels like a farewell and a beginning."

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her cozy study, the girl studies Dutch and Japanese scrolls about winds, stars, and navigation. A gentle, older woman resembling the author Murayama Saki appears as a guiding spirit from the pages, radiating warmth and kindness in her simple kimono.  
**Dialogue**: "Every loss is just a harbor before the next voyage."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The girl assists a fisherman in repairing his torn sail, utilizing the arithmetic and knowledge of wind she has learned. The newly mended sail catches the wind beautifully, and they both laugh as it billows, symbolizing rebirth and gentle forgiveness.  
**Dialogue**: "Look how it catches the breeze!"

### Panel 4
**Scene**: As evening descends, the girl sits on the pier with a brush in hand, composing a tanka on a small scroll that flutters in the sea breeze. The moonlight and the gentle ripples of the water create a serene atmosphere.  
**Dialogue**: (Writing) "風の港 別れも再会も ひとつ空 赦して進む 旅のつづきに"  
**Tanka (translation)**: "The harbor of winds, both farewells and reunions, one sky above, forgiving as we move forward, continuing the journey."  
**Tanka (original)**: 「風の港  
　別れも再会も  
　ひとつ空  
　赦して進む  
　旅のつづきに」
