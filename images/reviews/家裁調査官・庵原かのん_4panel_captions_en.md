### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through an Edo street at dusk, overhearing neighbors quarreling—an old woman scolding a boy who stole sweets. She pauses, puzzled by the anger and sadness mingled together, gazing at the boy’s downcast face. The background shows narrow alleys, hanging lanterns, and wooden houses.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: Inside a dimly lit terakoya, the townsgirl opens a Dutch book beside a scroll of legal codes. On the scroll’s portrait appears a calm, middle-aged woman with gentle eyes and composed dignity—her hair neatly tied back, simple kimono, reflecting the imagined presence of author Asa Nonami as an Edo-era scholar. The portrait seems to whisper: “To understand is not to judge.” The townsgirl listens deeply, brush poised over her notes.
**Dialogue**: “To understand is not to judge.”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next day, she kneels beside a crying child who had broken another’s toy. Instead of scolding, she quietly offers a sweet dumpling. The child hesitates, then smiles faintly. The wind blows cherry petals through the alley, symbolizing a small act of understanding bridging hearts.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4
**Scene**: That night, by lamplight, the townsgirl writes a waka on a small tanzaku slip, her expression serene yet thoughtful. The poem is written vertically in delicate brushstrokes.
**Dialogue**: None  
**Tanka (translation)**: To listen is not to judge; it is to light a lamp in the darkness of solitude.  
**Tanka (original)**: 聞くことは  
裁くにあらず  
人の音  
孤独の闇に  
灯すことなり
