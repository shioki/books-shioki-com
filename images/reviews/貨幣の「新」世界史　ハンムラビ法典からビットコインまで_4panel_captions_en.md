### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through a lively Edo marketplace, observing merchants exchanging coins and goods. She stops to watch a money changer counting koban, pondering, “What truly gives silver its worth?” The background features an Edo street adorned with paper lanterns and a distant temple bell, capturing the essence of daily life and quiet wonder.
**Dialogue**: “What truly gives silver its worth?”

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In a dimly lit study filled with scrolls and imported books, the townsgirl studies a thick Dutch-bound book titled “The World History of Currency.” Beside her, the spirit-like figures of the co-authors appear: a calm, intellectual woman in her forties with straight black hair and gentle eyes, and a thoughtful man of Indian descent, both explaining how “money mirrors human trust” through gestures and expressions.

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl organizes a neighborhood “trust market” where villagers barter items and favors without using coins—trading rice for sewing and tea for help with calculations. Initially, there is confusion, but laughter erupts when a child offers a handmade toy as “interest.” The scene is dynamic, filled with motion and joy, with ink splashes symbolizing the invisible flow of trust.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]

### Panel 4
**Scene**: In the evening, the townsgirl sits by a lantern with a brush in hand, gazing at the moon reflected in a coin resting on her palm. She writes a tanka on a narrow strip of paper, her expression soft and contemplative.
**Dialogue**: [No dialogue]
**Tanka (translation)**: The thread of trust,  
Silver and paper are mere shadows,  
Hearts intertwine,  
Value is born anew,  
Like the moon in the night sky.
