## 🎯 Core of the Book
『大江健三郎全小説　第３巻』 delves into themes such as "self and authority," "individual and state," and "identity and alienation" in post-war Japan through extreme psychological portrayals. In the first half, a young man trapped in self-loathing and loneliness seeks salvation by identifying himself with the absolute figure of the Emperor, descending into a state of intoxication with violence and faith.

In the latter half, a young man from the Gilyak people comes to accept his origins and reaches self-affirmation beyond discrimination and alienation. Through these contrasting narratives, Oe presents a fundamental question about how a self dominated by "the gaze of the other" can move towards regeneration.

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **The gaze of the other defines the self**  
  The protagonist's self-loathing stems from the gaze of others. His violent impulses reflect a desire to escape this gaze, which can be interpreted as a pioneering depiction of contemporary SNS-like self-awareness.

- **The fusion of faith and sexual impulse**  
  The depiction of the Emperor's worship intertwined with sexual pleasure reveals how faith in authority intertwines with personal desires. Oe uncovers the fundamental impulses of humanity lurking behind religious ecstasy.

- **Violence is a reflection of powerlessness**  
  A sense of powerlessness that cannot change reality breeds fantasies of violence. Oe portrays violence not merely as destruction but as a distorted means of affirming one's existence.

- **"Peace" is experienced as nihilism**  
  The depiction of post-war peace as a loss of meaning is central to Oe's literature. The paradox that a generation that has lost the sense of life can only find themselves in destruction symbolizes the spiritual history of post-war Japan.

- **A story of ethnic awakening and regeneration**  
  The Gilyak youth's acceptance of his origins contrasts sharply with the self-denying protagonist of the first half. Here, Oe suggests the possibility of new self-formation that transcends discrimination and alienation.

---

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
The themes of "identification with authority" and "regeneration of ethnic identity" resonate sharply in contemporary society. In 2020s Japan, as the country approaches the 80th anniversary of the post-war era, issues of "inheritance of memory" and "the relationship between state and individual" are being revisited, and Oe's portrayal of the structures of authority and violence echoes the resurgence of nationalism and social division.

Moreover, the "acceptance of origins" seen in the Gilyak youth's story connects with contemporary themes such as the reevaluation of disabilities, minorities, and local cultures. Oe's ideas of "coexistence" and "regeneration through relationships with others" remain relevant in today's discussions surrounding care ethics and multicultural coexistence.

## 🚀 Practical Suggestions
1. **Practice freeing oneself from the gaze of others**  
   Measuring oneself by internal standards rather than relying on the gaze of others is the first step towards mental freedom.

2. **Do not use faith or ideals as a means of escape**  
   The attitude of trying to maintain oneself by depending on an absolute being is dangerous. If one holds ideals, it is necessary to connect them with personal responsibility.

3. **Transform self-loathing into creative energy**  
   Negative imagination can work for both destruction and creation. By expressing and acting on inner negation, one can find opportunities for regeneration.

4. **Accept one's origins and background**  
   Accepting one's roots and weaknesses without denial forms the foundation for building true relationships with others.

5. **Find "actions" that replace violence and extreme ideologies**  
   Overcoming feelings of powerlessness through small, realistic actions is the most reliable way to restore a sense of self-efficacy.

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
『大江健三郎全小説　第３巻』 is a book that vividly depicts the spiritual desolation and potential for regeneration in post-war Japan through an intensely internalized narrative. Themes of violence, faith, sexuality, and identity intertwine in complex layers, challenging readers with fundamental questions about "what is the self" and "how to relate to others."

This volume embodies the core structure of Oe's literature, which revolves around "the rupture and regeneration with others," and offers rich insights into contemporary issues of loneliness and social pressure. It provides a thought-provoking literary experience that simultaneously delivers pain and awakening to its readers.
