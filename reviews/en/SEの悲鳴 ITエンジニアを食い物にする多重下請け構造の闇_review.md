## 🎯 The Core of This Book
This book vividly portrays the deep-rooted pathology of the "multi-layer subcontracting structure" that plagues the Japanese IT industry. The author, 田中宏明, draws on his experience as a field engineer to expose the extraction practices of SIers and SES companies, making the structural problems within the industry visible.

Moreover, the book does not merely end with criticism; it offers practical value by suggesting concrete directions for reform, such as improving IT literacy among end-user companies, fostering independence in SES companies, and enhancing transaction transparency. While depicting the new roles of engineers in the AI era, it carries a message that encourages "transformation from the ground up."

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **Disregarding IT Diminishes Corporate Competitiveness**  
  As long as management continues to view IT as a cost center, Japanese companies will lag behind in the wave of digital transformation (DX). The author insists on the need to shift towards "proactive IT" and integrate IT strategy into the core of management.

- **The Multi-layer Subcontracting Structure Hinders Talent Development**  
  Engineers at the bottom-tier SES companies are unable to express their creativity and are bogged down by simple tasks. The author warns that this stifles talent growth and diminishes the overall technical capabilities of the industry.

- **The Essence of SES Contracts Lies in "Operations"**  
  Rather than denying the system itself, there is a need for operations that ensure transparency and fairness. The author argues that re-examining the nature of contracts is the first step toward structural reform.

- **Transaction Transparency is Key to Revival**  
  By utilizing technologies like blockchain, the previously hidden multi-layer subcontracting structure can be visualized. It is crucial to adopt the mindset of using technology as a "weapon for structural reform."

- **Redefining the Role of Engineers in the AI Era**  
  In an era where AI replaces routine tasks, human value shifts towards "problem-solving ability" and "customer understanding." The author suggests that engineers should evolve into more creative roles.

---

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
In the mid-2020s, the Japanese IT industry faces a serious talent shortage and the "blackening" of the SES structure has become a social issue. Terms like "#SES地獄" are spreading on social media, highlighting the noticeable departure of young engineers from the industry. This reality substantiates the "screams of engineers" depicted in this book.

Additionally, there are policy movements, such as the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry and the Fair Trade Commission strengthening guidelines to rectify multi-layer subcontracting. As advancements in AI and automation render SES-dependent development models outdated, the proposals in this book are highly insightful for contemplating the "next-generation IT industry structure."

---

## 🚀 Practical Suggestions
1. **Companies Should Enhance IT Literacy and Promote In-house Development**  
   By establishing CIO positions and strengthening internal IT departments, companies can reduce dependence on SIers and enable appropriate ordering decisions. This is the most realistic first step to changing the industry structure.

2. **SES Companies Should Establish Independence with Technical Expertise**  
   Rather than merely providing personnel, they should position themselves as proposal-based technical partners. To gain negotiating power, building expertise and trust is essential.

3. **Promote Transaction Transparency Through Technology**  
   By utilizing blockchain and other technologies, clarifying the flow of contracts, labor, and payments can prevent unjust subcontracting. Transparency breeds trust.

4. **Encourage the Hiring and Reskilling of Talent from Different Industries**  
   Welcoming individuals with diverse backgrounds fosters flexible thinking. In the AI era, not only technical skills but also "human insight" will hold value.

5. **Strengthen Cross-industry Discussions and Collaboration**  
   SES companies, SIers, and end-users should share challenges beyond their boundaries and work towards forming common rules, supporting the sustainability of structural reform.

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
『SEの悲鳴』 is not just a critique of the industry; it is a proposal for structural reform based on the real voices from the field. The author's writing oscillates between anger and hope, prompting readers to question "Where do I fit within this structure?"

This book is essential reading not only for those in the IT industry but also for corporate executives and policymakers on the ordering side. As a "declaration of reform from the ground up" to restore the dignity of engineers, this book will serve as a starting point for contemplating the future of Japan's IT industry.
