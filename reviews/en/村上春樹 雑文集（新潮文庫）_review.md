**Dialogue**: (Thinking) “To see clearly… perhaps one must wait before judging.”

From the ink mist, a calm apparition of a middle-aged man appears — gentle-eyed, serene, and quietly wise. He tells her that a writer observes much but judges little. She listens, brush poised in midair.  
**Dialogue**: Apparition: “A writer observes much, judges little.”  
Townsgirl: “To observe… without deciding…”

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The next morning, she strolls through the lively marketplace — merchants bargaining, children laughing, a cat stealing a fish. She simply watches and takes notes, refraining from judgment. When a gust of wind scatters her papers, she laughs, realizing the beauty of not knowing. Her hair and sleeves flow with the wind’s motion.  
**Dialogue**: (Laughing) “So even the wind teaches me!”

**Scene**: That night, by candlelight, she writes a waka on a slender tanzaku strip. The room is silent except for the sound of her brush. Her expression is serene — enlightened yet humble.

## 🎯 Core of the Book
*村上春樹 雑文集* is a collection of essays that distills Haruki Murakami’s reflections on the ethics of being a writer, the mission of literature, and the dignity of human beings. Murakami defines a novelist as “a person who observes much and judges little,” arguing that the essence of literature lies in truthful depiction born from observation.  

For him, fiction is not a means of escaping reality but a kind of “white magic” that illuminates it—a creative act that protects human dignity through storytelling. Literature, he suggests, may lack immediacy, but it is a “continuous endeavor” that sustains human ethics and freedom over time.  

As symbolized by the “wall and the egg” metaphor from his Jerusalem Prize speech, Murakami consistently stands on the side of the individual soul, resisting the violence of systems. Through his three creative principles—observation, hypothesis, and continuity—he quietly questions the role literature should play in society and in the human condition.  

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **The balance between observation and judgment is the heart of creation**  
  Murakami asserts that “observation is the only way to approach truth.” Rushing to judgment narrows the possibilities of a story; only through sustained observation can one portray the true complexity of reality.

- **Fiction as a device for understanding reality**  
  Fiction is not escapism but a “hypothetical experimental apparatus” that sheds light on reality. Through fiction, readers can reconstruct their relationship with society and cultivate ethical imagination.

- **The value of literature lies in its continuity**  
  Murakami notes that literature has never caused war or violence, viewing its power as a “long-term ethic that safeguards human dignity.” This quiet continuity is, for him, the reason literature continues to matter in society.

- **The ethical stance of “the wall and the egg”**  
  Murakami declares that he always stands with the fragile individual—the “egg.” This represents his moral commitment as a writer to protect the human soul against the might of systems and power.

- **The honesty of sharing “the absence of conclusions”**  
  Faced with the complexity of reality, Murakami values the act of “sharing confusion” rather than offering easy answers. Literature, he suggests, is a space where we can live through that confusion together.

## 🔍 Relevance in the Modern Context
In today’s world—marked by post-pandemic isolation and a deepening mental health crisis—Murakami’s “wall and egg” metaphor resonates as a symbol of the loneliness of individuals surrounded by systems and social media, and their struggle to reclaim autonomy.  

His emphasis on “observation and the absence of conclusions” reminds us of the importance of resisting hasty judgment in an age of information overload. Murakami’s literary stance is increasingly seen as a form of human intelligence that resists the automation of judgment in the AI era.  

Moreover, his sensitivity to music and translation as forms of cross-cultural understanding gains new significance in a globalized, borderless world. His essays offer guidance for individuals seeking to rediscover “shared narratives” in an age of fragmentation.  

---

## 🚀 Practical Applications
1. **Deepen observation before making judgments**  
   Avoid snap decisions in daily life; by observing carefully, you gain a more layered understanding. This intellectual attitude benefits not only creative work but also relationships and professional life.  

2. **Think in terms of accumulating hypotheses**  
   Before drawing conclusions, form hypotheses and test them. Murakami’s idea of “stacking hypotheses” offers a practical method for cultivating creative thinking.  

3. **Use fiction to relativize the self**  
   Engaging with diverse stories helps you see your own values and positions in perspective, fostering flexibility of thought. Fiction serves as a mirror for understanding reality.  

4. **Engage in dialogue that embraces “the absence of conclusions”**  
   Instead of rushing to answers, share the discomfort of uncertainty. This is the first step toward sincere communication and the foundation of mutual understanding.  

5. **Cultivate the sensibility to translate culture**  
   Reinterpreting music and literature in your own words strengthens your ability to understand others’ expressions—an essential exercise in creative comprehension.  

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
*村上春樹 雑文集* is a quiet yet powerful work of thought that directly confronts the ethics of authorship and the mission of literature. While exploring the inner ethics of the novelist, it also presents a universal philosophy for protecting “the dignity of the individual” in contemporary society.  

This book is not only for aspiring writers but for all readers who wish to reclaim the power of observation in an age of information overload. After reading, one’s gaze upon the world feels calmer, and a space opens up for thought that values understanding over judgment.
