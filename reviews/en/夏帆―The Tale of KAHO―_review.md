## 🎯 Core of the Book
This book tells the story of Kaho, who is labeled "ugly" by others, as she redefines her wavering self-image and acquires her own "face." This face is not her innate appearance, but rather the very history of her life shaped by hopes, sorrows, choices, and failures.

The otherworldly developments featuring characters like an anteater, a jaguar, a queen termite, and a man claiming to be a guardian angel intentionally blur the boundaries between good and evil, truth and falsehood. As Kaho confronts her conflicts and losses with her mother, she stands at the center of her own story, transforming her wounds into meaning through creation.

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **A person's worth is not determined by external evaluations imposed by others**  
  The insults from Sahara are words thrown to manipulate Kaho's self-perception. In contrast, this book illustrates that a person's true face is born not from the superiority or inferiority of appearance, but from the accumulation of experiences, emotions, and choices.

- **Self-acceptance is not the disappearance of anxiety, but a determination to move forward with it**  
  Kaho's anxiety about her appearance does not fully vanish by the end of the story. Nevertheless, her choice to "live as myself" redefines self-acceptance not as a completed state, but as an ongoing journey of exploration.

- **Curiosity is both the gateway to growth and a manipulable weakness**  
  Kaho's interest in the unknown leads her to new worlds and creations, while also drawing her closer to the traps set by Sahara. This insight suggests that one should not deny the impulse to know, but also maintain vigilance to discern the intentions of those providing information.

- **Salvation is different from forcing oneself to love another**  
  Kaho acknowledges the possibility of not being able to love her mother, yet still chooses to seek rescue and reconciliation. Rather than faking emotions, taking necessary actions becomes a catalyst for liberating oneself from the prison of the past.

- **Creation is a technique for reliving chaotic experiences**  
  "Searching for a face" and "Misora's story" are not escapism, but devices through which Kaho rearranges her experiences and understands their meanings. The act of storytelling allows her to embrace loss and fear without erasing them, empowering her to return to life.

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
The theme of external evaluation in this book resonates sharply in the age of social media, where images and short phrases spread instantly, making it easy to confuse evaluations of appearance with a person's worth. Kaho's reaction to Sahara's words reflects a modern danger: even baseless attacks, if repeated, can erode one's self-image.

Moreover, the book's focus on a female protagonist and the complexities of mother-daughter relationships, including distance, love-hate dynamics, and the challenges of reconciliation, is significant. However, it should be read not merely as a depiction of social issues, but as literature questioning how to reclaim subjectivity away from others' measuring sticks through otherworldly experiences and creation.

## 🚀 Practical Suggestions
1. **Separate others' evaluations into facts and interpretations**  
   When receiving hurtful words, it helps to write down verifiable facts, the other person's interpretations, presumed intentions, and your own emotions separately. This aids in distinguishing truth from manipulation without directly incorporating evaluations into your self-image.

2. **Maintain small orders in life amidst anxiety**  
   Choose one physical task, such as cleaning, cooking, walking, or stretching. While this may not directly solve problems, it prevents anxiety from overwhelming your thoughts and creates space for necessary judgments.

3. **Do not draw conclusions based on a single explanation**  
   When thoughts are cycling in the same place, it is beneficial to seek other sources of information or trusted third parties. Gaining multiple perspectives makes it easier to distinguish confirmed information from assumptions about yourself and others.

4. **Transform complex experiences into stories or art**  
   Utilizing accessible formats like diaries, short stories, drawings, or dialogue-style notes to externalize experiences can be effective. Creation generates distance from events, allowing you to find meaning and emotional structures amidst chaos.

5. **In repairing relationships, clarify actions rather than feelings**  
   There is no need to force yourself to like someone. By specifying what you want to confirm, what you wish to reject, and what you are willing to take on, you can step into a responsible relationship without faking emotions.

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
The significance of this book lies in its portrayal of self-affirmation not as simplistic encouragement, but as a nuanced exploration of others' malice, mother-daughter conflicts, the absurdity of imposed roles, and the void following loss. The intertwining of reality and the otherworldly highlights the loneliness and strength of making one's own judgments without oversimplifying good and evil.

This work particularly resonates with those who have experienced hurt from evaluations of appearance, those struggling with familial distances, and those interested in how creation can heal. The conclusion, where Kaho returns to her work desk after her tears have dried, quietly teaches that regeneration is not forgetting, but continuing daily life while holding onto memories.
