## 🎯 The Core of This Book
This book is an intellectual document that fundamentally questions the relationship between capitalism and humanity through the philosophical trajectory of economist 宇沢弘文.  
Amid the post-war economic currents that shifted from Keynesianism to neoliberalism, 宇沢 constructed a unique theory of "social common capital" and attempted a theoretical counterattack against market fundamentalism.  

His aim was to transition from an economics that prioritizes efficiency and growth to one centered on human dignity and ethics, termed "economics for humans."  
His ideological struggle, accompanied by academic solitude, resonated deeply with the issues of inequality, environment, and publicness that modern society faces.  

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **Economics as a Thought Device that Guides Society**  
  Economics is not merely a tool for mathematical analysis but a philosophical foundation that determines the direction of society. 宇沢 was aware of this power and questioned the ethical responsibilities of economics.

- **"Social Common Capital" as a Theoretical Counterattack to Neoliberalism**  
  The concept proposed by 宇沢 aims to separate education, healthcare, and the environment from the market, framing them as shared societal assets. This signifies a fundamental redesign of capitalism.

- **Fusion of Theory and Reality**  
  宇沢's theories deepened as they connected with real-world issues such as pollution and environmental destruction. They embody an economics that responds to the pains of society rather than relying on abstract models.

- **A Wake-Up Call Against Ethics-less Economics**  
  宇沢 criticized how economics became a device to justify financialization and the widening gap, warning that unless humanity is restored, capitalism will self-destruct.

- **A Struggle as a Solitary Intellectual**  
  Despite being a global theorist, 宇沢's isolation in Japan symbolizes the difficulty of balancing academic independence with social responsibility.

---

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
In modern Japan, issues like "new capitalism," correcting inequality, and reevaluating publicness have emerged as policy challenges. The "social common capital" proposed by 宇沢 provides a theoretical foundation for these trends.  

With pressing challenges such as climate change, aging social infrastructure, and disparities in education and healthcare that cannot be solved by the market alone, 宇沢's thoughts are regaining practical significance.  
His theories are being referenced across various fields, including policy-making, civic movements, and education, as a design philosophy for a "sustainable welfare society" that transcends economic growth.  

---

## 🚀 Suggestions for Practice
1. **Be Aware of Balancing Economic Rationality and Ethics**  
   It is important to consider not only efficiency but also human and social impacts in daily decision-making and work judgments.

2. **Take Action to Protect Social Common Capital**  
   Recognize areas such as education, healthcare, and the environment as "shared assets" and strive to ensure that individual actions and corporate activities do not undermine them.

3. **Open Specialized Knowledge to Society**  
   Overcome the "closed academia" criticized by 宇沢 and make efforts to return knowledge to society. Restoring the public nature of knowledge through education, dialogue, and dissemination is key.

4. **Evaluate Policies from an Ethical Perspective**  
   Scrutinize policies and reforms not only for "efficiency" and "growth" but also from the perspectives of "human dignity" and "social justice," which will be the first step in practicing 宇沢's thoughts.

5. **Rediscover Common Capital in Local Communities**  
   Participate in activities that maintain and regenerate local social capital, such as public transportation, libraries, and natural environments, to concretely embody 宇沢's ideals.  

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
『資本主義と闘った男』 is a grand intellectual challenge that brings economics back from a mere technical discipline to a "human science."  
Through the thoughts of 宇沢弘文, readers will confront the ethical questions lurking at the core of capitalism.  

This book serves as a profound stimulus for not only those interested in economics and social thought but also for anyone wishing to fundamentally reconsider the nature of modern society.  
It is a guiding light for reconstructing an "economy for humans" beyond the era of growth and efficiency.
