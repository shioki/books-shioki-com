## 🎯 Core of the Book
*メルカリで知らん子の絵を買う* is an essay collection that redefines concepts often dismissed as “useless,” “unnecessary,” or “wasteful” as sources of creativity and freedom. Author 藤原麻里菜 observes her own depression, loneliness, and sense of alienation from society through strange and irrational actions, discovering vitality within them.  

Her practice serves as a quiet rebellion against a society obsessed with efficiency and productivity, an attempt to reclaim human creativity through the act of “making useless things.” The very act of buying “a random kid’s drawing on Mercari” generates serendipitous connections with others and prompts reflection on the meaning of existence.  

This book is a modern philosophical work that humorously and experimentally illustrates the paradox that life’s richness resides precisely in the things that seem trivial.  

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **“Uselessness” is the mother of creativity**  
  Fujihara argues that having the courage to do something “useless” creates future value. In a society that overemphasizes efficiency, it is the “margin” — the unproductive space — that nurtures creativity and helps us reclaim our humanity.  

- **Freedom exists within the irrational**  
  Acts such as “buying children’s clothes” or “not using an umbrella” symbolize the freedom that comes from stepping outside social norms of “rightness.” By following emotion and impulse rather than logic, self-liberation begins.  

- **Even in depression, excitement can be a source of life**  
  Even in despair, thinking about “pointless things” can stir the heart again. Fujihara doesn’t try to overcome depression but instead finds a sense of curiosity and vitality within it.  

- **Love what’s unnecessary, and have the courage to let it go**  
  Her humorous comparison of creative works to “poop” reflects a refreshing detachment. Embracing and then releasing what’s unnecessary becomes a process of renewal.  

- **Loneliness is a friend of creativity**  
  Embracing boredom and solitude as forms of play is a sign of independence. By transforming loneliness into a kind of “pastime,” one can rediscover oneself.  

---

## 🔍 Connection to Contemporary Context
Between 2024 and 2025, as ideas like “making useless things” and “slow living” gain attention, this book stands at the heart of that movement. In a society exhausted by efficiency, affirming unproductive activities directly relates to restoring mental health and creativity.  

Moreover, with the expansion of C2C markets like Mercari, anonymous connections with “unknown others” are forming new types of human relationships. Fujihara’s act of “buying a random kid’s drawing” symbolizes how we coexist with loneliness in the digital age.  

In publishing and creative fields, such “inefficient creativity” is increasingly valued as a driving force for diversity, and this book can be seen as one of its philosophical pillars.

## 🚀 Suggestions for Practice
1. **Deliberately do something useless**  
   Engage in irrational acts without seeking meaning or purpose. For example, “read a book while standing on one leg.” Such exercises help break mental boundaries and train creativity.  

2. **Reexamine and gratefully let go of what’s unnecessary**  
   Before discarding something, reflect on *why* it feels unnecessary. This process helps organize your relationship with the past. Letting go becomes preparation for new creation.  

3. **Find excitement in “pointless things”**  
   In gloomy days, allow yourself to be moved by things that seem meaningless. Small sparks of curiosity can reignite stagnant emotions.  

4. **Practice freedom from others’ eyes**  
   Wear clothes that don’t suit you, try bold makeup — express yourself without worrying about judgment. Stepping away from social “correctness” nurtures self-acceptance.  

5. **Turn boredom into creative time**  
   Don’t fear boredom. Enjoy unproductive moments — playing in a sandbox, gazing at the sky. By linking solitude with play, everyday life becomes richer.  

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
*メルカリで知らん子の絵を買う* is a book of solace for those weary of a society obsessed with efficiency. Blending humor and philosophy, 藤原麻里菜 reminds readers of the courage to do things that “serve no purpose.”  

This is a must-read for anyone seeking to reclaim creativity, struggling with loneliness, or losing sight of life’s meaning. Through it, readers can rediscover the freedom and hope hidden within “uselessness.”
