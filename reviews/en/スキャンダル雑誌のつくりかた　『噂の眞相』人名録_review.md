## 🎯 Core of the Book
This book questions the conditions of journalism that instills fear in the powerful, through the trajectory of 『噂の眞相』 and its editor, 岡留安則. It views the approach of stepping into the taboos of politics, bureaucracy, corporations, prosecution, and existing media not merely as voyeurism but as a practice of power surveillance.

At the same time, it does not romanticize the magazine's vulgarity, sexual expressions, or management weaknesses. By depicting the resolve to face lawsuits and assaults, the freedom of the editorial organization, and the tension of being chosen by readers, it reexamines the value of a contradictory and heretical media.

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **Freedom of the press is measured by practice**  
  Even with institutional freedom, if one cannot delve into the truths that the powerful wish to hide, it is hard to call it genuine freedom. This book uses the functionality of power criticism, rather than the scale of the medium, as the benchmark.

- **Proximity to subjects can lead to self-censorship**  
  While press clubs facilitate access to information, they can also lead to silence or insufficient scrutiny to maintain relations with authorities. Examples of independent magazines or local papers breaking stagnation highlight the importance of diverse information sources.

- **Courage is needed to publish scoops**  
  Merely grasping the facts does not constitute reporting; one must also be prepared to face the dangers of lawsuits, intimidation, and assaults when deciding to publish. 岡留's stance reflects the editor's obsession with transforming even victimization into transparency.

- **Formal neutrality can weaken scrutiny**  
  What this book criticizes is not verification or fairness, but the dual reporting that obscures responsibility. It argues that rather than pretending to lack a stance, one should be aware of their own judgments while deeply pursuing the facts.

---

## 🔍 Connection to Contemporary Context
Scandal reporting has expanded from print magazines to web articles, videos, and social media. While independent media continue to play a role in making visible the scandals that existing media find difficult to handle, the risk of rapid dissemination of personal exposures and unverified information through snippets and secondary sharing has also increased.

In this environment, not only the anti-establishment nature embodied by 『噂の眞相』 but also the protection of sources, verification of evidence, and editorial responsibility become even more crucial. This book does not unconditionally praise exposure but provides a perspective to consider scandal media as a battleground where public interest and vulgarity, critique and commercialism contend.

---

## 🚀 Practical Suggestions
1. **Cross-check multiple media**  
   For issues involving the government or large corporations, check not only national newspapers and television but also local papers, magazines, and independent media. Combining media that do not rely on the same information sources makes it easier to discover reporting taboos and oversights.

2. **Investigate the order of reporting**  
   Confirm which media first raised questions and who followed later. By examining the timeline of reporting, one can discern the differences between original reporting and follow-up coverage, as well as highlight initial silences.

3. **Look for depth of verification over dual reporting**  
   Use criteria such as the presentation of primary materials, specificity of questions, follow-up questions to counterarguments, and clarification of unresolved points. A stance that does not equate fairness with merely presenting balanced opinions is necessary.

4. **Delay sharing provocative information**  
   Before disseminating, investigate the original article, issuing entity, source of videos, and corroborating reports from other media. In an era where generative AI and snippets are prevalent, prioritizing verifiability over speed is effective.

5. **Protect dissenting voices within critiques**  
   Organizations that advocate anti-establishment views must not dismiss internal criticism or issues of expression through personal attacks. A system that allows for the examination of one’s blind spots can prevent the authority of critique from becoming dogmatic.

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
The significance of this book lies in its refusal to simplify 『噂の眞相』 as a heroic anti-establishment magazine, instead portraying its courage, free organizational culture, vulgarity, and male-centric blind spots as an integrated whole. Through the format of a directory, it conveys that the ideology of a medium is shaped not only by institutions but also by the character, resolve, and relationships of editors and reporters.

This book is suitable not only for readers interested in journalism, publishing, and organizational management but also for those who do not wish to passively consume daily news. After reading, one is left questioning not only "what was reported" but also "who remained silent and who took risks to publish."
