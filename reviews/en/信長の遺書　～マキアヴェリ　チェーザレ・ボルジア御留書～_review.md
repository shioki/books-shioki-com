## 🎯 The Core of This Book
This book is a historical philosophical novel that depicts the ideological interplay between Oda Nobunaga, the warlord of the Sengoku period, the giant of Western political thought Machiavelli, and the Jesuit Varignano. It illuminates Nobunaga's governance from the perspective of "Machiavellianism," questioning "What is governance?" and "What is humanity?" through the clashes of religion, power, art, and civilization.

The narrative unfolds as a philosophical drama attempting to merge Western realism with Japanese views of power, symbolized by the scene where Nobunaga reads "The Prince" and questions, "What makes a ruler a ruler?" Nobunaga's vision of "the world = the earth" aspires to create a universal order that transcends mere unification of the realm.

The cold realism of treating religion as a tool of politics, the aesthetics of using art as a symbol of power, and the state ideology that integrates destruction and rebirth elevate Nobunaga's character to a new dimension.

## 💡 Key Insights
- **Cold Realism of Treating Religion as a Political Tool**  
  Nobunaga perceives Christianity not as faith but as a means of governance, stating, "It is most convenient to come to terms with Deus." His approach to utilizing religion to establish order demonstrates the cold rationality of a ruler who prioritizes reality over ideals.

- **Eastern Rebirth of Western Political Thought**  
  The parallel drawn by Varignano between Nobunaga and Cesare Borgia represents an attempt to transplant Machiavellian thought into a Japanese context. This reveals the universal structures of power that transcend Eastern and Western cultures.

- **Dialectics of Destruction and Rebirth**  
  Nobunaga's assertion that "to create a new world, everything that has come before must be destroyed" embodies the historical dynamism of destruction for the sake of creation. The idea that reform is a continuous cycle of destruction and reconstruction is a prevailing theme.

- **Visualization of Power through Art**  
  Artistic expressions such as Azuchi Castle and the wall paintings of Eitoku are depicted as devices carrying political messages. Nobunaga visualizes "the world = the universe" through architecture and art, embedding the ideals of governance into space.

- **The Solitude and Nihilism of the Ruler**  
  In a scene where Nobunaga states, "The place after the banquet is nothing but emptiness," the solitude of the ruler is encapsulated. The emptiness that remains after success is the fateful shadow of power.

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
The tendency to seek "strong leadership" and "principles of organizational reform" from history continues in modern society. Nobunaga's rationalism and Machiavellian realism are being re-evaluated as theories of leadership in times of rapid change.

On the other hand, in a contemporary context where the dangers of power concentration and charisma dependence are becoming apparent, the book's portrayal of "the solitude of the ruler" and "the conflict between ethics and desire" serves as a mirror reflecting the challenges of democracy and organizational governance.

Moreover, Nobunaga's method of visualizing ideals through art and design resonates with modern brand strategies and national image formation. This work prompts a reconsideration of the relationship between power and representation throughout history.

## 🚀 Practical Suggestions
1. **Actively Incorporate Ideas from Different Cultures**  
   Like Nobunaga and Varignano, the attitude of learning from and merging knowledge of different civilizations can be a source of creative inspiration. Learning beyond one’s own boundaries opens new perspectives.

2. **Balance Ideals and Reality**  
   Learning from Machiavelli's realism, understanding not only ideals but also dynamics is essential for driving organizations and society.

3. **Embrace Destruction and Aim for Reconstruction**  
   Nobunaga's thought that "to create a new world requires the courage to destroy" illustrates the resolve of a leader in times of change. It is necessary to adopt an attitude that does not fear reconstruction, free from existing frameworks.

4. **Strategically Use Symbolic Expressions**  
   Like Azuchi Castle, materializing ideals through space and design is a strategy that modern leaders can also apply. One should hone the ability to convey messages through expressions beyond words.

5. **Set Aside Time to Confront Solitude**  
   Even amidst power and success, securing time for introspection supports a sustainable vision. Nobunaga's awareness of emptiness symbolizes the self-reflection necessary for leaders.

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
『信長の遺書』 is an intellectual adventure that transcends the boundaries of historical fiction, bridging Eastern and Western political philosophy. Its uniqueness lies in portraying Nobunaga not merely as a hero but as a thinker standing at the intersection of power, religion, and art.

For modern readers interested in leadership and thought, this book serves as an opportunity to reconsider the universal theme of "destruction for the sake of creation." Through the lens of the Sengoku period, it illuminates contemporary issues of power and ethics.
