## 🎯 The Core of This Book
At the heart of this book is a clear assertion: "It is carbohydrates, not calories, that determine health." Yoshinori Makita points out that carbohydrate intake disrupts blood sugar levels, leading to a chain of obesity, aging, and disease.  
He scientifically elucidates the roots of modern lifestyle diseases through two biochemical processes: glycation (AGE) and oxidation. This redefinition of "how to eat" from a physiological perspective is a notable feature of the book.  

Furthermore, this book is not just a manual for carbohydrate restriction; it is also a "philosophy of living" centered around natural ingredients. It advocates that regaining the sense of living in harmony with nature, like the Jomon people, is the ultimate health strategy for modern individuals.  

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **The End of "Calorie Belief"**  
  It emphasizes understanding the metabolic process of carbohydrates rather than merely reducing caloric intake. It rejects traditional diet methods reliant on calorie counting and presents a new perspective on health based on metabolic science.  

- **Carbohydrates as the Cause of Obesity and Aging**  
  Eating fat does not lead to weight gain, but consuming carbohydrates accumulates triglycerides. Carbohydrate restriction is positioned not just as a means of weight management but as a fundamental approach to slowing aging and disease progression.  

- **The Dangers of AGE (Advanced Glycation End-products)**  
  The accumulation of AGE degrades cells, leading to aging and diseases. Practical insights such as low-temperature cooking and the use of acidity to suppress AGE can be readily applied to everyday meals.  

- **"Carbohydrate Addiction" as a Modern Illness**  
  The temporary pleasure from sweets is an illusion of the brain, which actually leads to decreased concentration and depressive symptoms. Breaking free from carbohydrate dependency is key to restoring mental and physical performance.  

- **Return to Traditional Foods**  
  Traditional Japanese ingredients like tofu, natto, and seaweed support health and longevity. Reevaluating a food culture rooted in nature is shown to be scientifically rational.  

---

## 🔍 Relevance to Modern Context
In contemporary society, there is a simultaneous occurrence of "an overflow of health information" and "excessive obsession with ideal diets." Makita's assertions provide clear guidance amid this chaotic view of health. His scientific basis, rooted in AGE research, distinguishes it from mere trendy diets.  

Additionally, it resonates with recent nutritional discussions on issues like gluten, artificial sweeteners, and the reevaluation of three meals a day. Makita's framework of "carbohydrate restriction + natural food + moderate exercise" serves as a "compass for food" in an age of information overload.  

---

## 🚀 Practical Suggestions
1. **Reduce staple foods and switch to a side-dish-centered diet**  
   Decrease rice and bread, focusing on meat, fish, tofu, and vegetables. Stabilizing blood sugar levels helps prevent obesity and aging.  

2. **Change cooking methods to "boiling and raw"**  
   Choose boiling or steaming over frying or grilling, and utilize vinegar and lemon juice to suppress AGE production.  

3. **Make "chewing well and eating slowly" a habit**  
   Aim to chew each bite 30 times and take 30 minutes to finish a meal. This activates the satiety center and prevents blood sugar spikes.  

4. **Avoid processed foods and artificial sweeteners**  
   Limit sugary drinks and canned coffee, opting for natural beverages like water, tea, and soy milk. Protecting gut health contributes to maintaining immunity.  

5. **Skip carbohydrates at dinner and enjoy alcohol wisely**  
   Eliminate staple foods at night and enjoy low-carb alcoholic beverages like wine or shochu in moderation. Aim for a balance of relaxation and blood sugar control.  

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
『医者が教える食事術 最強の教科書』 is not just a health guide but a "re-education book on food" backed by scientific evidence. Yoshinori Makita's clinical experience and research findings converge to provide guidance for fundamentally reassessing modern eating habits.  

For readers often swayed by health information, this book prompts reflection not only on "what to eat" but also on "how to live." It offers an intellectual reading experience that encourages engagement with one's body through food.
