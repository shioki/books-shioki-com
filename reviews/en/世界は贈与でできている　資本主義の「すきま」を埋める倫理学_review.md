## 🎯 The Core of This Book
This book is a philosophical attempt to rediscover the ethical principle of "gift-giving" that capitalist society has overlooked. The author, 近内悠太, argues that what cannot be bought with money is the foundation of human relationships and trust, and is the key to filling the gaps in capitalism.

In a modern society dominated by the logic of exchange, relationships with others are measured by whether they are "worth it," leading to the collapse of trust and bonds. This book presents a new perspective on how humans can reconnect with others through the "ethics of gift-giving," which stands in stark contrast to this exchange logic.

It is also notable for using Wittgenstein's philosophy of language to depict gift-giving as an act that shares the same structure as "understanding" and "creation." The author shows that the source of love and creativity lies within the irrationality that transcends rationality.

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **Gift-giving begins with "receiving"**  
  The starting point of gift-giving is the awareness that "I have already received something." This consciousness of being a recipient generates new gifts and forms an ethical cycle.

- **True gift-giving is an act without calculation**  
  Actions that are based on expectations of return are exchanges, not gifts. Pure gift-giving can only exist as an "irrational" act that does not calculate future benefits.

- **Gift-giving can also act as a "curse"**  
  Just as goodwill can bind others, gift-giving carries the risk of depriving the recipient of their freedom. True gift-giving requires the imagination and restraint to liberate the other.

- **Irrationality drives creativity and understanding**  
  Gift-giving, love, and scientific discoveries all emerge from things that cannot be explained by reason. An attitude that does not eliminate contradictions and anomalies leads to a new understanding of the world.

- **Unnoticed gifts support society**  
  The ethics of "unsung heroes," who do not seek recognition, is at the core of this book. It is the unnoticed gifts that quietly support the trust and stability of society.

---

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
In modern society, economic activities that are "gift-like," such as the sharing economy, volunteer work, and crowdfunding, are being reevaluated. However, at the same time, the "exchange of approval" through social media and labor environments that prioritize efficiency are trapping relationships back into the logic of exchange.

In this context, the ideas of "anonymous gifts" and "irrational love" presented in this book resonate as an ethics that restores human dignity beyond economic rationality. 近内's concept of "intelligence that notices the letters that have arrived" encourages the revival of imagination that heals isolation in the digital age.

## 🚀 Suggestions for Practice
1. **Reflect on the gifts you have received**  
   Take time to find the thoughts and support from others in past events. This awareness will be the starting point for the next gift.

2. **Build relationships that are not judged by "worth"**  
   By engaging with others based on trust and empathy rather than profit and efficiency, relationships that transcend the logic of exchange can be created.

3. **Observe irrational events without rejection**  
   Rather than eliminating incomprehensible phenomena or emotions, adopt an attitude that seeks the meanings hidden within them, deepening creativity and understanding of others.

4. **Practice anonymous gift-giving**  
   Help someone without revealing your identity, support without seeking gratitude. Such acts can regenerate the invisible trust in society.

5. **Learn to "re-encounter the world"**  
   Learn not just to accumulate knowledge, but to regain sensitivity to the world. It is important to perceive learning itself as a gift.

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
『世界は贈与でできている』 is an ethical book that questions how humans can reconnect with others in a modern world overshadowed by economic rationality. By traversing philosophy, ethics, and sociology, it finds the roots of ethics and creativity within the seemingly irrational act of gift-giving.

Through this book, readers will reconsider the meaning of "receiving" before "giving." When they become aware of the small gifts hidden in their daily lives, the world will emerge as a richer and more human place.
