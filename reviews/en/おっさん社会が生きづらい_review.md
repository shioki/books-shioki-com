## 🎯 Core of the Book
『おっさん社会が生きづらい』 explores the social program of "ossan-ness" within individuals and outlines the intellectual and emotional processes needed to uninstall it. The author perceives "ossan-ness" not as a characteristic of specific middle-aged men, but as a learned trait inherent in society as a whole.

This book exposes the structure that binds men to the mantra of "be strong" and "dominate," revealing how this very structure causes suffering for them. Standing at the intersection of feminism and men's studies, the author proposes a reconfiguration of relationships through care and active listening, presenting an intellectual practice aimed at transforming the "ossan society."

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **"Ossan-ness" is a product of social learning present in everyone**  
  Regardless of gender or age, the social reward structure fosters "ossan-ness." Recognizing one's own dominant attitudes is the first step toward change.

- **The curse of "masculinity" also torments men themselves**  
  The societal conditioning to "be an eternal sexual conqueror" denies men the allowance for weakness, leading to isolation. The author critiques this structure as the foundation of "ossan society."

- **Listening is the starting point for social change**  
  Letting go of dominant conversational styles and listening without interruption contributes to uninstalling "ossan-ness." Active listening is not merely a courtesy but a technique for social transformation.

- **"Complaining" is an intellectual and emotional technique for recovery**  
  Sharing grievances is an act of acknowledging weakness and a process of social healing. It serves as a key to overcoming the culture that equates expressing vulnerability with defeat.

- **Maturity as "becoming an obasan"**  
  The concept of "becoming an obasan"—not being domineering, not taking up space, and stepping down from male privilege—represents a form of maturity that is open to others. This is presented as a way of being that stands in stark contrast to "ossan-ness."

---

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
In modern Japan, while discussions of gender equality and diversity are advancing, norms of "masculinity" and "femininity" remain deeply entrenched. Keiko Kojima's book visualizes this structural issue through the concept of "ossan-ness," prompting a reevaluation of the societal conditioning that permeates individual psyches.

Moreover, the influence of "ossan society" extends to various aspects, including harassment issues on social media and in workplaces, as well as generational divides in values. The author's proposals for "active listening," "care," and "becoming an obasan" serve as practical hints for transcending these divisions, making them highly suggestive in contemporary society.

---

## 🚀 Suggestions for Practice
1. **Incorporate "active listening" and "care communication" into daily life**  
   Listen without interrupting, and avoid rushing to judgments or conclusions. This is the first step in dismantling a dominant conversation culture and building equitable relationships.

2. **Do not be ashamed to "complain"**  
   Complaining is not a sign of weakness but an intellectual technique for connecting with others. Building relationships where grievances can be shared freely promotes social recovery.

3. **Learn the structures to avoid being hurt by "becoming knowledgeable"**  
   Understanding one's pain as a societal structural issue can help escape self-denial. Learning about gender and power structures is the first step toward freedom.

4. **Aim for "becoming an obasan"—maturity through humility and listening**  
   Maintaining a humble and open attitude towards others becomes a counterpoint to "ossan-ness." Maturity is the courage to relinquish control.

5. **Practice "Share, Care, Fair"**  
   This triad of principles, emphasizing fairness and empathy, serves as a practical guideline for reconstructing relationships in homes and workplaces.

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
『おっさん社会が生きづらい』 is an intellectual challenge that elucidates the suffocating aspects of contemporary society across gender and generational lines. Through the sharp concept of "ossan-ness," Keiko Kojima visualizes the dominant values that everyone has internalized and illustrates a path to overcoming them through healing and dialogue.

This book will serve as a mirror for both men and women to reflect on their own "ossan-ness." By reading it, one is prompted to explore new forms of maturity that facilitate living together with others.
