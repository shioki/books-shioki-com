## 🎯 The Core of This Book
This book attempts to redefine the "outcomes" of education not merely as test scores, but as a totality of culture, values, and understanding of humanity. The author, Lucy Crehan, visits top-performing countries in the PISA rankings to explore the social and psychological factors behind their educational systems.

The countries compared include Finland, Japan, Singapore, and three other educational powerhouses, revealing how each country supports "academic ability" through different philosophies and systems. The author concludes that teacher autonomy, a culture that believes in effort, and the pursuit of equity are key factors that influence educational outcomes.

Ultimately, this book returns to the fundamental question of "What is the purpose of education?" and presents an approach that emphasizes character formation and social responsibility in education.

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **Educational reform cannot be understood without culture**  
  The author points out that simply importing educational systems will not lead to success. Without understanding the cultural values and views of humanity behind these systems, reforms will become superficial. The insight that education is a reflection of society and is inseparable from culture is central to this book.

- **Finland's "late selection" supports equity**  
  By not sorting children early and instead lifting everyone up through support, Finland minimizes disparities. It is important to note that equity is achieved not through "the same materials" but by ensuring "the same opportunities."

- **Japan's education fosters "the ability to live within a group"**  
  Through group activities and collaborative work, children learn cooperation and responsibility. This is not just about developing academic ability; it reflects a cultural characteristic that emphasizes character formation as a member of society.

- **Trust and autonomy of teachers determine the quality of education**  
  An environment that trusts teachers and respects them as professionals enhances the quality of education more than surveillance or evaluation. When intrinsic motivation is institutionally supported, teachers can work creatively.

- **A culture that believes in effort supports academic ability**  
  The common belief in East Asia that "effort leads to improvement" enhances learning motivation and outcomes. A culture that views intelligence as malleable rather than fixed forms the foundation of educational success.

---

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
In recent years, while Japan maintains high academic performance in PISA, educational disparities and the early onset of entrance exams have been increasing. Research showing that household income and parental education levels affect academic ability contrasts sharply with Finland's "late selection."

The concepts of "equity" and "supporting everyone" depicted in this book are suggestive of the disparity issues currently facing Japanese society. Additionally, Singapore's example of excessive competition and early selection harming children's happiness serves as a warning for Japan, where the fervor for entrance exams is reigniting.

As discussions about "thinking skills education" in the AI era unfold, the author's emphasis on "character formation" and "purposeful learning" presents values that transcend mere skills education.

---

## 🚀 Suggestions for Practice
1. **Implement culturally rooted educational reforms**  
   Instead of mimicking systems from other countries, it is necessary to design reforms that are suitable for one's own culture and social structure. Education reflects societal values, and reforms that ignore cultural context will not be sustainable.

2. **Respect the professionalism and autonomy of teachers**  
   Rather than surveillance and reporting, a system design that trusts teachers and guarantees their discretion as professionals is required. This allows teachers to engage in creative and responsible educational practices.

3. **Avoid early selection and ensure equity through support**  
   Instead of dividing children based on academic ability, it is preferable to increase support to uplift everyone. Equity begins with establishing "the same starting line."

4. **Promote mutual learning among teachers**  
   By sharing excellent practices and building networks for mutual learning, the overall quality of the educational environment can be enhanced. A culture of trust and collaboration supports the sustainability of education.

5. **Broaden the purpose of education to include character formation**  
   Shift from an emphasis on academic ability to teaching "how to live as a person." Education that prioritizes morality and social responsibility lays the foundation for a sustainable society.

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
This book is an intellectual exploration that redefines education as a "cultural endeavor." The author's field research and psychological insights elevate education from mere institutional theory to the realm of human understanding.

Rich in implications not only for educators but also for parents and policymakers, it encourages reflection on the "philosophy of society" behind academic ability. After reading, a fundamental question about what education is meant to cultivate will quietly linger within the reader.
