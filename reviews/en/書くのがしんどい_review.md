## Dialogue

### Panel 1
**Scene**: The townsgirl sits at her writing desk in a quiet Edo townhouse, staring at a blank scroll with her brush poised but unmoving. Outside, the bustling street contrasts with her stillness, and she sighs, murmuring that her words refuse to come, even though her heart is full. A small window shows paper lanterns swaying in the evening breeze.  
**Dialogue**: "Why won't the words come, when my heart is so full?"

### Panel 2
**Scene**: She visits a scholarly merchant's shop filled with Dutch books and scrolls. An older man, resembling author Takeshi Shunsuke, hands her a worn manuscript. He explains that “writing” is not about perfection but about “conveying.” His demeanor is gentle yet firm, embodying quiet wisdom.  
**Dialogue**: "Remember, writing is not about perfection; it's about conveying."

### Panel 3
**Scene**: Inspired, the townsgirl walks through the marketplace, observing people's gestures, laughter, and worries. She begins jotting notes on a small paper pad, capturing snippets of overheard words. Suddenly, she stops, smiling, realizing that every sound and mistake around her is material to “convey.”  
**Dialogue**: "Every sound, every mistake... it's all part of the story."

### Panel 4
**Scene**: Night again. The townsgirl writes by lamplight, her expression serene as the brush moves smoothly across the paper. In a small vertical tanzaku panel beside her, her short poem (waka) reads:  
**Tanka (translation)**: "I wish to convey, / Words become a bridge of wind, / Lighting up someone's heart."

## 🎯 Core of the Book
『書くのがしんどい』 addresses the suffering of those who struggle to write, asserting that the root cause lies in the mindset of wanting to "write." The author, 竹村俊助, argues that by shifting the perspective from "writing" to "communicating," one can begin to write more freely.

This book emphasizes the mindset of the writer over technical writing skills. Under the guiding principle of "writing what only you can write in a way that anyone can understand," it encourages readers to draw from everyday observations and failures, promoting a playful attitude towards trial and error.

Moreover, it redefines writing as an act of "editing," highlighting the importance of having a reader's perspective. Writing is not a solitary endeavor but a dialogue with others and a process of self-discovery.

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **The Consciousness of "Communicating" Moves the Pen**  
  Those who struggle to write often fall into perfectionism by focusing too much on "writing." The author suggests that by shifting the focus to "communicating," the writing process becomes more natural. This is a shift in thinking centered on relationships with others rather than self-expression.

- **Negative Experiences Can Also Be Material**  
  Transforming emotional turmoil and failures into something constructive can lead to relatable writing. By organizing emotions through writing and sharing them with others, one can achieve both self-healing and social connection.

- **Write Like "Lacquering"**  
  Instead of aiming for perfection in one go, revising multiple times adds depth to the writing. Viewing writing as a process of repetition and refinement fosters sustained creativity.

- **Alternating Between "Writer" and "Editor"**  
  Write with innocence and read with a critical eye. By switching between creativity and critique within oneself, one can maintain both objectivity and passion. This is a practical method to enhance self-editing skills.

- **Designing the Reader's "Reason to Read"**  
  Writing also takes away the reader's time. Therefore, it is essential to be aware of "why the reader is reading" and to clarify the benefits of the information, which reflects the integrity of a sincere writer.

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
In modern society, the rise of social media and business writing has turned everyone into a "writer." However, writing fatigue and mental blocks due to perfectionism have become serious issues.竹村's suggestions serve as a remedy for this "overabundance of writing culture."

Additionally, with the proliferation of AI writing tools making "efficient writing" easier, the value of "communicating in one's own words" is being questioned again. This book reaffirms the significance of communication rooted in human emotions and experiences, even in an age reliant on technology.

The perspective that writing is not a job or obligation, but an act of "editing the world through oneself," serves as an important guideline for individuals living in a decentralized society, fostering both creativity and mental well-being.

## 🚀 Practical Suggestions
1. **Create a "Notebook of Ideas"**  
   By cultivating the habit of jotting down daily observations and emotions, writing material will accumulate naturally. Observing everyday life without waiting for special experiences is the first step in creativity.

2. **Use Voice Input to Free Your Thoughts**  
   By speaking out ideas rather than overthinking them, one can reduce the psychological burden of writing. It is important to focus first on "producing" rather than avoiding perfectionism.

3. **Ask Yourself "In Other Words?" and "For Example?"**  
   Alternating between abstract and concrete thinking enhances the persuasiveness of writing. This self-editing method is effective for deepening thought and training structural skills.

4. **Write Innocently, Read Critically**  
   Encourage yourself while writing, and be strict when reviewing. This dual attitude is key to enhancing the quality of creation.

5. **Write What You Would Like to Read**  
   By starting from your own interests and emotions rather than overly focusing on others, you can ultimately create universally relatable content. Trusting your own feelings is the source of compelling writing.

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
『書くのがしんどい』 transcends the boundaries of writing techniques and connects the act of writing with the act of living. It offers a shift in perspective and practical healing for anyone struggling with the pains of writing.

This book will serve as a new starting point for self-expression for business professionals, creators, and those who regularly share on social media. It encourages readers to reflect on themselves through writing and rediscover the joy of connecting with others.
