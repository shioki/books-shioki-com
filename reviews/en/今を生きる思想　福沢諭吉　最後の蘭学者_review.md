## 🎯 The Core of This Book
This book reexamines Fukuzawa Yukichi as "the last Dutch scholar" and attempts to reconsider the formation of modern Japanese civilization from the lineage of Dutch studies. The author, Okubo Takeharu, interprets Fukuzawa's thought not merely as part of the lineage of enlightenment thinkers but as an extension of the "translation" activities of Dutch studies. Through translation, Fukuzawa created new concepts and engaged in an intellectual struggle to reconstruct civilization itself.

The spirit of "practical learning," "seeking principles," and "debate" that Fukuzawa emphasized is not just an academic discourse but a social philosophy that supports independence and civilization. The author presents this spirit as the key to reviving reason in a contemporary world marked by information overload and emotional division.

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **Dutch studies are the intellectual embryo of modern Japan**  
  Fukuzawa's thought inherited the academic spirit of Dutch studies that emerged in the late Edo period and theoretically supported the civilization and enlightenment of the Meiji era. Dutch studies functioned not merely as imitation of the West but as a unique "embryo of civilization."

- **Freedom is born from discussion and dissent**  
  Fukuzawa's concept of "debate" is an intellectual practice to protect freedom. His stance that the clash of differing opinions fosters societal reason and maturity resonates with modern democracy.

- **Translation is an act of thought creation**  
  Fukuzawa's attempts to translate beschaving as "civilization" and right as "通義" were not mere substitutions of words but creative acts of reconstructing foreign thoughts within a Japanese context.

- **Practical learning is the driving force of civilization**  
  Fukuzawa viewed scholarship as a means of social transformation, striving for national independence through scientific practice. His attitude of "applying knowledge to society" underpins modern innovation education.

- **A warning against the crisis of reason**  
  He foresaw the emotional divisions in an information society and warned that the lack of reason and critical spirit would lead to the degradation of civilization. Fukuzawa's spirit of "seeking principles" serves as a guide to overcoming contemporary intellectual confusion.

---

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
In modern society, the rise of populism and the excessive flow of information are shaking the foundations of democracy. These situations resonate remarkably with Fukuzawa's warning of the "decline of reason." His thought of "independence and self-respect" is being reevaluated as an ethics for individuals to live independently within society without abandoning their own thinking and judgment.

Moreover, Fukuzawa's spirit of "practical learning" is gaining attention in the context of educational and community revitalization. The attitude of linking knowledge to practice and contributing to the public aligns with the core of modern educational reform and community design. Fukuzawa's thought is not merely a historical legacy but a philosophical resource for reconstructing contemporary reason and freedom.

## 🚀 Suggestions for Practice
1. **Cultivate a culture of discussion that welcomes dissent**  
   Intentionally create spaces for debate with others, unafraid of conflicting opinions. Fukuzawa's "debate" is the foundation of a rational society.

2. **Address social issues with the spirit of "practical learning"**  
   Rather than just accumulating knowledge, apply scientific thinking to real-world problems. An attitude that views scholarship as a means of social transformation is essential.

3. **Maintain critical thinking towards information**  
   Resist emotional reactions and engage in fact-checking and logical analysis. Practicing the spirit of "seeking principles" daily strengthens reason to withstand the tumult of the information society.

4. **Understand other cultures through translation**  
   Reconstruct others' values in your own words to deepen mutual understanding. Fukuzawa's translational thinking connects to intellectual independence in a global age.

5. **Practice autonomy and public participation in local communities**  
   Engage in public activities within your living area and cultivate "patriotism of inquiry." The accumulation of small autonomies supports a truly independent society.

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
『今を生きる思想　福沢諭吉　最後の蘭学者』 is an ambitious work that repositions Fukuzawa Yukichi not merely as an enlightenment thinker but as an intellectual practitioner who carries on the spirit of Dutch studies. It carefully illustrates the continuity of thought from Dutch studies to "practical learning" and then to "civilization," shedding new light on the intellectual foundations of modern Japan.

In the context of today's information society, this book provides a clear answer to the question of how to maintain reason and freedom, presenting Fukuzawa's thought as a guide. It is a profound read for those who aspire to apply scholarship to society and for those contemplating the revival of democracy.
