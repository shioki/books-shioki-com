## Dialogue

### Panel 1
**Scene**: At dusk on an Edo street, the young townsgirl walks past a clockmaker adjusting a large Dutch-style clock. She pauses, intrigued and slightly puzzled by the notion that “time” can be measured so precisely. Lanterns flicker to life among narrow wooden houses, the quiet street flowing gently with the rhythm of evening.  
**Dialogue**: *“Can something as fleeting as time truly be captured?”*

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her small study, she leans over a Dutch scientific book describing devices said to “preserve” or “freeze” time. On the wall hangs a portrait of a calm scholar—sharp-eyed, unkempt hair, holding a quill—whose gaze seems to follow her, silently guiding her through the invisible layers of time and existence.  
**Dialogue**: *“If time can be held still… what becomes of the self that moves within it?”*

### Panel 3
**Scene**: She experiments with a sundial, marking its shadow every few breaths. Suddenly, she notices the marks overlap—the past and present coexist. Startled, then amused, she laughs softly as a breeze scatters her notes. A cat leaps through the frame, a fleeting symbol of time’s circular dance.  
**Dialogue**: *“So even moments can meet again…”*

### Panel 4
**Scene**: By candlelight, she writes a waka on thin paper, her expression serene. She reflects that to “disappear” might be another form of freedom, as the flame trembles beside her.  
**Dialogue**: *“To vanish is also to live, perhaps.”*  
**Tanka (translation)**:  
Layers of time—  
memory and town dissolve,  
mingling as one;  
to fade away is yet  
another shape of life.

溶けあいて

## 🎯 Core of the Book
*その針がさすのは* explores the fragility of human existence through four interwoven axes: time, the city, the body, and memory. Set in Nakano, Tokyo, the novel mirrors the city’s constant transformation with the shifting inner world of its protagonist and the evolving dynamics of a marriage.  

Through the deeply personal experience of infertility treatment, 羽田圭介 layers the themes of “the creation of life” and “the distortion of time.” Scientific apparatuses such as cryopreservation and semen collection rooms become metaphors for freezing time and manipulating life, exposing the ethical and philosophical dilemmas of our technologically mediated age.  

In a society increasingly defined by remote work and AI interactions—where “not showing oneself” has become the norm—the novel captures the essence of a contactless world. Time and the body dissolve into the urban landscape, and ultimately, “disappearance” itself emerges as a new form of life.  

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **Time is nonlinear and exists in layers**  
  The recurring clocks and depictions of warped spacetime suggest that life is not a single line but a collage of overlapping fragments. The coexistence of past, present, and future challenges readers to redefine their sense of time.  

- **The city is both an archive of memory and a device of forgetting**  
  The transformation of Nakano symbolizes the mutability of human memory. As the city renews itself, old memories vanish—revealing how the modern metropolis overwrites human existence itself.  

- **Reproductive technology as a means to freeze time**  
  The act of controlling life through cryopreservation represents an attempt to halt the natural flow of time, prompting a fundamental reconsideration of the relationship between life and temporality. Here, the novel condenses the modern tension between science and human ontology.  

- **A society where “not showing oneself” becomes the norm**  
  In an age where remote work and AI interactions dominate, society is increasingly composed of disembodied presences. 羽田 captures the quiet realism of this “non-physical existence” with subtle precision.  

- **To “disappear” can also mean to be free**  
  When the protagonist steps away from social and familial structures, the act is both loss and liberation. The novel’s core philosophy lies in embracing the thinning of existence as a pathway to freedom.

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
Winner of the 2nd Tokyo Nakano Literary Award, this work stands at the intersection of regional literature and contemporary social issues. The theme of infertility treatment resonates strongly in late-2020s Japan, where public support for reproductive medicine has expanded as part of national efforts to address declining birthrates.  

The novel’s portrayal of urban transformation and remote living also reflects the post-pandemic “contactless lifestyle,” symbolizing the isolation of modern individuals. Yet 羽田 presents this isolation not as tragedy, but as an opportunity to redefine existence—offering a new vision of what the modern city can be in literature.

## 🚀 Practical Reflections
- **Observe your own sense of time**  
  Try perceiving daily life not as a linear flow but as overlapping layers. Allow past memories and future anxieties to coexist in the present, and explore ways of living untethered from chronological time.  

- **Document the transformation of your city**  
  Record changes in your neighborhood through photos or journals, and compare them with your past memories. By tracing the city’s evolution, you can gain perspective on your own transformation and the fluidity of memory.  

- **Intentionally create “disappearing time”**  
  In a constantly connected society, make space to disconnect from the digital world. Embrace silence and solitude as opportunities to reconstruct your sense of being—this conscious absence can become a modern form of freedom.  

- **Reaffirm reality through the body**  
  Reclaim bodily awareness often lost in the information age. Walking, touching, and breathing—experiencing the world through the body—can restore a tangible sense of existence.  

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
*その針がさすのは* maps the intersections of time, existence, the city, and the body in contemporary life. Through the intimate lens of infertility treatment, 羽田圭介 reveals both the loss of substance and the potential for renewal that define our era.  

Readers will find themselves questioning which moment in time they truly inhabit amid the city’s noise—and what “needle” guides their own existence. Quiet yet deeply philosophical, this is a work for anyone seeking to contemplate what it means to exist in the modern world.
