## 🎯 The Core of This Book
This book captures the practical work of the information systems department (情シス) across the entire lifecycle of "planning, development, operation, and maintenance," systematically organizing "best practices" learned from failure cases. It is not merely a technical book but provides a strategic perspective that bridges management and IT.  

The authors assert that the success of a system is determined not by technical skills but by the "accuracy of the planning phase" and "relationships among people and organizations." They dissect common issues that arise in the field, from creating RFPs to defining requirements, selecting contract types, and building teams, presenting them as reproducible wisdom.  

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **Errors in the planning phase can lead to 200 times the cost in later stages**  
  Quantitative data illustrating the exponential increase in correction costs highlights the importance of requirement accuracy in the early stages. They argue that early consensus and reviews are the most cost-effective investments.  

- **RFPs are not specifications but communication tools**  
  Positioning RFPs as "blueprints for dialogue" that promote mutual understanding between the client and the vendor, rather than mere order documents. This shift in perspective can prevent future troubles.  

- **Neglecting non-functional requirements renders systems unusable**  
  Clarifying non-functional requirements such as performance, security, and availability is key to operational success. Failing to consider user perspectives can result in a formally completed system that is "unusable."  

- **Choosing contract types is part of risk management**  
  The importance of distinguishing between quasi-commission contracts and contracts for work is emphasized to minimize legal and practical risks. Contracts are not just formalities; they define the project structure itself.  

- **"People are everything" — structure and relationships determine success**  
  Success hinges not only on technical rationality but also on trust and collaboration among stakeholders. Strategically building relationships is crucial for the quality of the final deliverables.  

---

## 🔍 Relevance to Modern Context
In recent years, Japanese companies have faced a severe shortage of IT personnel under the banner of promoting DX, increasing the burden on information systems departments. In fields like healthcare and manufacturing, delays in system updates and security incidents are directly linked to management risks. In this environment, the book's principles of "involvement from the planning phase" and "pursuit of overall optimization" hold extremely practical significance.  

Moreover, with the rise of AI agents and increasing dynamic loads, we have entered an era where traditional static system design can no longer cope. Information systems must evolve into entities that control risks from both management and technical perspectives, and this book provides the foundational thinking for that evolution.  

---

## 🚀 Suggestions for Practice
1. **Thoroughly clarify requirements and needs in the planning phase**  
   To prevent the inflation of correction costs, utilize 5W2H and review meetings to eliminate ambiguity. The accuracy in the early stages determines later success.  

2. **Design RFPs as "blueprints for dialogue"**  
   Rather than just specifications, clearly articulate the background, objectives, and expectations to build trust with vendors. This minimizes the risk of later specification changes.  

3. **Establish and maintain a system grand design**  
   Document the overarching design philosophy for the entire organization and ensure that individual projects adhere to it. This forms the basis for overall optimization.  

4. **Visualize decision-making through FIT & GAP analysis**  
   When introducing services, quantitatively organize compatibility with business operations and identify knockout factors early. This enhances transparency and acceptance in decision-making.  

5. **Strategically build relationships and structures**  
   Beyond technical rationality, focus on involving stakeholders and constructing collaborative frameworks. Understanding internal politics and increasing allies are prerequisites for success.  

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
『情シスの定石』 is a practical book that redefines information systems as a "strategic department supporting management." It goes beyond mere technical discussions, systematically addressing "invisible elements" such as planning, contracts, and human relationships.  

In particular, the method of structurally demonstrating "why things didn't go well" through failure cases offers high immediacy for practitioners in the field. For those in information systems, IT planning roles, or management, this book serves as a compass connecting organization and technology in the era of DX.
