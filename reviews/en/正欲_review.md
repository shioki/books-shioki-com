## 🎯 The Core of This Book
"正欲" is a story that fundamentally questions the frameworks defined by society, such as "correctness," "normalcy," "love," and "sex." 朝井リョウ exposes the structure of exclusion that those who stand on the "normal side of the shore" unconsciously enact, revealing the underlying violence within it.

The characters carry unrecognized desires and loneliness, yet they connect through a small promise of "not disappearing." Their attitude of existing together without being fully understood serves as a quiet rebellion against the "empathy fatigue" of modern society.

This book is a literary attempt to transcend the modern resignation of "living to avoid death" and reclaim "the act of living" itself.

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **"Correctness" Can Turn into Violence**  
  The "healthiness" and "justice" that society advocates often function as mechanisms for excluding those who are different. 朝井 portrays this structure as the "pleasure of exclusion" that occurs unconsciously in daily life, prompting readers to reevaluate their own positions.

- **Desire is the Root of Humanity**  
  Sexual preferences and fetishes are not merely sexual acts; they relate to the very core of thought, philosophy, and human relationships. The book argues that society's denial of these aspects is tantamount to denying the existence of individuals.

- **The Illusion of Being "Normal"**  
  Belonging to the majority is merely a product of chance, and stable "normalcy" does not exist. 朝井 visualizes this fragility and confronts readers with the precariousness of the term "normal."

- **Coexistence Over Understanding**  
  The characters connect through the assurance of existence, "not disappearing," while giving up on fully understanding each other. Living alongside incomprehensible others is presented as the true form of empathy.

- **Affirmation of Loneliness**  
  Living with a loneliness that is not understood by others is not a defeat. Rather, it is a quiet form of happiness that allows one to maintain their own contours while being with others.

---

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
The "unrecognized desires" and "structures of exclusion" depicted in "正欲" sharply critique the superficial acceptance of diversity in the 2020s. In the age of social media, only "differences that can be empathized with" are welcomed, while "difficult-to-understand desires" are pushed into the shadows. 朝井 makes this reality visible through the language of literature.

Additionally, it connects to contemporary phenomena such as "oshi-katsu" (supporting one's favorite) and "conspiracy theories," where individuals immerse themselves in narratives they believe in. As society only accepts "empathizable stories," people become increasingly isolated, retreating into self-contained worlds. "正欲" anticipates this precariousness and questions how we can coexist with "others we cannot understand."

## 🚀 Suggestions for Practice
1. **Question Your Own "Correctness"**  
   Consciously inspect whether your common sense or ethics are excluding someone in your daily life. Before condemning others in the name of justice, reflect on the underlying structures.

2. **Be with the Uncomprehendable Other**  
   Practice accepting the presence of those you cannot understand without excluding them. Aiming for "coexistence" rather than understanding will form the foundation of a diverse society.

3. **Do Not Deny Loneliness**  
   Accept loneliness as a "premise" rather than a "defect." Engaging with others while carrying loneliness can create true connections.

4. **Broaden Your Understanding of "Sexuality"**  
   Do not dismiss sexual preferences and desires as "abnormal," but understand them as fundamental expressions of humanity. This is the most practical ethics for respecting others.

5. **Listen to the Unspoken Voices**  
   Pay attention to the silence of those deemed "bugs" by society. Recognizing their existence not as abnormal but as a reality overlooked by society is the first step toward empathy.

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
"正欲" not only uncovers the violence hidden within modern society's "correctness" but also portrays the dignity of "living without being understood." 朝井リョウ confronts the limits of ethics and empathy while not overlooking the warmth of humanity that remains.

What lingers after reading is a quiet determination to coexist, even while accepting the impossibility of fully understanding others. This novel poses profound questions to all who feel suffocated by society's "normalcy."
