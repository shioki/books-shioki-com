## 🎯 Core of the Book
The core message of this book is not to eliminate anger as a negative emotion, but to accept it as a necessary feeling for self-protection. The goal is not to become someone who never gets angry, but to discern situations where anger is warranted and express it in a way that avoids regret.

To achieve this, the book combines short-term techniques to calm impulses with long-term training to reassess the "shoulds" and reaction patterns that generate anger. It also encourages readers to articulate the underlying anxieties and sadness behind their anger, shifting the focus from aggression to problem-solving.

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **Separate Emotions from Actions**  
  The book does not deny the feeling of anger itself but encourages readers to reconsider their subsequent words and actions. The fundamental approach is not to suppress emotions but to adjust how they are expressed.

- **Combine Short-term Coping with Long-term Change**  
  While using breathing techniques or counting to prevent outbursts, it also emphasizes understanding situations that trigger anger through recording and reflection. This practical approach does not rely solely on immediate solutions.

- **Question Your "Shoulds"**  
  Anger often stems from a mismatch between personal beliefs and reality. Recognizing that others have different values allows one to distinguish between non-negotiable demands and expectations that can be let go.

- **Translate Anger into Requests**  
  Using "I" messages, the book teaches how to convey one's feelings and desired actions specifically. This method shifts the focus from blaming others to expressing in a way that promotes mutual resolution.

---

## 🔍 Relevance to Modern Context
In a society where issues like power harassment, customer harassment, and verbal abuse at home are prevalent, managing anger is not just a personal trait but a matter of safety in organizations and relationships. The book's stance of "not denying emotions but choosing actions" clearly distinguishes it from mere training that demands endurance.

Moreover, the method of breaking down anger into anxiety or sadness and conveying it as specific requests can be applied in various contexts, such as workplaces, parenting, and caregiving. However, it is essential to read this alongside the perspective that emotional management should not be solely demanded from victims; maintaining distance and organizational responses are also necessary.

---

## 🚀 Suggestions for Practice
1. **Rate Your Anger from 0 to 10**  
   Immediately after feeling anger, quantify its intensity. This creates space to step back from emotions and prevent reflexive reactions.

2. **Establish Personal Emergency Coping Strategies**  
   Choose one method from deep breathing, reverse counting, a catchphrase, or stepping away. Deciding on this during calm moments makes it easier to implement during intense anger.

3. **Keep a 21-Day Anger Log**  
   Record the date, person involved, event, and intensity each time anger arises, without analyzing while writing. Reviewing later reveals patterns in reactions.

4. **Organize the Importance of Your "Shoulds"**  
   Write down your expectations and separate what is non-negotiable from what can be accepted. This reframes the cause of anger not solely as the other person's fault but as a mismatch with your expectations.

5. **Use "I" Messages to Communicate Requests**  
   Instead of saying "you," express "I am troubled" or "I would like this next time." Focusing on a single point leads to improvement rather than blame.

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
This book is a practical guide that presents techniques for observing, recording, reflecting, and communicating, rather than a mere philosophy for suppressing anger. The manga format makes psychological concepts more accessible, making it easier for first-time readers of anger management to engage.

It is particularly beneficial for those who feel self-loathing after getting angry, those who suppress their feelings even when necessary, and those struggling with how to reprimand subordinates or family members. Readers can transform anger from a destructive force in relationships into a pathway for self-understanding and problem-solving.
