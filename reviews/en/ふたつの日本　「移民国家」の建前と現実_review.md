## 🎯 Core of the Book
This book sharply analyzes the structural issues that have led Japan to continue denying the reality of being an "immigrant nation" from sociological and ethical perspectives. The author, 望月優大, exposes how the state has effectively rendered immigrants invisible through "side-door" mechanisms such as the technical intern training and study abroad systems, while still accepting them in practice.  

He redefines the "immigration issue" not merely as a challenge for foreign workers, but as a problem affecting the entirety of Japanese society, which is increasingly losing its support systems. As the state and corporations withdraw from their "responsibility to support," foreign workers find themselves in the most vulnerable positions. The book confronts this reality and presents the ethical proposition that "a country that denies immigrants is a country that denies humanity."

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **"Immigrants" Already Exist**  
  While Japan claims it does not have an "immigration policy," millions of foreigners have settled in the country. The author points out that this denial breeds social distortions and perpetuates systemic injustices.

- **Structural Violence of the Technical Intern Training System**  
  The fact that over 70% of cases violate labor standards indicates that the system inherently contains human rights violations. The book reveals how the ostensibly altruistic "international contribution" has become a means to secure cheap labor.

- **"People Who Cannot Return" and the Inhumanity of Immigration Administration**  
  Through issues of forced deportation and long-term detention, the book uncovers how state governance can strip away human dignity. The author criticizes this situation as "institutional violence."

- **Without "Support," "Rights" Are Empty**  
  In the absence of social support, legal rights, no matter how well-protected, fail to function in reality. The author argues that reconstructing "support" is the foundation for social integration.

- **The Issues of "Immigrants" and "Japanese" Are Interconnected**  
  The instability faced by foreign workers and non-regular Japanese workers stems from the same root problem. Considering them separately undermines social solidarity.

---

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
In Japan, where declining birth rates and an aging population have led to labor shortages, the number of foreign workers is rapidly increasing through the expansion of the specified skills system. However, the lack of social integration policies and the facade of being a "non-immigrant nation" create systemic ambiguities and cultural friction.  

In Western countries, similar histories of immigration have led to political conflicts, and Japan is at risk of following the same path. The author's warning clearly highlights the risks of "avoiding discussion" in the context of these international trends. The question now is whether Japan will recognize immigrants as "coexisting beings" rather than rendering them "nonexistent."

---

## 🚀 Proposals for Action
1. **Clearly Define "Immigrants" and Recognize Them in Policy**  
   As long as the existence of immigrants is denied, systemic distortions will not be resolved. The first step is for society as a whole to share a self-awareness as an "immigrant nation."

2. **Fundamental Reform or Abolition of the Technical Intern Training System**  
   Maintaining a system that contains human rights violations signifies an ethical failure of the state. A transition to a labor and residency system that aligns with reality is urgent.

3. **Institutionalize Education, Medical Care, and Living Support**  
   To treat foreigners as "people," society must bear the social costs of ensuring access to education and healthcare. Support should be institutionalized as a "right" rather than a "special case."

4. **Introduce Judicial Oversight of Immigration Administration**  
   Reform the current situation where third parties cannot contest detention or deportation, and establish mechanisms for judicial checks to prevent human rights violations.

5. **Reconstruct Social Solidarity as a Form of "Support"**  
   Position foreign support as part of creating a "society where we live together," and encourage collaboration among local communities, businesses, and government to rebuild social support.

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
『ふたつの日本』 is a critical social commentary that illustrates the "loss of support" in Japanese society through the lens of immigration issues. 望月優大 not only exposes systemic distortions but also sharply illuminates the ethical challenges that lie within.  

This book is essential reading not only for those interested in foreign workers and human rights issues but for anyone who wishes to reconsider the very structure of contemporary Japanese society. After reading, the fundamental question of "What kind of society do we want to support?" quietly yet firmly lingers in the mind.
