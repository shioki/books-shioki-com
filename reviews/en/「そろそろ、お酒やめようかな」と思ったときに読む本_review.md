## 🎯 Core of the Book
This book serves as a practical guide that elucidates the structure of alcohol dependence and the recovery process from both medical and psychological perspectives. The author, 垣渕 洋一, views addiction not as a "weakness of will" but as a "disease that needs treatment," providing readers with insights to reflect on their drinking behaviors based on scientific evidence and clinical knowledge.

Notably, the book explains the process of abstaining from alcohol as "relearning for the brain," emphasizing a structured understanding of the physical and mental changes involved, rather than merely a mental exercise. Furthermore, it defines recovery from addiction not as "cure" but as "recovery," emphasizing the importance of support from peers and the environment.

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **Recognizing Addiction Lies in the "Next Morning"**  
  The state of one's body and behavior the next morning serves as a crucial sign that distinguishes mere overindulgence from addiction. If symptoms ease with morning drinking, it signals that one has already entered the stage of dependence.

- **The Health Benefits of Alcohol are an Illusion**  
  The author asserts that there are "zero benefits from a physical health perspective." He challenges the common belief rooted in drinking culture that "moderate drinking is good for health," urging a reconsideration based on scientific evidence.

- **Abstinence Requires "90 Days" for the Brain to Relearn**  
  Abstaining from alcohol is about reconstructing habits rather than willpower, with a continuous period of 90 days being key. Understanding the process by which the brain learns to be in a state of "not needing to drink" makes maintaining abstinence a realistic goal.

- **Addiction is "Recovery" not "Cure"**  
  As symbolized by the metaphor of takuan (pickled radish), addiction cannot revert to its original state, but with appropriate support, "recovery" is possible. This perspective guides the transition from self-denial to hope.

- **Abstinence is a Reinvestment in Life**  
  Reclaiming the time and energy spent on drinking leads to a reconstruction of life. The book argues that abstinence is not merely moderation but a redefinition of happiness.

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
The issues addressed in this book are deeply connected to the modern society's health consciousness and the expanding lifestyle of "not drinking." Recently, particularly among younger generations, the "sober curious" movement promoting abstinence and moderation is gaining traction, with non-drinking interactions becoming more common in workplaces and friendships.

Additionally, the growth of the non-alcoholic beverage market and advancements in scientific discussions regarding the health risks of alcohol provide a societal backdrop that supports the book's claims. 垣渕 seems to anticipate this trend by portraying "not drinking" as a positive choice, proposing not only recovery from addiction but also a lifestyle of abstinence.

## 🚀 Practical Suggestions
1. **Postpone the Peak of Cravings**  
   Cravings typically subside within 30 minutes to an hour, so fill that time with other activities. This scientifically grounded method is a practical strategy to prevent impulsive drinking.

2. **Consciously Change Weekend Activities**  
   By intentionally incorporating outings with family or hobbies, one can break the rhythm of a drinking-centered lifestyle. Gaining new fulfillment supports the continuation of abstinence.

3. **Enhance Self-Awareness through "Visualization"**  
   Keep track of drinking amounts and moods to objectively observe one's behavior. This cognitive-behavioral approach allows for conscious rewriting of unconscious habits.

4. **Join Support Groups or Communities**  
   Interacting with others who share similar experiences helps prevent isolation and brings psychological stability through empathy. Social connections are the foundation of recovery.

5. **Articulate the Benefits of Abstinence**  
   Writing down the happiness gained from not drinking helps maintain motivation. This serves as a psychological reinforcement for sustaining behavioral change.

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
This book is an ambitious work that redefines the personal issue of alcohol dependence within a social and cultural context. It uniquely combines medical knowledge and psychological insights, presenting "not drinking" as a choice for happiness.

Especially through the concept of "recovery," it impressively provides readers with a perspective of self-recovery rather than self-denial. It serves as a practical and philosophical guide for anyone looking to reassess their drinking habits, as well as for those interested in changes in addiction and habits, encouraging a reconstruction of life.
