**Scene**: The townsgirl walks through an Edo street at dusk, captivated by a crowd surrounding a foreign "mechanical eye" brought by a Dutch trader—a primitive camera obscura. She tilts her head, pondering how such a device can truly "see." The townscape is illuminated by lantern light, reflecting her curiosity and unease.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 2
**Scene**: In her modest study, the townsgirl studies a Dutch optics manual and a scroll labeled "心眼." Behind her, the spirit or portrait of an older scholar resembling the author appears—a wise, calm man with short hair and gentle eyes, dressed in simple robes. He gestures toward her heart, indicating that true sight comes from within.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 3
**Scene**: The townsgirl observes a commotion in the marketplace where a peddler is accused of theft. While others judge quickly, she pauses, noticing the man's trembling hands and worn sandals. With "heart's eyes," she realizes he is hungry, not deceitful, and offers him rice instead of accusation, softening the crowd.
**Dialogue**: None

### Panel 4

## 🎯 The Core of This Book
『心眼』 is a work that transcends the boundaries of police novels to question "what it means to be human." Through the analog method of investigative work, it illustrates the value of the "human eye" and "heart" in a technology-dominated modern society.  
The protagonist, Katagiri, gains a "heart eye" that sees beyond mere visual perception, acquiring insights through experience and suffering. This journey is not only a story of growth as a detective but also a philosophical exploration of human dignity and justice.  
In an era where AI and surveillance cameras dominate investigations, the author quietly asserts that "human judgment and ethics are the last bastions."

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- **"Heart eye" is the crystallization of experience and human understanding**  
  The "heart eye" that Katagiri learns is not just intuition but an insight born from accumulated experience and observation. The ability to perceive the essence of humanity, which cannot be captured by data, is presented as true investigative power.  

- **Analog efforts generate trust**  
  Katagiri's act of manually organizing photographs, though seemingly inefficient, is depicted as a practice that fosters respect and concentration towards the subject. In a modern world increasingly dominated by digitalization, it prompts a re-examination of the meaning of taking the time and effort.  

- **Criminals are also victims of society**  
  Aiba does not condemn crime as merely individual evil. He portrays the "another victimhood" that arises within social structures like poverty and isolation, encouraging readers to broaden their understanding of humanity.  

- **Technological innovation tests ethics**  
  The evolution of AI facial recognition and surveillance cameras carries the danger of stripping away "human judgment" behind the facade of efficiency. The author sharply illustrates the ethical void brought about by technological advancement.  

- **Justice resides not in systems but in beliefs**  
  The image of detectives striving to uphold human dignity even against orders from higher-ups demonstrates that the roots of justice lie not in systems but in individual conscience.  

---

## 🔍 Relevance to Contemporary Context
The core theme of this work, the "conflict between analog and digital," is deeply intertwined with concerns about an AI surveillance society. In a modern world where security cameras and facial recognition technology have become commonplace, there is a risk that "human judgment" may be overlooked in the pursuit of efficiency and safety.  
Moreover, the reality that poverty and isolation serve as breeding grounds for crime resonates with the social divisions in contemporary Japan. Aiba Hiroshi sheds light on societal distortions through crime, prompting readers to question the difference between "seeing" and "understanding."  
『心眼』 can be read as a literary alarm that calls for the reclamation of the "human eye" in the face of the ethical void brought about by technological progress.

## 🚀 Practical Suggestions
1. **Be aware of the "heart eye" in daily life**  
   Cultivating a habit of imagining the background and motives behind people and events, rather than making superficial judgments, can strengthen your insight.  

2. **Reclaim time for manual tasks**  
   Incorporating analog activities like writing in a notebook or organizing materials into your digital life can deepen your thoughts and enhance emotional engagement.  

3. **Adopt an attitude of "cultivating information"**  
   Rather than seeking short-term results, delve into information while building trust. This is a universal approach that applies to both business and personal relationships.  

4. **Cultivate judgment that does not overly rely on technology**  
   It is crucial to train yourself to make judgments based on your own ethics and experiences, rather than blindly accepting AI or data results.  

5. **Be mindful of the "fifteen-second void"**  
   When faced with unexpected situations, taking a moment to breathe before making a decision can enable calm responses. This is a skill relevant to crisis management.  

---

## ⭐ Overall Evaluation
『心眼』 is a "book of human understanding" that transcends the boundaries of police novels. Aiba Hiroshi questions the value of ethics and intuition in the technological age through the extreme human drama of investigative scenes.  
Readers will experience the difficulty of "seeing" and the preciousness of "perceiving" alongside Katagiri. In a modern society that pursues efficiency and results, this work quietly reexamines the "meaning of being human."
